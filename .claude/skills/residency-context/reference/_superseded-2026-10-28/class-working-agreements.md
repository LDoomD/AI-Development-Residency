These are the working agreements the class co-created in a working session. They define how sessions run, what the teacher and students each commit to, and how the class stays clear, supportive, and productive.

## How We Run Working Sessions

- Every session has a clear objective and ends with one tangible output on your own screen — hands-on, building together, not just watching demos.
- Morgan and the whole class use Claude to build live, each on their own computers.
- The teacher sets and shares the session schedule and the specific deliverables to complete.
- In-week and weekend sessions follow the same structure; the ~3-hour weekend session repeats the week's content so anyone who missed the first can get the same material.
- Pauses happen during sessions to make sure everyone is keeping up, and there are breaks in the 3-hour session.
- The weekend session gives a choice: code along with the instructor, work in pairs, work alone, or opt out.
- Every session and lecture is recorded with a transcript.
- Always start with an agenda set ahead and always end on time.

## A Shared Baseline for Mixed Levels

- Each session establishes a shared baseline and essential vocabulary/process first, then separates core tasks from optional advanced work.
- Not everyone finishes at the same level — but everyone should be able to follow the session and reach the core learning outcome.
- Reflects Tech Fleet's inclusive purpose: giving people opportunity regardless of previous qualifications or experience.

## Questions & Office Hours

- The room stays open after each lecture for questions.
- Open office hours on Wednesdays; send questions ahead if you can't make it.
- Dedicated project time on Saturdays.

## Homework & Deliverables

- Two tiers of homework: basic (core, required) and extended (optional, advanced).
- Each deliverable comes with documentation on how it connects to and affects the rest of the work.

## One Source of Truth

- **Notion is the single source of truth.** Weekly objective, essential tasks, optional tasks, examples, estimated workload, and recordings all live there.
- No scattering resources across platforms; FigJam is for live collaboration.
- NotebookLM is provided to sift through recordings efficiently.

## The Teacher's Responsibilities

- Provide a structured learning pathway: introduce a concept, demonstrate it, let students practice hands-on, answer questions, confirm understanding before moving forward.
- Model the process out loud; show how the work and the prompting actually get done, including the steps in between, not just finished deliverables.
- Give clear, detailed expectations per week; define "done" and "good"; estimate how long it should take.
- Be clear about which role is speaking (instructor or teammate) and what the live session provides that Claude and notes cannot.
- Equip students with the framework to find answers themselves; teach how to scaffold with Claude.
- End feedback with an agreed action, owner, and date.

## Student Responsibilities — Our Promises

- Practice as much as possible and experiment with Claude.
- Ask Claude, teammates, and the teacher to close knowledge gaps.
- Speak up when something is needed.
- Get comfortable being uncomfortable and not knowing everything right away.
- Adopt an AI mindset to guide learning and curiosity.
- Take initiative, self-start, come to sessions having done individual prep.
- Share advice and learnings with each other.
- Be accountable for own learning while recognizing when to ask for help.

## Psychological Safety

- Everyone is at different levels — be kind, patient, supportive.
- React with curiosity and willingness to understand other perspectives.
- Respectful tone; make room for everyone to contribute; don't make anyone feel they're talking too much or too little.
- Psychological safety means anyone can respectfully question the structure, teaching, instructor, or teammates without being blamed or punished.
- Remember everyone has lives outside of class; assume good intent.

## Sharing Best Practices

- Decide together which channel(s) to use, make sure everyone's voice is heard, check in regularly to keep them current.
- Share tips and learnings through the student-built Notion and in class discussions.

## Avoiding "Chaos"

Chaos looks like: unclear expectations, the same questions asked and answered repeatedly, scattered or inconsistent content, too much too fast, criticism with no path forward.

Avoided with: one source of truth, clear weekly objectives, time estimates, a steady pace with weekly check-ins, constructive criticism that always offers a next step or solution.
