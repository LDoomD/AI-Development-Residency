# Audience Segmentation, Goals, and Use Cases — workshop instructions

Source: `Audience Segmentation, Goals, Motivations, and Use Cases Workshop
(Community).pdf`, a Tech Fleet / Figma Community workshop template,
downloaded 2026-10-24.

## What is this?

To properly understand how you should be solving problems for your
audiences, teams need to work to understand the day in the life of users.
This happens in a variety of ways. It involves a lot of brainstorming,
assumption mapping, and research to understand how to communicate goals
and motivations of your audiences. Understanding how you want to describe
your audience is up to you and the team and the clients involved. You can
use segmentation to describe your audiences from different perspectives:
where they live, how they behave, their background, or their
socioeconomic status. From there, you can work together to identify the
motivations and goals that audiences have, and what kinds of segments are
the most valuable to focus on for the business. This workshop helps teams
brainstorm about the segmented traits of potential audiences, and
communicate what they care about to gain a common understanding.

## Steps to run the workshop

1. Identify potential traits of segments based on all four lenses:
   a. Geography
   b. Behavior
   c. Demographics
   d. Socioeconomic status
2. Pick the most impactful traits and describe the audiences to focus on.
3. Identify the motivations of the audience segment.
4. Identify the situational goals and life goals that audience segment has
   in the end-to-end experience.
5. Identify the use case scenarios that are most impactful to support for
   each audience segment.

## Goals of the workshop

1. Build semantic labels around audience segments.
2. Agree to the audience segment descriptions the team wants to align on.
3. Brainstorm motivations, goals, and scenarios to support that will be
   important to audience segments.

## Deliverables of the workshop

1. Identify semantic labels for audience segments based on common traits.
2. Identify situational goals that are important to support in release
   planning.
3. Identify use case scenarios that are important to support in release
   planning.

## Why is it valuable?

1. The team can align with clients, stakeholders, and each other about the
   areas of focus that will bring the most value to audiences, based on
   how they describe the audiences.
2. The team can base user research, market research, and design
   implementation on what they define for segmentation.

## Who owns this deliverable?

The Product Manager hat owns the audience segmentation. As teams do more
research over time, the segmentation of audiences changes and evolves.
The product manager hat maintains the definitions of target audience
segments over time.
