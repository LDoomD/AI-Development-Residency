# Subjects

The starting set. Not a limit — the system has to handle anything added later.

Each subject is tagged with the **modes** it needs. Most need more than one, and that
is the whole reason the router exists: the same subject often has a part you must
understand, a part you must recall instantly, a part your ear or eye must learn to
judge, and a part your hands or mouth must actually do. Those four need different
treatment, and treating them the same is what makes studying feel like it isn't working.

**Modes**

- **MAP** — understand how the pieces fit together. Build a diagram, then explain it back.
- **DRILL** — recall exact things fast. One topic at a time, spaced out over days.
- **EXAMPLES** — train the eye or ear. Many examples, deliberately shuffled.
- **REPS** — your body or mouth doing the real thing. No diagram substitutes for this.

## What the tagging is doing

**Every subject on this list has a REPS or EXAMPLES component except Keto and
Biohacking.** Those two are almost purely knowledge. Everything else has a doing part,
and the research is blunt that reading about a doing part does not transfer to doing it.
The system must not let a subject sit in MAP mode forever and feel like progress.

**Six subjects need DRILL.** Two languages, music theory, the F&I product and compliance
facts, analytics syntax, and nutrition numbers. These are the only ones where flashcards
are the main event rather than a support.

**Nine subjects need EXAMPLES.** This is the mode most likely to be skipped, because it
looks like consumption rather than study. Recognizing an FM bell tone by ear, or why one
layout reads better than another, or why a joke lands — none of that comes from a
diagram. It comes from volume and deliberate shuffling.

**The languages split three ways** and are the clearest illustration of why one method
per subject fails: vocabulary wants DRILL (one topic at a time, spaced), listening wants
EXAMPLES (shuffled), speaking wants REPS. Run all three the same way and two of them
underperform.
