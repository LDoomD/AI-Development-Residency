## Why this matters

California regulates auto sales and financing more heavily than most states. A federally
competent F&I manager can still be non-compliant in California. This is the knowledge
that separates competent from best-in-state, and it is also the knowledge that keeps him
out of a lawsuit.

## What to research — verify every statute against a primary source

Leads to check. **These are unverified starting points from an assistant's memory —
confirm each one exists, get the real citation, and correct anything wrong. Do not
reproduce a lead as fact without verifying it.**

1. **The state law governing conditional sales contracts / auto financing.** Believed to
   be the Automobile Sales Finance Act, sometimes called Rees-Levering. Verify: real
   name, Civil Code sections, what it actually requires of the F&I office, and what the
   penalties for violation are (including whether a defective contract is voidable).
2. **Car Buyer's Bill of Rights.** Believed enacted around 2006. Verify what it actually
   requires — particularly around finance-charge markup limits, the itemization the
   buyer must receive, and used-vehicle history disclosure.
3. **The contract cancellation option** on used vehicles — believed to be a mandatory
   *offer* (purchasable, not free) on used cars under a price threshold. Verify: which
   vehicles, what price cap, what the dealer must offer, and what it costs the buyer.
4. **DMV salesperson licensing.** Does he personally need a California DMV salesperson
   license to work in F&I? What does it cost, how long does it take, what disqualifies
   someone, and is there a deadline after starting employment? **This is the most
   time-sensitive item in the brief — answer it first.**
5. **Rate markup / finance reserve limits in California.** Are there statutory caps on
   dealer participation, and how do they interact with federal fair-lending exposure?
6. **Add-on product regulation in California** — GAP in particular. Are there state rules
   on GAP pricing, refunds on early payoff, or mandatory refund notification? Several
   states have recently legislated on unearned GAP refunds — check whether California has.
7. **California-specific advertising and disclosure rules** that reach the F&I office.
8. **Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act** — how it interacts with selling a vehicle
   service contract, and whether anything about the VSC pitch is constrained by it.
9. **Recent enforcement.** California AG or DMV actions against dealers in the last few
   years — what fact patterns actually get prosecuted here.
10. **Anything else material** that a California F&I manager must know and an
    out-of-state one would not.
