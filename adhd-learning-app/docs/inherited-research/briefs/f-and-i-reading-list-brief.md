`specs/research/2026-08-08_f-and-i-reading-list.md`

## What he needs covered

1. **The F&I job itself** — the mechanics: menu selling, product knowledge (VSC, GAP,
   tire & wheel, prepaid maintenance, credit life/disability), lender relationships,
   reserve, compliance. Is there a genuine standard text for this role, or is the
   knowledge held in certification programs and trade press instead? **Answer that
   honestly — "no canonical book exists" is a valid and useful finding.**
2. **Trust and rapport with a stranger, fast** — the FBI behavioural literature. Robin
   Dreeke, Jack Schafer, Chris Voss. (He asked for "CIA tactics"; the real body of work
   is FBI — correct this factually.)
3. **Persuasion science** — Cialdini and anything else with genuine evidence behind it.
4. **Charisma and reading people** — Cabane, Van Edwards, and similar.
5. **Anything else** a first-year F&I manager should genuinely read.

## Required table

Rank in the order he should actually read them, most useful first.

| # | Title | Author | Year | ISBN | Why THIS book for THIS job | Evidence grade | Access route | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **"Why this book for this job"** must be specific to sitting in an F&I box with a
  stranger for 30 minutes. Not a generic blurb. If a famous book is *not* actually
  useful for this job, say so and rank it low or leave it out.
- **Evidence grade:** `High — rely on as-is` / `Moderate — directional` /
  `Extrapolated — tune by feel` / `Not covered`. A bestseller is not evidence. Label
  practitioner lore as lore — lore is still often useful, he just needs to know which
  he's holding.
- **Access route:** prefer in this order and label clearly —
  (a) free full-text from the author or publisher,
  (b) available through a public library via Libby/OverDrive,
  (c) available through an academic library — **he has a College of the Sequoias `.edu`
  account, so check EBSCO / ProQuest Ebook Central availability and interlibrary loan**,
  (d) purchase, with actual current price.
- **Cost:** real figures from a retailer or publisher page, or mark UNVERIFIED.

## Also include

- **Free long-form material from the same authors** — podcasts, recorded talks, the
  Black Swan Group's published material, author YouTube. Much of this is the author
  teaching the method at length, it's free, and it's citable. Give direct URLs you
  actually fetched.
- **A "skip these" section.** Popular books in this space that are thin, recycled, or
  actively misleading for a regulated sales environment. Name them and say why.
- **A minimum viable list**: if he only reads TWO things before his first week, which
  two, and why those.

## HARD CONDITIONS

- **Verify every book exists** — author, publisher, year, ISBN. Fetch a real listing.
  **If you cannot verify it, discard it.** Do not caveat. State how many candidates you
  discarded. A previous pass in this project caught two fabricated citations from search
  summarisers; assume summariser output is guilty until proven innocent.
- **Do not include or link to pirated copies, file-sharing mirrors, or shadow-library
  sites.** Legitimate access routes only — library, academic access, publisher, retailer,
  author-provided free material. This is a hard constraint on the deliverable.
- No padding. A short verified list beats a long speculative one.

## Style

One table, ranked. Short. He is tired and starts work imminently — the list needs to be
actionable at a glance, not read like a literature review.
