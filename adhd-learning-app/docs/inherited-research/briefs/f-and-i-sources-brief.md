## What to research

### A. F&I practitioner resources — what actually teaches the job

- **"Product Prep"** — Orlando named this specifically as a YouTube channel. Find it,
  verify it exists, report the actual channel URL, subscriber count, what it teaches, and
  whether it's genuinely useful for a new F&I manager. If it does not exist under that
  name, say so plainly and find the closest real thing.
- Other F&I-specific training: named YouTube channels, podcasts, courses, trainers.
- **AFIP certification** (Association of Finance & Insurance Professionals) — verify what
  it is, what it costs, whether it's the industry-standard compliance credential.
- NADA, F&I and Showroom magazine, Automotive News — what does the trade press actually
  say about menu presentation and product penetration benchmarks?
- **Real benchmark numbers if they exist and are citable**: average PVR, product
  penetration rates by product, typical reserve caps. Cite the source or mark UNVERIFIED.

### B. Trust-building and behavioral influence — the "CIA tactics" request

Orlando asked for "CIA tactics." **Note honestly that the best-known material in this
space is FBI, not CIA** — correct him factually and give him the real thing:

- **Robin Dreeke** — former FBI Behavioral Analysis Program chief. *It's Not All About
  "Me"*, *The Code of Trust*. Verify credentials and books.
- **Jack Schafer** — former FBI. *The Like Switch*. Verify.
- **Chris Voss** — former FBI lead international kidnapping negotiator. *Never Split the
  Difference*. Verify — and specifically assess tactical empathy, mirroring, labeling,
  and the "that's right" vs "you're right" distinction for a menu-presentation context.
- **Chase Hughes** — behavioral influence. Verify credentials carefully; assess whether
  the claims are evidence-backed or self-promotional.
- For each: **is there peer-reviewed support, or is this practitioner lore?** Both can be
  useful; the point is to label which is which.

### C. Persuasion science — what actually replicates

- **Cialdini** — *Influence*, *Pre-Suasion*. The six/seven principles. **Critically:
  report the replication record.** Some social-priming and persuasion effects failed
  replication in the 2010s. Which of Cialdini's principles hold up and which are shaky?
  This matters — do not present all seven as equally solid if they are not.
- Anything else with real evidence relevant to a one-shot, high-stakes, seated sales
  conversation with a stranger.

### D. Charisma and communication

- Olivia Fox Cabane, *The Charisma Myth*; Vanessa Van Edwards, *Captivate* / *Cues*.
- Verify existence, assess evidence base honestly — much of this genre is
  popular-science-shaped but thinly evidenced. Say so where true.

### E. Ethics line — mandatory section

F&I is a **regulated** environment. Some influence techniques are illegal here.
Explicitly map which techniques are legitimate rapport/clarity work versus which cross
into deception, and connect this to the actual rules: payment packing, powerbooking,
straw purchase, Reg Z, Reg B/ECOA, Red Flags, GLBA Safeguards, IRS Form 8300.
**Verify these rule names and what they require — the existing map lists them from
memory.** A technique that raises close rate and also gets him fired or sued is a bad
technique, and the document must say so.

## HARD CONDITIONS

- **Verify every source exists.** Real book → author, publisher, year, ISBN where
  findable. Real channel → URL. Real regulation → the actual CFR/statute or a primary
  regulator page. **If you cannot verify it, DISCARD it — do not include it with a
  caveat.** State how many candidates you discarded.
- **Grade every claim:** `High — rely on as-is` / `Moderate — directional` /
  `Extrapolated — tune by feel` / `Not covered`.
- **Separate "evidenced" from "popular."** A bestselling book is not evidence. Say
  plainly when something is practitioner lore — lore can still be worth using, but he
  should know which is which.
- **Surface contradictions.** Where trainers disagree with each other or with research,
  present both.
- **No padding.** "Not covered" is a valid answer.

## Style

Research file: terse, tables, graded, cited.
Learning file: plain English, no jargon, readable by a tired person at 6am.
