`specs/research/2026-08-08_f-and-i-adjacent-reading.md` — **this file only.**

## The gaps to fill

Seven areas, each genuinely load-bearing for this job but absent from the existing list.
Find the best verified book (or two) per area. If an area has no good book, say so.

1. **Behavioral economics of money decisions under pressure.** The single biggest gap.
   The customer is fatigued, spending more than they planned, and being asked to buy
   protection against low-probability/high-cost events. Loss framing is already graded
   **High** evidence in `2026-08-08_f-and-i-resources.md`. Who wrote the definitive
   accessible treatment — Kahneman, Thaler, Ariely, someone else? Which is most useful
   to a practitioner rather than a student?

2. **How consumer auto lending actually works.** Credit scoring, underwriting, tiers,
   advance, subprime auto. He needs to understand what the lender on the other end of
   the approval is doing and why deals get structured the way they are. Include serious
   journalism on subprime auto lending — knowing how the industry harms people is part
   of not being that person.

3. **Money shame and financial anxiety.** Customers arrive embarrassed about their
   credit. Handling that well is a real skill and probably the difference between a
   trusted F&I manager and a hated one. Is there a credible book on the psychology of
   money shame?

4. **Negotiation beyond Voss.** Fisher & Ury, Shell, or whatever the current best is —
   principled/interest-based negotiation as a complement to tactical empathy.

5. **Emotional regulation and rejection tolerance.** He'll run many deals a day, hear no
   constantly, and needs to walk into deal eleven as fresh as deal one. **Note: Orlando
   has ADHD** — prefer material that is compatible with that rather than willpower-based
   productivity advice, which the research in this repo has repeatedly found unsupported.

6. **Numbers literacy for his own metrics.** Reading his own PVR, penetration rate, and
   chargeback data honestly and spotting when a number is being spun at him. This also
   overlaps his Data Analytics subject in `specs/subjects.md` — flag any overlap.

7. **Ethical selling in a regulated environment.** Selling hard and selling honestly at
   the same time. Not compliance law — that's covered — but the practitioner mindset.

- **Access route**, labelled: free from author → Libby/OverDrive → academic via his
  College of the Sequoias `.edu` (EBSCO / ProQuest Ebook Central / interlibrary loan) →
  purchase with a fetched price. Legitimate routes only; no file-sharing or shadow
  libraries.
- Include a **skip list** for popular books in these areas that are thin or misleading.
- Rank everything into a single **"read after the core five"** order at the end.
