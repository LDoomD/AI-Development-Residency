---

## Bottom line

**The evidence does not support a single learning system that works identically across all
subject types.** It supports a weaker but still useful claim:

- A small number of **scheduling and retrieval** principles are broadly robust.
- **Representational** techniques — including visual-spatial node/relation decomposition —
  are strongly moderated by material type, and in one well-documented case
  **reverse sign**.

---

## ⚠️ The single most damaging finding

**Brunmair & Richter (2019).** *Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning
and its moderators.* *Psychological Bulletin* 145(11), 1029–1052. `10.1037/bul0000209` ·
PMID 31556629. **59 studies, 238 effect sizes, 158 samples.**

The *same* technique (interleaving), applied to different material:

| Material type | g |
|---|---|
| **Paintings** | **+0.67** |
| Mathematical tasks | +0.34 |
| Expository text | non-significant |
| **Words / vocabulary** | **−0.39** ← *blocking beats interleaving* |

**Material type is not a minor moderator. It flips the recommendation.** A system that
applies one setting to all subjects will actively harm vocabulary learning while helping
visual discrimination.

---

## ⚠️ The second most damaging finding

**Hattie, Biggs & Purdie (1996).** *Effects of learning skills interventions on student
learning: A meta-analysis.* *RER* 66(2), 99–136. `10.3102/00346543066002099`.
**51 interventions.**

Verdict: training should be *"in context, use tasks within the same domain as the target
content."* A direct empirical verdict **against decontextualized general learning systems**.

**Note the one carve-out:** *"other than for simple mnemonic performance."* Pure mnemonic
techniques (loci, keyword) are the single category exempted — they *are* portable, but only
for the narrow job of memorizing arbitrary material. They do not make you understand anything.

## Domain-by-domain verdict

| Content type | Does node/relation decomposition apply? | Grade | What the evidence favours instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical / systems / conceptual** | **Yes — best-supported case** | High | Learner-*constructed* maps (g = 0.72) > studying given maps (0.43) |
| **Narrative (history)** | **Partially** — captures causal structure, misses evidence & perspective | Moderate | Discipline-specific heuristics: sourcing, contextualization, corroboration |
| **Narrative (literature, biography)** | Unknown | **Not covered** | — |
| **Procedural / motor** | **No supporting evidence**; specificity principle argues against | Not covered (technique) / High (limited transfer) | Reps in the target task |
| **Vocabulary / words** | **No — evidence points the other way** | High | Blocked, spaced, retrieval-based practice |
| **Grammar** | **Plausibly yes** — genuinely relational systems | Moderate | Explicit instruction > implicit |
| **Visual art / perceptual** | **No — the opposite method wins** | High | Interleaved exemplar exposure (g = 0.67) |
| **Design taste** | Unknown | **Not covered** | — |

### Narrative
**Reisman (2012)**, *Cognition and Instruction* 30(1), `10.1080/07370008.2011.634081` —
"Reading Like a Historian," six months, five San Francisco high schools, 236 students. The
curriculum is built on **sourcing, contextualization, corroboration, close reading** —
heuristics extracted from what historians actually do, not a node map. Wineburg's position
underneath it is an explicit **rejection of a single set of skills transcending disciplines**.

Narrative carries three things nodes handle badly: **temporal sequence, contested evidence,
authorial perspective.**

### Procedural / motor
**Oppici & Panchuk (2022)**, *Psychology of Sport and Exercise* 59:102118,
`10.1016/j.psychsport.2021.102118` — systematic review, 17 studies. **Specificity** of
transfer between sports with overlapping affordances; **generality** between sports without.
Either way the portable component is thin. Six studies showed an expertise effect.

**No verified evidence** that decomposing a motor skill into functional nodes improves motor
acquisition. Most defensible use: a **pre-practice orientation device**, explicitly separated
from practice. **Do not let it substitute for reps.**

### Aesthetic / perceptual — the most interesting result
The correct method is nearly the **opposite** of schema deconstruction.

- **Kornell & Bjork (2008)**, *Psych Science* 19:585–592, `10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02127.x`
  — the foundational painting-style induction study.
- **Brunmair & Richter (2019)** — paintings show the **largest** interleaving benefit of any
  material type.
- **Kellman, Massey & Son (2010)**, *Topics in Cognitive Science* 2(2), PMID 25163790 —
  perceptual learning modules produce durable encoding changes, trained by **high-volume,
  interleaved, feedback-rich exemplar exposure**, not by verbal or schematic decomposition.

And **Sala & Gobet (2020)**, *Memory & Cognition* 48(8), PMID 32728850 — 54 studies, 6,984
children: once design quality is controlled, music training's effect on cognitive and
academic skills is **null and highly consistent**. Significant effects appeared only in
lower-quality designs.

## Far transfer

**Sala et al. (2019)**, *Collabra: Psychology* 5(1):18, `10.1525/collabra.203` —
**second-order meta-analysis** across working-memory training, video games, music, chess,
exergames. Near transfer present; **far transfer small or null, and disappearing entirely**
once placebo and publication bias are controlled.

> *"The lack of generalization of skills acquired by training is thus an invariant of human
> cognition."*

**Barnett & Ceci (2002)**, *Psych Bulletin* 128(4), PMID 12081085 — the canonical
nine-dimension taxonomy. More careful than the popular reading: a single far-transfer effect
size **oversimplifies**; transfer under some conditions is substantial.

**How to hold both:** these concern different phenomena — *training a capacity and expecting
other capacities to improve* vs *applying a study procedure to new material*. The field
conflates them constantly.

**What DOES generalize within a domain:** **Uttal et al. (2013)**, *Psych Bulletin* 139,
PMID 22663761 — 217 studies, **g = 0.47**, stable over delays, transferring to untrained
spatial tasks. Read precisely: spatial training transfers to *other spatial tasks*, not to
non-spatial learning.

## Payoff varies by domain regardless of system quality

**Donker et al. (2014)**, *Educational Research Review* 11, `10.1016/j.edurev.2013.11.002` —
58 studies, 180 effect sizes. Same strategy family, **~3.5× spread by subject**:

| Domain | g |
|---|---|
| Writing | 1.25 |
| Science | 0.73 |
| Mathematics | 0.66 |
| Reading comprehension | 0.36 |

**Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014)**, *Psych Science* 25(8), PMID 24986855 — variance
explained by deliberate practice: **games 26% · music 21% · sports 18% · education 4% ·
professions <1%**.

**Contradiction preserved:** **Ericsson & Harwell (2019)**, *Front Psychol* 10:2396,
`10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02396` — applying stricter criteria, reports **29%, rising to 61%
corrected**. Different estimands; both can be right. **Not adjudicated.**

> ROI is a function of the domain's **feedback structure**, not of the system's design quality.

## The techniques that DO generalize

**Dunlosky et al. (2013)**, *PSPI* 14(1), 4–58, `10.1177/1529100612453266` · PMID 26173288 —
evaluated generalizability across materials, conditions, learners, and criterion tasks:

| Utility | Techniques |
|---|---|
| **HIGH** | **Practice testing · Distributed practice** |
| MODERATE | Elaborative interrogation · Self-explanation · Interleaved practice |
| **LOW** | Summarization · Highlighting · **Keyword mnemonics** · **Imagery for text** · Rereading |

> ⚠️ **"Imagery for text" — the nearest published cousin to visual-spatial deconstruction of
> prose — is in the LOW tier.** Concept mapping (g = 0.58) fares much better. The apparent
> difference: **active construction of explicit relations** vs **forming mental pictures**.
> *(That reconciliation is inference, not a published finding — Grade: Extrapolated.)*

Supporting: Rowland (2014), *Psych Bulletin* 140(6) — the definitive testing-effect
meta-analysis. Cepeda et al. (2006), *Psych Bulletin* 132(3) — distributed practice synthesis.

## Honest answer to the design question

If the system is built on the premise that one visual-spatial method works for any subject,
**the evidence does not support that premise as stated.** It supports a modified premise:

- **A thin universal layer is defensible:** retrieval practice + spaced scheduling. These are
  the only two techniques rated HIGH for cross-material generalization.
- **A representational layer must be domain-conditional.** Node/relation mapping is
  well-evidenced for structured conceptual material, plausible for grammar and historical
  causation, **unevidenced for motor skill**, and **actively contraindicated** as the primary
  method for vocabulary and for perceptual/aesthetic discrimination.
- **The honest framing** is not "one system for any subject" but
  **"a fixed scheduling engine plus a subject-type router."** Smaller claim, and the one the
  literature will actually back.

## Contradictions left standing

1. **Deliberate practice magnitude** — Macnamara (~11–26%) vs Ericsson & Harwell (29–61%).
   Different construct definitions. Unresolved.
2. **Far transfer: never vs sometimes** — Sala et al. call non-generalization "an invariant";
   Barnett & Ceci say a single effect size is the wrong question.
3. **Visual representation: helpful vs low-utility** — concept mapping g = 0.58 vs "imagery
   for text" rated LOW.
4. **Spatial training transfers vs training doesn't** — Uttal g = 0.47 vs Sala null. Both
   right at different distances on the Barnett–Ceci dimensions.
