# Book extraction — Pre-Suasion (Robert Cialdini)

**Author:** Robert Cialdini, PhD — same author as Influence (already extracted); social psychologist, Arizona State. This is his 2016 sequel, built partly on the same three-year undercover fieldwork in sales training programs.
**Edition used:** Simon & Schuster, 2016.
**Original mode:** companion to Influence (#5); the presentation/priming layer the F&I reading list flagged for menu-presentation psychology.
Extracted per the pipeline in `specs/reference-library-workflow.md`, 2026-08-13. Full read: author's note + all 14 chapters (Parts 1–3, including the ethics chapter and Post-Suasion).

## Author's stated purpose

From the author's note and ch. 1, near-verbatim: the book "identifies what savvy communicators do **before** delivering a message to get it accepted." His core empirical claim from the undercover years: the top performers in every persuasion profession didn't have better offers or better arguments — "they spent more time crafting what they did and said before making a request." Central thesis (stated twice, ch. 2 and ch. 4): **"frequently the factor most likely to determine a person's choice in a situation is not the one that counsels most wisely there; it is one that has been elevated in attention at the time of the decision."** He names these windows *privileged moments*, and the tools that open them *openers*. Unlike Influence (written as consumer defense), he says explicitly this book teaches harnessing influence — which is why he devotes ch. 13 entirely to why unethical use destroys the organization from the inside. Closing line of the book: "who we are with respect to any choice is where we are, attentionally, in the moment before the choice."

## Framework — the underlying mental model

Three-part structure, each part one mechanism:

1. **Attention (Part 1):** What's salient is judged important (focusing illusion — Kahneman: "Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it"); what's focal is judged causal. Certain content grabs attention by itself (sexual, threatening, novel/different) and certain content *holds* it (self-relevant, unfinished/Zeigarnik, mysterious).
2. **Association (Part 2):** All thought is associative; language's main function is not description but *channeling attention to a sector of reality pre-loaded with favorable associations*. Openers work in proportion to the strength of the link between opener concept and target behavior. Links can also be manufactured from scratch (ad pairings, Pavlov).
3. **Optimization (Part 3):** The best concepts to pre-load are the six Influence principles + a new seventh, **unity** (shared identity — being together via kinship/place, acting together via synchrony/co-creation), sequenced by the **core motives model**: cultivate relationship (reciprocity, liking, unity) → reduce uncertainty (social proof, authority) → motivate action (consistency, scarcity). Durability requires **post-suasion**: lock momentary shifts in with active/effortful/voluntary commitments, or engineer recurring cues into the environment.

## Claims to NOT repeat as fact

- **Word-priming behavior effects** (scrambled-sentence aggression +48%, elderly-words-slow-walking genre) — this is the epicenter of social psychology's replication crisis. The *strong-association* effects (metaphor framing, agenda setting, implementation intentions) hold; the subtle-single-word-exposure effects often don't. Teach the mechanism as real-but-fragile; never cite the specific percentages.
- **Embodiment effects** (heavy clipboard → serious candidate; warm cup → warm feelings) — failed or shrunk in major replications. Drop.
- **Stereotype threat magnitudes** (letter-grade improvements from two self-affirmation essays) — real phenomenon, shrunken effect sizes under replication; keep the testing-hygiene practices as low-cost/no-harm, drop the dramatic numbers.
- **The Valentine-Street bravado study and similar small-N French field studies** (guitar-case phone numbers, sunny-day dates) — charming, tiny samples, one lab (Guéguen, whose work has faced integrity questions). Illustrations only, never evidence.
- **Facilitated anecdotes** (Jim the alarm salesman, the pyramid-scheme bus, Erickson's truck-noise trick) — practitioner lore, valuable as pattern illustrations, not data.
- **Flag-exposure → 8-month Republican shift** — one striking study; the persistence claim outruns what a single result can carry.
- **The banner-ad "no wear-out below awareness" finding** — interesting, single line of work, don't generalize.

## Compliance note specific to F&I

This is structurally the most dangerous book in the library **and** the one with the most explicit built-in ethics case. Pre-suasion's whole point is influence that operates before scrutiny — the customer never sees the opener working. Applying the project's standing test (natural vs. fabricated levers): **channeling attention to true, decision-relevant features (real risks, real costs of repairs, genuine popularity data, honestly admitted drawbacks) is legitimate pre-suasion; installing openers that manufacture receptivity the facts haven't earned (single-chute self-labeling questions, fictive-family intimacy, fear without remedy, unitary evaluation of one product) is exploitation.** Cialdini's own ch. 13 supplies the enforcement argument in the only currency that moves a sales manager: undetected unethical culture still costs ~20% performance, drives out the honest staff, and retains the people who will eventually cheat the store itself.
