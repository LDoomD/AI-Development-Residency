## Executive summary

Six products. Four researched in depth. Here is what matters most.

1. **Elevate and Pinnacle are the same product.** Both brochures are dated 08/22 and are
   word-for-word identical except the cover. Orlando's read of his own paperwork was right.
2. **Only Platinum is exclusionary.** Silver and Gold are stated-component. This is the
   honest answer to "why is Platinum more expensive."
3. **Express 5's windshield coverage never pays for a replacement.** Repair only. On a
   modern car with cameras in the glass, that's a real gap.
4. **Roadside is sold twice.** Both the VSC and Express 5 include it, from two different
   administrators, with near-identical benefits.
5. **GAP cannot legally be sold** in California if the customer is financing less than
   **70% of the vehicle's retail value.** Not "shouldn't" — cannot.
6. **Stargard's theft benefit is a refund**, capped at $1,295 — not a cash payout.

## 1. Elevate / Pinnacle — the vehicle service contract

**Administrator:** Ascent Administration Services, LLC (Mesa / Tempe, AZ). Phone 866-660-7003.

**Elevate is the pamphlet. Pinnacle is the contract.** Same coverage, two names — a
sales-facing brand and the paperwork name. Confirmed two ways: Orlando's own documents,
and both published brochures being identical text.

### Coverage type — the key question

| Tier | Type | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Silver** | Stated-component | Covers **only** the parts on the list |
| **Gold** | Stated-component | Silver's list **plus** more named parts |
| **Platinum** | **Exclusionary** | Covers **everything factory-installed except** the exclusions list |

Grade: **High** — read directly from brochure text.

Plain language: *stated-component* means the contract names what's covered. *Exclusionary*
means it names what's **not** covered, and everything else is in. Exclusionary is broader
coverage. That is the real reason Platinum costs more.

### What each tier covers

| Tier | Systems |
|---|---|
| **Silver** | Engine, cooling, automatic transmission, manual transmission internals, drive components (FWD/AWD/RWD/4x4), fluids with a covered repair, seals & gaskets |
| **Gold** | Silver **plus** suspension, steering, brakes, electrical, A/C, fuel system |
| **Platinum** | Silver + Gold + all other factory components except the exclusions |

**Clutch is never covered.** Not on any tier, including Platinum. Grade: **High.**

**Platinum's main exclusions:** clutch assembly, exhaust manifold, batteries, brake
rotors/drums/pads, catalytic converter, mufflers, frame and body panels, tires, wheels,
glass, convertible tops, lighting, trim, paint, bumpers, safety restraints, screens,
GPS/nav, radio, backup camera, Bluetooth, bulbs, fuses, software, maintenance items,
wiper blades.

| Eco | Plug wires, emissions canisters, PCV, distributor, smog pump, catalytic converter, muffler, exhaust, **hybrid drive batteries** |
| Oil Change | $50/change for 6, 8, or 10 changes, paid direct to the shop |

**Surcharges apply for:** commercial use, diesel, turbo/supercharger, 4WD, AWD, lift kit.

### ⚠ The deductible discrepancy — ask about this

The brochure **banner** says "$100 Disappearing Deductible." The brochure **body text**
says $0 at the selling dealer / $100 elsewhere.

Those are different things. A true disappearing deductible shrinks over claim-free years.
What's described is a dealer-vs-elsewhere mechanic. **Confirm with the F&I manager which
one actually governs** — a customer will ask, and the two answers aren't compatible.
Grade: **Moderate** (brochure copy is internally inconsistent).

## 3. The roadside overlap — say this before they find it

Both products include roadside. Confirmed side by side from primary brochures.

| | Express 5 (SafeRide) | Elevate/Pinnacle (Ascent) |
|---|---|---|
| Uses | 3 per 12 months | 3 per contract year |
| Towing | inside a $100/incident cap | up to $100 |
| Flat tire | inside that $100 | up to $50 |
| Jump-start | inside that $100 | up to $50 |
| Lockout | inside that $100 | up to $50 |
| Fluid delivery | yes, customer buys the fluid | yes, customer buys the fluid |

Grade: **High.**

A customer buying both gets **two separate roadside programs** doing nearly the same thing,
from two administrators, with two phone numbers and no coordination.

**Draft talking point** — wording to be adapted to how the store actually speaks:

> "You already get roadside with your service contract. Express 5's roadside is a second,
> separate safety net — not an upgrade to the first one. Where Express 5 actually adds
> something new is the windshield, tire and wheel, dent and ding, and key coverage."

Saying this before the customer notices is the difference between sounding like a
consultant and sounding like someone who hoped they wouldn't ask.

## 4. Stargard — theft protection

**Correct spelling is "Stargard"** — one word, no "u". Every source uses it.
**Company:** ALDS, Inc. dba Stargard GPS, La Mesa, CA. Hotline 1-800-443-5535.

⚠ Confirm the dealership's paperwork says "Stargard." If not, this research doesn't apply.

### What it actually is

A **physical GPS device** with app tracking (geofencing, speed and after-hours alerts),
**plus** a contractual money-back guarantee. It is **not** a VIN-etch or registry product.

### The theft benefit — read this carefully

| Term | Detail |
|---|---|
| Payout | **Refund of the purchase price paid, up to $1,295** |
| What it is **not** | A flat cash bonus. Some competitor products pay one; this doesn't |
| Trigger | Vehicle stolen **and** Stargard unable to assist recovery |
| Waiting period | **30 days** |
| Customer must | File a police report **within 24 hours** of discovering the theft |
| Term | **3 years**; free renewal if on time, **$99** if late (up to 6 months) |
| **Cancellable?** | **No — non-cancellable and non-transferable** |
| Disputes | Binding arbitration (JAMS), class-action waiver |

Grade: **High** — from Stargard's own terms PDF.

### Effectiveness — be careful what you quote

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| ~79% of stolen vehicles recovered overall (2025, 642,000+ thefts) | **High** — NICB |
| 34% recovered same day **without** any tracking device | **High** — NICB |
| NICB says tracking devices are "very effective" | **Moderate** — no isolated percentage published |
| **"90%+ recovery with tracking devices"** | **Discarded.** Could not be verified on any NICB page. Recirculated marketing. **Do not quote it** |
| Stargard's own recovery rate | Not covered — not published |

### Company

**BBB rating: D-**, not accredited, 3 unresponded complaints. Common complaint theme:
tracking inconsistent or device stuck. Owner forums report dealers pre-installing Stargard
and presenting it as non-removable — which is exactly the practice California's disclosure
rules target. Grade: **High** for BBB, **Moderate** for forum reports.

## 5. Cilajet — paint protection

**Verified:** aviation-origin ceramic coating, cilajet.com, ceramic polymer with graphene,
9H hardness, dealer-applied only, automotive since 2007. Conforms to **Boeing D6-17487**
and **Airbus AIMS09-00-002**.

### ⚠ There is no national warranty document

Cilajet's own FAQ sends buyers to their dealer. Published durations **conflict across
Cilajet's own pages** — 3 years, 5 years, 10 years depending where you look.

**Orlando must pull the actual warranty certificate his dealership issues.** Grade: **High**
(the absence was confirmed, not assumed).

| Item | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Transferable | Yes | Moderate |
| Voids it | Solvent-based cleaners, bug/tar/sap removers; repaint or panel replacement without reapplication | High |
| Maintenance | Regular washing, no solvent cleaners | High |
| Interior products | **Cilajet HIDE** and **Cilajet FAB** — carpet, mats, seats | High |
| Who pays a failed-finish claim | **Not covered** — not published |

### On the Boeing/Airbus claim — the honest version

Both spec conformances come **only from Cilajet's marketing**. No independent body confirms
them. Grade: **Extrapolated / vendor claim only.**

Meeting an aerospace material spec means the chemistry passed a lab test **for aviation
requirements**. It does not mean anyone independently evaluated it as a **car** coating.
No third-party durability testing was found.

The claim is technically true and narrow. "Boeing-grade" does more emotional work in a
pitch than the spec supports for daily UV, road salt, and washing. Say that plainly when
someone asks "is this really worth it" — it will land better than the pitch will.

### Category risk

**FTC v. Napleton Automotive Group (2022), $10M settlement** explicitly named "paint
protection" as a junk-fee add-on packed into deals without consent. Grade: **High** (FTC.gov).

Nothing found against **Cilajet specifically** — no FTC action, class action, or state AG
action. Grade: **Not covered** (nothing found ≠ clean record).

The enforcement risk in this category is about **how it's sold**, not the product.

### Pricing

Reported retail $399–$2,000+, commonly $799–$995 for a sedan. One single-source blog claims
dealer cost of $30–$80 per application. Grade: **Extrapolated — do not repeat the markup
figure to anyone as fact.**

## 6. GAP and California law

> Builds on `2026-08-08_california-f-and-i-law.md`. That file already covers the DMV
> license, dealer reserve cap, GAP's 4% cap / 30-day refund / treble damages, and the CARS
> Act. Not repeated here.

### 🔴 The 70% rule — the most important thing in this document

**GAP cannot legally be sold in California if the amount financed is less than 70% of the
vehicle's retail value.** Civil Code §2982.12. Grade: **High** — confirmed against statute text.

Not "shouldn't." **Cannot.** If the customer put enough down, or has enough trade equity,
that they're financing under 70% of what the car is worth, selling them GAP is prohibited.

**This is a gift for consultative selling.** It gives a legally clean, honest reason to say
"you don't need this" — and a customer who hears that from an F&I manager believes
everything else they say afterward.

### Other hard GAP rules

| Rule | Detail | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Required warning | Bold: **"YOU CANNOT BE REQUIRED TO BUY A GAP WAIVER."** Heading min 12-point, body min 10-point | High |
| Separate document | GAP terms must be in their own document, separately signed | High |
| Coverage-limit mismatch | Can't sell if amount financed exceeds the product's stated max | High |
| LTV over the max | Can't sell — **unless** conspicuously disclosed and separately acknowledged | High |
| Seller/administrator ID | Must appear on the document | High |

### GAP waiver vs. GAP insurance

| | GAP **waiver** | GAP **insurance** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Debt-cancellation promise from the seller/creditor | An actual indemnity insurance product |
| Governed by | Civil Code §2982.12 | Insurance Code §1758.992 |
| License to sell | None needed | **Credit insurance agent license required** |

What a CA dealership sells is almost certainly the **waiver**. Grade: **High.**

### VSC cancellation in California — different clock from GAP

| Product | Full-refund window |
|---|---|
| **VSC, new vehicle** | **60 days** |
| **VSC, used** (no manufacturer warranty) | **30 days** |
| **GAP waiver** | **30 days flat** |

Civil Code **§1794.41**. Cancellation fee capped at **the lesser of 10% or $25**. Refund
due within **30 days**. If a claim was already paid, the refund drops to pro-rata even
inside the window. Grade: **High.**

> Correction to the prior file: the operative statute is **§1794.41**, not §1794.4.

### Required VSC disclosures — Insurance Code §12820

| Requirement | Grade |
|---|---|
| If backed by an insurer: contract must print the guarantee language, the insurer's name and address, and the CDI contact (**1-800-927-4357**) with the 60-day language | High |
| Exclusions and buyer duties must be **bold**, same size or larger than surrounding text | High |
| Obligor's name, address, phone, and VSCP license number must appear | Moderate |

### The "not required to buy" rule — the honest current state

| Product | Statute today? |
|---|---|
| **GAP** | **Yes** — explicit, bold, mandated wording |
| **VSC** | **No equivalent statute found** as of Aug 2026 |
| **Both, from Oct 1 2026** | CARS Act (Civil Code §1784.42) requires written disclosure that any add-on is "optional and not required to purchase the vehicle or obtain financing" |

**What to do at the desk today:** say it every time, for every product. GAP legally requires
it. VSC doesn't yet — but tying either to credit approval risks Unfair Competition Law
exposure (the Paul Blanco fact pattern), and it becomes mandatory for both in about seven
weeks anyway. Build the habit now.

### GAP refund-on-early-payoff — where the industry keeps getting sued

| Case | Detail | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Wells Fargo (C.D. Cal. 8:18-cv-00332) | Refused GAP refunds on early payoff. Reported **$500M settlement**, 2021 | Moderate |
| Toyota Motor Credit (2:20-cv-10518) | **California subclass.** Alleges tens of millions in unearned GAP fees retained, refunded only on request | Moderate |
| Honda Financial Services | Same pattern, CA court; specifics unconfirmed | Moderate |

The exposure is not at the desk — it's in the administrator's back office after a trade-in,
refinance, or payoff. Worth asking the GAP administrator: **do you refund unearned premium
proactively, or only when the customer asks?**
