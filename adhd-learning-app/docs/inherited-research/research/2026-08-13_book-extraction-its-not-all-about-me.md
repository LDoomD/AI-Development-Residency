# Book extraction — It's Not All About Me (Robin Dreeke)

**Author:** Robin Dreeke — former Marine Corps officer, FBI special agent, chief of the FBI's Counterintelligence Behavioral Analysis Program.
**Edition used:** Revised & Updated, 2023. ISBN 9781637748466.
**Original mode:** rapport/trust-building for F&I (per `specs/research/2026-08-08_f-and-i-reading-list.md`).
Extracted per the pipeline in `specs/reference-library-workflow.md`, 2026-08-13.

## Author's stated purpose

From the Introduction, near-verbatim: Dreeke states the book offers "a highly actionable process for treating individuals the way we all wish to be treated every minute of every day," distilled from his FBI counterintelligence/behavioral-analysis career and refined by social/evolutionary psychology research. He's explicit about the audience and use case: this is meant for *any* relationship — "at home, work, play, or even in conversations with strangers" — not a sales-specific manual, despite how directly it maps onto F&I discovery/rapport.

**Explicit ethical warning, stated by the author himself** (from "Putting It All Together," the closing section): *"The content in this book is so effective that I urge the reader to think carefully about how it is used. I do not endorse or condone the application of these skills in malicious or deceptive ways."* This is the author's own compliance flag, not something added externally — worth citing directly if a compliance question ever comes up about any technique below.

## Framework — the underlying mechanism

**"It's not about how you make people feel about you — it's about how you make them feel about themselves."** Every one of the 10 techniques is a variant of the same move: suspend your own agenda/ego and make the other person the most important thing in the room. The mechanism Dreeke cites repeatedly is dopamine release from feeling heard/validated (real neurochemical claim, not independently verified in this pass — flag as author's claim, not confirmed research finding) and evolutionary reciprocal-altruism (humans reciprocate gifts/help because ancestors who didn't help others didn't get helped in return).

The 10 techniques are explicitly cumulative, not exclusive — Dreeke states no single technique guarantees success, but combining several significantly raises the odds of a good interaction.

## Retention note

Given the earlier retention research (`2026-08-13_book-extraction-retention-methodology.md`), the useful reconciliation prompt for each of these is short: *"why does this work, in one sentence, in your own words"* — not a full essay.
