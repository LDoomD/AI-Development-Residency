# California F&I Law — Study Aid

**Not legal advice.** This is a study aid built for a specific F&I role and points at primary
sources; it is not a substitute for asking your dealership's compliance officer or a licensed
attorney about anything ambiguous or specific to your situation. Where something is genuinely
unclear below, that's flagged and the right move is to ask your compliance officer, not to
guess.

Researched 2026-08-08. Everything else in this repo's research so far covers federal law only —
this fills the California-specific gap. Every claim below was checked against a primary source
(leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, dmv.ca.gov, or oag.ca.gov). Law-firm blogs and vendor pages were
used only to find leads, never as the sole citation. **7 leads were discarded** because they
could not be confirmed against a primary source — they're listed at the end of each section so
you know what was checked and rejected, not silently dropped.

## 1. First 30 days — what you must personally do or hold

### 1.1 DMV vehicle salesperson license (answer this first — most time-sensitive)

**Yes, you need it.** California defines "vehicle salesperson" broadly enough to cover F&I: it's
anyone employed by a dealer who sells, negotiates, or is regularly employed to negotiate vehicle
sales *or contracts related to sales*. Presenting and signing deal paperwork, itemizing charges,
and upselling GAP/VSC in the F&I box is squarely inside that definition — this is not a
gray area exception for F&I versus sales-floor staff.
— Veh. Code §§ 675, 11800; DMV: [Vehicle Salesperson License](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-industry-services/occupational-licensing/occupational-licenses/vehicle-salesperson-license/). **Grade: High.**

**There is no grace period to work unlicensed.** The statute makes it unlawful to act as a
salesperson without *already holding* a license or a temporary permit — not "start now, license
later." Verbatim: *"It shall be unlawful for any person to act as a vehicle salesperson without
having first procured a license or temporary permit issued by the department..."*
— Veh. Code § 11800 (text confirmed directly, verbatim). **Grade: High.**

**The temporary permit is the real-world bridge.** DMV can issue a temporary permit valid for
**up to 120 days** while it completes the background investigation — this is what lets a new
hire actually start working quickly instead of waiting for the full license. Verbatim: *"a
period of not more than 120 days while the department is completing its investigation of the
applicant for the license."* The permit is canceled immediately if the application turns out to
be fraudulent, incorrect, or incomplete, and becomes invalid once the real license is issued or
refused.
— Veh. Code § 11803 (text confirmed directly, verbatim). **Grade: High.**

**Practical sequence:** ask your dealer/GM to submit your application and get you a temporary
permit issued *before* you start negotiating any deal paperwork — the permit is what makes you
lawful on day one, not an assumption that DMV will look the other way while paperwork is
pending.

**What the application requires:**
- Application for Salesperson's License, **Form OL 16S**
- Live Scan fingerprinting (**Form DMV 8016**)
- A DMV-run background check
- No exam is required.
— DMV Vehicle Salesperson License page and OL 16S form (fetched directly). **Grade: High.**

**Cost:** $50 non-refundable application fee + $1 mandatory Family Support Program fee = **$51
total**. Reinstatement $50, duplicate/transfer $15.
— Same DMV page (fetched directly). **Grade: High.**

**What can disqualify you.** DMV may refuse, suspend, or revoke the license for reasons
including: an unsatisfied court judgment tied to licensed activity; failing to turn over funds
or a vehicle owed to your employing dealer; a prior out-of-state occupational license that was
revoked/suspended for cause; working as a salesperson for two unrelated dealers at once; or being
a manager during a subordinate's misconduct that got the dealer's license suspended.
— Veh. Code § 11806 (text confirmed directly). **Grade: High** for these listed grounds.
Separately, general California occupational-licensing law (Bus. & Prof. Code § 480/§ 490) lets
licensing boards deny an application over a conviction substantially related to the license's
duties — this pattern is standard across CA occupational licenses, but I did not pull that
section's text verbatim against leginfo. **Grade: Moderate.** If you have anything in your
history involving fraud or dishonesty, raise it with DMV's Occupational Licensing branch before
applying, not after.

**Bottom line:** apply immediately (OL 16S + Live Scan), ask your dealer to push for the
temporary permit, and don't negotiate a single deal until you're holding either the permit or
the license.

### 1.2 Everything else to know in month one

| Item | What it means for you on day one | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Dealer reserve / finance-charge markup cap** — you cannot mark up a loan's rate beyond an APR spread of **2.5%** (contracts ≤ 60 months) or **2.0%** (contracts > 60 months) over the buy rate. This is a hard numeric ceiling, not guidance. | Know this cap cold — it directly limits how much you can move a customer's rate before you're violating state law, separate from any federal fair-lending exposure on *how* you apply markup within that cap. | High |
| **GAP waiver rules** — price capped at **4% of amount financed**; cannot be a condition of credit; buyer can cancel anytime with no cancellation fee; full refund within 30 days, pro-rata after; violations can trigger **treble damages** against the holder. | This is the product you'll sell constantly. Know the 4% cap and refund mechanics before you quote GAP pricing. | High |
| **Contract itemization** — the conditional sale contract must itemize cash price, taxes, doc fees, every add-on product, trade-in value, rebates, finance charge, and amount financed on its face; add-ons additionally require a separate pre-signing written disclosure showing price and payment impact for each one. | This is the legal basis for your itemized menu presentation — it's not optional formatting, it's statutory. | High |
| **Used-vehicle cancellation option (current rule, expires soon — see § 3 below)** — dealers must *offer* a purchasable cancellation option on used vehicles under $40,000; it is not free and not automatic. | Know that this whole framework is being replaced October 1, 2026 (see next section) — don't get trained on a script that's about to be obsolete. | High |

### Pending change you will be trained under within your first 8 weeks: the CARS Act (SB 766)

**Confirmed directly against the enrolled bill text** — operative **October 1, 2026**.

Effective that date, the current paid/opt-in 2-day cancellation option (Veh. Code § 11713.21) is
repealed. It's replaced by a new mandatory, **free** right to cancel under new Civil Code Title
1.5B:

| New rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Right to cancel | 3 calendar days from contract execution (extends to next business day if day 3 falls on a dealership closure) |
| Applies to | Used vehicles sold at retail, **$50,000 or less** |
| Mileage limit | Vehicle must not exceed **400 miles** driven between execution and cancellation |
| Restocking fee | 1.5% of price ($200 min / $600 max), or actual shipping cost if charged, plus $1/mile over 250 miles (capped at $150) |
| Add-on disclosure | Must disclose in writing that add-ons are "not required" and the vehicle can be bought without them (Civ. Code § 1784.42) |
| Price disclosure | Total price must be shown in all ads and the first written communication; monthly-payment comparisons must note that lower payments "often increase the total amount" (Civ. Code §§ 1784.40, 1784.41) |

— SB 766 (2025–2026 Reg. Session), Chapter 354, Statutes of 2025, signed 10/7/2025:
[leginfo bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB766).
**Grade: High** — confirmed directly against the enrolled bill text, including section numbers.

**This is the single most consequential thing in this document for your first months on the
job.** Ask your dealership specifically whether their cancellation-option scripts, menu
paperwork, and advertised-price templates have been updated for October 1, 2026 — if you're
trained on the current § 11713.21 script without a plan to switch, you'll be running an obsolete
process within roughly eight weeks of a typical start date.

## 3. What gets dealers prosecuted here — real enforcement patterns

| Case | Authority | Date | What happened | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **People v. Paul Blanco's Good Car Company** (Blanco Family Dealerships) | California Attorney General (Bonta) | Settlement announced Nov. 7, 2022 | Deceptive advertising targeting low-income and senior buyers; falsifying credit applications and vehicle values submitted to lenders (~20,000 instances); deceiving customers into add-on/service-contract purchases by concealing charges or falsely claiming they were mandatory | $27.5M judgment; permanent ban on the corporate entities from CA auto sales; 10-year personal ban for Paul Blanco; admitted 670,000 UCL/False Advertising Law violations |
| **AutoNation California dealerships** | Multi-county District Attorneys (LA County lead, joined by Santa Clara, SF, Sonoma, Ventura, Riverside) — not the AG or DMV | Feb. 2025 | Failure to transfer used-vehicle title/registration within the 30-day statutory deadline, thousands of instances since 2019 | $650,000 (civil penalties + cost recovery + consumer-protection fund); injunctive compliance program |

**The pattern that actually gets prosecuted in California: concealed or falsely-mandatory add-on
charges, falsified paperwork sent to lenders, and deceptive advertising aimed at vulnerable
buyers** — not honest mistakes on a form. The Paul Blanco case is the closest primary-sourced
match to F&I-desk conduct specifically (concealed charges, false "mandatory" framing on
add-ons, falsified credit applications).

**Gap, stated honestly:** a targeted search did not turn up a DMV administrative license action
(as opposed to an AG or DA case) specifically for F&I/GAP misconduct in 2021–2026. That's a
"not found in this pass," not "doesn't exist" — DMV's Occupational Licensing decision database
wasn't directly searchable here. **Grade: Not covered.**

## What was discarded (7 items) and why

1. **"OL 29B is the salesperson personal-history form"** — DMV's own page and forms point to
   **OL 16S**, not OL 29B (which applies to a different license type). Discarded as wrong.
2. **"Willful violation of Rees-Levering is a misdemeanor"** — appeared in a law review article
   and blog summaries; could not locate the specific Civil Code section imposing criminal
   liability. Treat violations as civil-remedy exposure (unenforceability, damages) unless this
   is independently confirmed.
3. **"Credit score disclosure" as a Car Buyer's Bill of Rights item** — traces to federal
   Dodd-Frank risk-based-pricing/FCRA law, not a distinct AB 68 provision. Discarded as
   mis-attributed to California.
4. **"DFPI directly caps or enforces dealer reserve"** — no DFPI regulation found; the actual
   cap is a Legislature-enacted Civil Code section (§ 2982.10), enforced through contract
   unenforceability/private right of action, not a DFPI rule. Discarded.
5. **"Civil Code § 1794.41" as a GAP citation** — appeared on a vendor site; that section number
   does not correspond to GAP content in the actual code. Discarded as mis-cited (correct
   citation is § 2982.12).
6. **Sage Auto Group "yo-yo financing/packing" case as a California enforcement precedent** —
   this is an FTC (federal) matter, not a California AG/DMV action. Discarded from the CA
   enforcement list; it's real, just not a CA citation.
7. **A specific Song-Beverly (Civil Code §1790s) clause "requiring" particular VSC-vs-warranty
   disclosure language** — not found in the accessible statutory text. The actual disclosure
   duties trace to Civ. Code § 1794.4 and Insurance Code provisions instead. Discarded as
   overstated.
