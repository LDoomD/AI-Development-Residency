## 2. Public speaking vs. one-on-one persuasion — real evidence, not assumption

**Finding: these are separate skill/anxiety domains, and formal public-speaking training doesn't have evidence of transferring to one-on-one sales performance.**

- McCroskey's Personal Report of Communication Apprehension (PRCA-24) — the field-standard instrument, decades of psychometric validation — measures communication anxiety across **four distinct but correlated contexts**: public speaking, dyadic/interpersonal, small group, meetings. Someone can be calm on stage and anxious one-on-one, or the reverse. **Grade: High** (peer-reviewed).
- Communication-studies literature treats public speaking (one-way, low-feedback) and interpersonal/dyadic communication (reciprocal, high-feedback, empathy/listening-driven) as different competency domains. **Grade: Moderate.**
- **No study found showing Toastmasters-style training improves one-on-one sales/negotiation outcomes.** Only vendor marketing material exists on this claim — a genuine literature gap, and the apprehension research actually argues against assuming transfer.

**Practical conclusion:** skip formal public-speaking training as a primary investment for this job. F&I is fundamentally a seated, one-on-one, high-stakes conversation — a different skill domain from stage presentation. Time is better spent on:
- **Adaptive selling** — reading and adjusting to *this specific* customer. High grade, Franke & Park 2006 meta-analysis (31,000 salespeople) — predicts objective sales performance.
- Deliberate rehearsal of the actual menu pitch using the behavior-modeling method already locked into `2026-08-09_f-and-i-presentation-technique.md` (mixed good/bad examples, trainee-written scenarios, named rule codes — Taylor et al. 2005, 117-study meta-analysis).

Public-speaking training is not harmful, but only relevant as a supplement if *speaking anxiety itself* (not conversational skill) turns out to be a blocker — not assumed to be one by default.
