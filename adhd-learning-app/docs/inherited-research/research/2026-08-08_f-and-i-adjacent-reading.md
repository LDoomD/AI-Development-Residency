# Adjacent reading — the non-F&I books that make an F&I manager good

Companion to `2026-08-08_f-and-i-reading-list.md` (trust/persuasion/charisma — Dreeke, Voss,
Schafer, Cialdini, Van Edwards, Cabane). This file does not duplicate that one. Seven gaps,
verified against a real publisher/retailer/library listing (author, year, ISBN-13). Anything
that didn't clear verification was discarded, not caveated — **9 candidates discarded total**
across all seven areas (counts per area below).

Grades: **High** — rely on as-is / **Moderate** — directional / **Extrapolated** — tune by
feel / **Not covered**.

## 1. Behavioral economics of money decisions under pressure

| Book | Grade | Why this job | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Predictably Irrational* (Rev. & Exp. Ed.), Dan Ariely — HarperCollins, 2010, ISBN-13 9780061353246 | Moderate — anchoring/decoy effects are solid; Ariely's *own* headline numbers carry extra risk (a 2012 dishonesty-study data-fabrication finding, unrelated to this book but worth knowing) — use the mechanisms, not his stats, as gospel | The F&I menu is a decoy machine: three warranty tiers where the middle one is built to look reasonable next to an inflated "everything" package, presented right after the customer already anchored on a payment above what they planned | 1. Free: none full-text, but danariely.com essays/talks. 2. Libby/OverDrive: confirmed (overdrive.com/media/158552). 3. | *Nudge: The Final Edition*, Thaler & Sunstein — Penguin, 2021, ISBN-13 9780143137009 | High for the default-effect mechanism itself (field data — 401(k) auto-enrollment, organ-donation opt-in/out — not lab priming); Moderate when transferred to a one-off high-stakes purchase like a car, since most Nudge evidence is repeated low-stakes choices | Menu selling *is* choice architecture — what's pre-checked, what's presented first, opt-in vs. opt-out framing for GAP — directly relevant to both effectiveness and disclosure/consent compliance | 1. Free: Thaler & Sunstein's "Choice Architecture" paper — free PDF, papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1583509. 2. Libby/OverDrive: confirmed (overdrive.com/media/6267493). 3. Academic: check COS catalog. 4. Purchase: $20.00 paperback, B&N confirmed. |

**Skip:** *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Kahneman) — rigorous but ~500 pages with few sales-floor-transferable examples, and its own headline priming chapter ("The Associative Machine") is one of the most publicized replication failures in psychology (Kahneman flagged this himself in 2012). Wrong format for someone starting in days. *Misbehaving* (Thaler) — intellectual memoir, lower practitioner yield than *Nudge*.

**Fastest path:** read the free SSRN "Choice Architecture" paper (20 min), then *Predictably Irrational* via Libby for the anchoring/decoy chapters.

**Discarded: 0.**

## 3. Money shame and financial anxiety

**No trade-published book exists specifically on "money shame" with Kahneman-level rigor.** What's credible comes from the applied financial-psychology field founded by Brad Klontz (clinical psychologist + CFP), built on a real peer-reviewed instrument (the Klontz Money Script Inventory, *Journal of Financial Therapy*, 2011, replicated in later JFT work).

| Book | Grade | Why this job | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Facilitating Financial Health* (2nd ed.), Brad Klontz, Rick Kahler, Ted Klontz — 2016, ISBN-13 9781941627877 | Moderate — the "money scripts" construct (shame-driven avoidance/secrecy/defensiveness) is High-grade via the KMSI research; the book's specific client-conversation scripts are practitioner craft, not RCT-tested | Written for professionals sitting across from a client managing money shame — structurally the same seat an F&I manager occupies. Gives language for de-escalating a customer who flinches at "let's pull your credit" or gets evasive about a past repo, instead of triggering more shame | 1. Free: none. 2. Libby: confirmed (NYPL OverDrive, media/3804214). 3. Academic: COS carries EBSCO Academic Search Complete; this title not individually confirmed — try ILL. 4. Purchase: check Kindle price directly — one marketplace paperback listing showed an outlier $306, don't pay reseller markup. |
| *Mind Over Money*, Brad Klontz & Ted Klontz — Crown/Broadway, 2009, ISBN-13 9780385531016 (hardcover) / 9780385531030 (ebook) | Moderate — same research base as above, consumer-facing/anecdote-heavy version | Shorter, faster read for someone starting imminently; names "money avoidance" and "financial denial" as patterns — vocabulary for recognizing a customer minimizing their own credit history before the pull | 1. Free: none. 2. Libby: confirmed, ebook + audiobook (overdrive.com/media/251778). 3. Academic: not needed, Libby covers it. 4. Purchase: hardcover only, $68–71 at ThriftBooks — Libby is the better route. |

**Adjacent, not on-target:** *Scarcity* (Mullainathan & Shafir, 2013, ISBN-13 9780805092646) — High-grade academic evidence (Harvard economist + Princeton psychologist) on cognitive load under scarcity, but it's about bandwidth/decision quality under scarcity broadly, not shame specifically. Worth a later read, doesn't answer this brief on its own.

**Skip:** *Secrets of the Millionaire Mind* (Eker), *You Are a Badass at Making Money* (Sincero) — "money mindset" self-help, no evidence base. *The Psychology of Money* (Housel) — real and good, but it's investing/wealth-building behavior, commonly mis-slotted into "money psychology" searches; not about shame.

**Discarded: 2** (an unverifiable "financial trauma" title recalled from memory with no matching ISBN; Housel's book, on relevance grounds after review, not verification failure — kept out of the count-as-recommendation but confirmed real).

## 4. Negotiation beyond Voss

| Book | Grade | Why this job | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Getting to Yes* (3rd ed.), Fisher, Ury, Patton — Penguin, 2011, ISBN-13 9780143118756 | High — the four-part framework (people/problem, interests not positions, options for mutual gain, objective criteria) plus BATNA is the foundational, still-taught model behind modern negotiation pedagogy | "I'll walk unless the payment drops $30/month" is a *position*. The method forces you to find the *interest* underneath — hard budget ceiling? competitor quote? trade-in resentment? — then invent options (term, down-payment sequencing, product rebundling) that satisfy it without just cutting margin. "Objective criteria" also gives language to defend pricing off fee schedules and lender buy-rates instead of "because I said so" | 1. Free: Harvard PON publishes the core framework free (pon.harvard.edu, confirmed live, no paywall). 2. Libby: very likely available (perennial top-checkout title) — verify in-app. 3. Academic: COS Library has a databases portal; specific holding unconfirmed — worth a librarian check (559-737-6179) or ILL. 4. Purchase: $21.00 paperback, confirmed at Penguin Random House. |
| *Bargaining for Advantage* (2nd ed.), G. Richard Shell — Penguin, 2006, ISBN-13 9780143036975 | Moderate — the "six foundations" structure is Wharton-course-tested; the self-assessment "bargaining style" quiz is self-report, treat as a starting hypothesis | Fisher/Ury doesn't tell you how to read and adapt to *who's in the chair* — a silent conflict-avoidant buyer vs. a loud one claiming a lower competitor quote. Shell's "personal style" and "authoritative standards" foundations cover exactly that, and go further on wielding published rate sheets against a markup accusation | 1. Free: no standalone equivalent to PON found. 2. Libby: plausible, not directly confirmed — check in-app. 3. Academic: same COS path, unconfirmed for this title. 4. Purchase: $20.00 paperback, confirmed at Penguin Random House. |

**Read order:** *Getting to Yes* first (shorter, single framework, free PON stopgap available same-day); *Bargaining for Advantage* as the follow-on once he's tested his own style against a few live deals.

**Skip:** *You Can Negotiate Anything* (Cohen) — dated, largely superseded. *Start with No* (Camp) — explicitly adversarial, conflicts with a repeat-customer/CSI-driven business. *Trump: The Art of the Deal* — ghostwritten memoir, not instructional. Low-effort "hostage negotiation for sales" repackagings of Voss — redundant with the companion list.

**Discarded: 0.**

## Read-after-the-core-five order

The "core five" (Dreeke/Voss/Schafer/Cialdini/Van Edwards-or-Cabane) come from the companion
list. This is what comes next, ranked:

1. **Getting to Yes** (Fisher, Ury, Patton) — shortest, single framework, free PON stopgap exists; extends tactical empathy into a structure for the actual give-and-take.
2. **How to Lie with Statistics** (Huff) — 144 pages, $13.95, immediately usable the first time a number gets waved at him in a meeting.
3. **Predictably Irrational** (Ariely) — explains the mechanism behind the tool he'll use every shift (the menu).
4. **Self-Compassion** (Neff) — the fastest-acting reset between deals; read before week two, not after burnout starts.
5. **Selling With Noble Purpose** (McLeod) — names the tension he'll feel by week one and gives him a test for it.
6. **Facilitating Financial Health** or **Mind Over Money** (Klontz) — pick the shorter one (*Mind Over Money*) first if time is tight; upgrade to the practitioner edition later.
7. **Nudge** (Thaler & Sunstein) — deepens #3 once the menu mechanics are familiar from daily reps.
8. **Taking Charge of Adult ADHD** (Barkley) — read alongside #4, not instead of it; this is the "why," Neff is the daily tool.
9. **Bargaining for Advantage** (Shell) — once he's felt his own bargaining style in a few live deals, this sharpens it.
10. **To Sell Is Human** (Pink) — grounds #5 in evidence once the noble-purpose framing is second nature.
11. **Intelligent Credit Scoring** (Siddiqi) — technical, back-office knowledge; useful but not urgent for the floor.
12. **The Data Detective** (Harford) — broader follow-on to Huff, longer, lower urgency.
13. **Bad Paper** (Halpern) — context/ethics reading, not a floor skill; read when there's slack, not in week one.
