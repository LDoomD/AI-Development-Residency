## Part 1 — What each brief concludes (one paragraph each)

**`2026-08-09_dyslexia-cognitive-science.md`** — Adult dyslexia is a persistent, lifelong word-level decoding and orthographic-processing difference (not something readers "grow out of"), and for this user it compounds with ADHD-driven working-memory deficits rather than merely coexisting alongside them. The brief's most load-bearing finding for a content-authoring system: Orton-Gillingham and other "structured literacy" methods have no evidence base for adults who already read and are learning new professional content — their best-verified meta-analysis found *non-significant* effects even in children, their intended population — so the build should not borrow that brand or its authority. What generalizes instead are the underlying mechanisms: morphology-first vocabulary introduction, meaning-unit chunking, sentence-complexity caps, and inline glossing, all authored into content itself rather than into CSS. Text-to-speech has a real but modest effect (d≈0.35) and should be framed honestly as one accommodation among several, not a headline fix — and the existing "explain it back" retrieval step already does double duty, since fluent-sounding reading and actual comprehension are dissociable in dyslexic adults.

**`2026-08-09_10x-learning-claims-audit.md`** — No rigorously validated technique produces a literal 10x improvement in learning (encoding + durable retention + transfer) for any adult learner, and every commercial claim in that space (speed reading/RSVP, superlearning, photographic-memory training, brain-training apps, 2025–2026 "AI learn 10x faster" products) is either directly debunked by controlled research or unsupported hype riding on real adjacent findings. What the project's already-adopted techniques (retrieval practice, spaced repetition, worked examples, desirable difficulties) actually deliver is three separable effects: roughly flat-to-modest gains on first-pass encoding speed, meaningfully fewer repetitions needed to reach a fixed fluency criterion, and the largest and most robust effect — substantially better retention at 30+ days for the same total study time, not a time-compression multiplier. The brief's central recommendation is to retire "10x faster" as a literal target and reframe the system's promise around eliminating wasted repetition and improving delayed retention, since any felt "10x" the user experiences will come from replacing near-zero-efficiency study habits (cramming, re-reading) with real ones — not from compressing cognition itself.

**`2026-08-09_ai-tutor-landscape-2026.md`** — The 2025–2026 AI-learning-tool market splits sharply into vendor-marketed "AI tutor" platforms (Socra, Squirrel AI, Century Tech, Riiid, Cognii, etc.) with essentially no independent efficacy evidence, versus a genuinely useful open-source category of LLM-augmented flashcard/SRS tooling that extends — rather than replaces — the project's existing Obsidian+Anki stack. The one clear, low-risk, high-value adoption is an LLM-assisted Markdown-to-Anki card-generation script (`md2anki`-style) with a mandatory human review pass to catch recognition-not-recall cards. True Recall (an Obsidian-native FSRS v6 plugin) is well-fit technically but running it means maintaining a second, parallel SRS/review-queue system alongside the locked Anki stack — the brief surfaces this as an explicit yes/no decision for the user, not a routine trial. AI essay/explanation graders carry a documented length/complexity bias that makes them actively risky as judges of the project's own self-explanation exercises; closed "AI tutor" platforms and voice-language tutors are out of scope or not composable.

**`2026-08-09_agentic-tutoring-workflow-design.md`** — Running the existing 5-phase loop as a persistent, multi-session agent system doesn't need a heavyweight multi-agent swarm; it needs a single primary tutor/orchestrator agent plus two narrow specialist roles (an independent examiner-verifier with no access to session rapport, and a separate gated content/deck-authoring pass) — because the loop is inherently sequential, and decomposition should track genuine need for an adversarial second opinion or genuinely independent subtasks, not be the default. The single biggest orchestration risk over dozens of sessions isn't one bad model call, it's silent architectural drift — session 47 quietly reintroducing a streak counter or a percentage load bar because each session reconstructs context from scratch rather than reading a full locked rules file. The mitigations are boring and mechanical: a consolidated, always-re-read "never" list; deterministic hooks (not prompted discipline) for mechanically-checkable rules like tracker status transitions; production-based (not recognition-based) gate criteria, which the rubric already leans on; and treating LLM-judge style/length bias as real and well-evidenced while treating "rapport erodes grading rigor over many sessions" as a plausible but untested extrapolation. One verified-effective adherence mechanism (de-identified team competition) has no home in a solo-learner architecture and is flagged as an open design question, not solved here.

**`2026-08-09_oss-vs-paid-stack-synthesis.md`** — A spot-check (not a full verified pass) of the AI-tutor tooling layer sitting on top of the locked Obsidian+Anki stack finds the orchestration frameworks (LangChain, LlamaIndex) and local inference (Ollama) are genuinely open source and self-hostable, but the highest-quality tutoring behavior — Socratic questioning, error diagnosis — still comes from a paid proprietary API (Claude), and unlike Obsidian's proprietary-core-neutralized-by-plain-files situation, there is no equivalent portable file format for "the tutor's reasoning." Total cash cost either way is trivial for a solo user (~$0–45/mo across all paths, illustrative), far below what people already pay for the unproven ADHD-productivity-app category. The recommendation is a hybrid: keep Obsidian+Anki, build the orchestration layer as a thin provider-agnostic wrapper from day one (the single decision that actually determines lock-in, not the vendor), use the paid Claude API specifically for the Examiner (the one piece already rated High and tied to the non-negotiable adaptivity floor), and route bulk/low-stakes generation through a local model. It also flags an unresolved structural question: this tooling doesn't clearly map onto any of the build plan's three locked layers (universal / ADHD supports / subject-type router), and that should be reconciled before merging.

**`2026-08-09_book-synthesis-audit.md`** — None of the six popular-science books audited (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*, *The Power of Habit*, *Atomic Habits*, *Make It Stick*, *Peak*, *Deep Work*) should be cited as evidence in the build plan, and three of them (Kahneman, Duhigg, Ericsson) built their flagship anecdotes on material the original researchers or subsequent meta-analyses have since walked back or substantially revised — Kahneman's own 2017 comment disowning the Ch. 4 social-priming studies, Ericsson explicitly disavowing the "10,000-hour rule" Gladwell popularized from his data, and Duhigg's "keystone habits" resting on zero controlled evidence. *Make It Stick* is the one safe-to-consume popularization because it's simply an accessible index into work this project already cites (Roediger, Bjork, Karpicke). The genuinely useful yield is three primary sources surfaced *through* the books but not yet in this project's cited corpus — Gollwitzer & Sheeran's (2006) implementation-intentions meta-analysis (d≈0.65), Lally et al.'s (2010) habit-automaticity timeline (median 66 days, actual range 18–254, missed days don't reset progress), and Wood & Neal's (2007) habit-cue taxonomy (context-stability beats reward-salience) — all three should be cited directly instead of the books that popularized them, and used to design the STUDY-session trigger as an if-then habit stack implemented purely as dashboard/environmental state, never a push notification. The brief also surfaces one direct design conflict worth flagging rather than adopting: Newport's *Deep Work* prescribes long willpower-driven undistracted blocks, which is in tension with this project's already-cited ADHD attention-regulation literature (Sibley et al. 2026; Alderson et al. 2013) favoring shorter, externally-structured, scaffolded sessions — the brief resolves this in favor of the existing ADHD sources, keeping Newport only as a vocabulary source for user-facing copy.

## Part 2 — Where the new findings REINFORCE locked build-plan decisions

1. **The three-layer architecture and adaptivity floor (`build-plan.md` "The reframe" + "The floor") are reinforced twice over.** The dyslexia brief's shared-working-memory-bottleneck finding (§5) gives an independent, dyslexia-side reason to keep units small and to protect struggling learners — the same mechanism the floor already protects against for ADHD, now doing double duty. The AI-tutor-landscape brief independently rejects Squirrel AI/Century Tech/Riiid specifically because their black-box adaptive sequencing offers no visible floor guarantee, which is the adaptivity-floor principle showing up as a rejection criterion for third-party tools, not just an internal design rule.

2. **"Explain it back" / retrieval-practice as the actual comprehension check is reinforced, not just repeated.** The dyslexia brief (§4) adds a second, independent justification beyond ADHD retrieval-practice needs: fluent-sounding reading and comprehension are dissociable in dyslexic adults (PMC9125151), so a fluency-only check would miss exactly the failure mode this user is most likely to hit. Keep it, and the design doc's rationale should say so explicitly (see Part 3, item 3).

3. **The "Examiner first, only piece rated High" build-order decision (`build-plan.md`, build order step 3) is reinforced by both the agentic-workflow brief and the oss-vs-paid brief.** The agentic-workflow brief treats the examiner's grading integrity as the single highest-leverage reliability problem in the whole runtime; the oss-vs-paid brief independently arrives at "use the paid Claude API specifically for the Examiner" because that's where model quality has the most leverage relative to the floor requirement. Two independent briefs converge on the Examiner as the correct first build target and the correct place to spend on model quality.

4. **No gamification / no notification-based adherence (locked, implicit in the design doc's "never" items) is reinforced.** The agentic-workflow brief re-confirms Nordby et al. 2022 (reminders don't move completion in ADHD adults specifically) and explicitly calls out "do not let 'adherence is dropping' become a rationale to quietly relax the no-notification decision" as a named failure mode to guard against — this is a warning worth keeping visible, not just a fact already known.

5. **The retrieval-practice + spaced-repetition foundation (`build-plan.md` "Universal layer") is reinforced, but with its claimed magnitude corrected downward.** The 10x-claims-audit brief confirms retrieval practice and spacing remain the two most robust, defensible mechanisms in the entire literature — nothing here threatens the choice to build on them. It does, however, retract informal effect-size language ("d=0.88," doubling, specific percentage-fewer-repetitions figures) that may still be floating in project docs as more precise than it actually is; `build-plan.md` decision item 1 already flags dropping `d=0.88` for a different reason (citation correction to g=0.65) — this brief adds that any *other* precise multiplier language anywhere in the docs should get the same scrutiny.

6. **Plain-file, no-vendor-lock-in philosophy (`build-plan.md`'s Obsidian/Anki/NotebookLM sections) is reinforced and extended one layer up.** The oss-vs-paid brief shows the same "exit cost is a function of how the code is written, not which vendor you pick" logic applies to the orchestration layer that will sit on top of Obsidian+Anki — consistent with, not a new principle relative to, the existing stack decision.

7. **The no-notification / no-reminder-based-adherence decision is reinforced a second, independent way by the book-synthesis-audit brief.** It doesn't just re-confirm the decision (the agentic-workflow brief already did that, Part 2 item 4) — it stress-tests it against a concrete UX temptation (implementation-intention "if-then" cues, and Lally's "you haven't lost progress" reassurance after a missed day) and explicitly concludes both should be implemented as dashboard/environmental state, never a push notification, rather than treating the no-notification rule as blocking those mechanisms outright. That's a useful precedent for how future asks should be resolved against this rule.

8. **The ADHD-specific, externally-structured short-session architecture (`adhd-learning-system-design.md`'s 15-minute micro-sprint / already-cited Sibley et al. 2026, Alderson et al. 2013) is reinforced by an explicit rejection of the competing model.** The book-synthesis-audit brief tested this project's session-length choice against Newport's *Deep Work* prescription (long willpower-driven undistracted blocks) and found it in direct conflict with the ADHD literature already cited — and resolved it in favor of what's already built, not in favor of the popular book. This is reinforcement via survived stress-test, not just repetition.

## Part 4 — Honest answer to the "10x faster" goal

**The literal claim does not survive.** No technique in the peer-reviewed literature — including everything already adopted in this system's design — produces a genuine 10x speedup in learning (encoding new material, retaining it, and being able to use it later) for an adult learner. That is true for a neurotypical learner and true for this user specifically; ADHD and dyslexia don't unlock some steeper technique that erases the ceiling everyone else studying this question has run into. Every commercial product currently marketing "10x," "learn anything in 20 hours" applied to mastery rather than basic competence, or "AI-optimized learning states" is either directly contradicted by controlled research or repackaging the same two real mechanisms (spaced repetition, retrieval practice) this project already uses, without adding anything beyond a nicer UI.

**What is real, and where it actually shows up:**
- **First-pass encoding speed** — the speed of understanding something for the first time — barely moves, and desirable-difficulty techniques (which this project deliberately uses) sometimes make the first pass feel *slower*, on purpose, because that friction is where the durability comes from.
- **Repetitions needed to reach a fixed skill level** — this moves meaningfully. Retrieval practice and spacing measurably cut the number of review reps needed to hit and hold a mastery bar, compared to re-reading or massed practice. This is a real, replicated, moderate effect — not a 10x one, and the specific multiplier depends on material and shouldn't be quoted as a fixed number without a primary source.
- **Retention over weeks and months at the same total study time** — this is the biggest, most defensible effect in the whole literature, and it's the honest home of anything that could be called "Nx" in this system. At equal hours invested, spaced retrieval-based study produces substantially better recall a month later than the way most people actually study.

**Where a felt "10x" can legitimately come from:** not from compressing cognition, but from replacing a near-zero-efficiency baseline. Most people study by cramming, re-reading, highlighting, with no spacing and no retrieval and no correction when comprehension silently fails. Going from that to a well-implemented system with retrieval practice, spacing, worked examples, an integration gate, and an honest mastery check can *feel* like an order-of-magnitude change in the user's lived experience — because the starting point was close to zero, not because the underlying cognitive process sped up tenfold. That reframe is defensible, still genuinely dramatic relative to how most people actually study, and — critically — it doesn't set the system up to be judged a failure against a benchmark no known technique clears.

**The evidence-based path to "as fast as it can honestly get":** build the three things this system already has right (retrieval practice, spacing, worked examples for procedural content) with rigor — a real mastery gate that doesn't grade leniently, a review queue that doesn't punish the learner for a missed day, content authored to reduce decoding and working-memory load specifically for this reader — and then measure the two honest numbers directly: repetitions-to-criterion and 30-day retention, against this user's own prior study-habit baseline, not against a fixed multiplier. That is the whole recommendation in one sentence, and it's the same one the 10x-claims-audit brief lands on independently.
