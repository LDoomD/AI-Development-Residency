# Book extraction — The Like Switch (Jack Schafer, PhD, with Marvin Karlins, PhD)

**Authors:** Jack Schafer — 20 years FBI Special Agent, behavioral analyst in the Behavioral Analysis Program (Dreeke's program), later PhD in psychology and university professor; with Marvin Karlins, academic psychologist.
**Edition used:** Touchstone, 2015. ISBN 9781476754482.
**Original mode:** nonverbal rapport cues for F&I (per `specs/research/2026-08-08_f-and-i-reading-list.md`, ranked #4, graded Moderate — Karlins is an actual academic psychologist; some cues draw on real social-psych literature, not systematically cited).
Extracted per the pipeline in `specs/reference-library-workflow.md`, 2026-08-13. Full read of intro + chapters 1–7; chapter 8 (digital-world relationships — catfishing caution, online footprint hygiene) read in part and deliberately not mined: it is a consumer-safety chapter with no technique that maps to any current subject.

## Author's stated purpose

From the introduction, near-verbatim: the same social skills Schafer developed "to befriend and recruit spies are equally effective in developing successful friendships at home, at work, or anywhere else that personal interactions take place." His three conditions for the book working, stated explicitly: (1) learn the techniques and *let the tools do the work* (his circular-saw analogy — don't force it); (2) actually use them — "knowledge without action is knowledge wasted"; (3) practice constantly, and push through the early "free fall" phase where new skills feel awkward and fail. He names the goal metric the "LQ — Likability Quotient."

**Author's own ethical frame:** he is candid that these techniques were built for manipulation-adjacent work (recruiting spies, obtaining confessions — he describes deliberately simulating friend signals at a suspect he found "reprehensible"). He does not include a warning section the way Dreeke does; the compliance/ethics line for this project has to be drawn by us, not quoted from him. Noted honestly.

## Framework — the underlying mental model

Two load-bearing ideas, everything else hangs off them:

1. **The Friendship Formula: Friendship = Proximity + Frequency + Duration + Intensity.** All relationships — one-night rapport to lifelong bonds — are governed by these four dials. You can consciously regulate them to build, evaluate, or gracefully exit a relationship. The brain doesn't flag this as manipulation because it mirrors how relationships form naturally (his "human baseline" concept — techniques work precisely because they fall inside normal behavior and pass the brain's "territory scan" unnoticed).
2. **The Golden Rule of Friendship: "If you want people to like you, make them feel good about themselves."** Identical to Dreeke's throughline ("it's not how you make people feel about you…") — unsurprising, same FBI program. The chief obstacle is your own ego wanting the spotlight.

Supporting frame: the **friend-foe continuum** — every stranger's brain is continuously scanning for friend/neutral/foe signals; your nonverbals get processed before you say a word.

## Claims to NOT repeat as fact

- **"70 percent of all information is transferred over food and drink"** (ch. 6) — unsourced; keep the eat-together-talk-more mechanic, drop the number.
- **The ~20-minute anger-reset window** (ch. 7) — physiologically flavored but uncited; "give them time" is right, the number is decoration.
- **The mating/self-esteem evolutionary passages** (ch. 4) and the **server-attractiveness tipping claims** (ch. 2) — dated pop-evolutionary and correlational material; nothing this project needs, don't port.
- The waiter-mirroring and touch-tipping studies are real research lines but cited loosely — same caution as Voss's version.

## Compliance note specific to F&I

This book is the most tactically manipulation-adjacent of the four extracted so far — the author openly frames techniques as working *because they're undetectable* ("passes the territory scan"). The project's line, applied to every flagged row above: **undetectable rapport-building in the customer's interest = fine; undetectable extraction or steering against the customer's interest = no, regardless of effectiveness.** Concretely: empathic statements, friend signals, face-saving, and anger-cycle work are all customer-serving. Elicitation-for-leverage, planted third-party flattery, and engineered-choice steering of product selection are the rows marked caution — same standard that excluded Belfort/Klaff.

## Cross-book convergence (four books in)

- **The Golden Rule of Friendship = Dreeke's core framework**, near-verbatim (same FBI Behavioral Analysis Program lineage — Schafer and Dreeke cite each other). Two books, one throughline: make them feel good about *themselves*.
- **Empathic statements ≈ Voss's labels ≈ Dreeke's validation** — third independent formulation of the same move. This is now the single best-attested technique in the library.
- **The consistency principle under Schafer's lip-purse tactic is Cialdini's commitment/consistency** (book #5, next in queue) — pre-empting objections before they're verbalized and Voss's "get 'That's right' before positions harden" are the same physics.
- **Alternate-choice / illusion of control = Voss's calibrated-question philosophy** in miniature ("the secret to gaining the upper hand is giving the other side the illusion of control" — Voss's words, Schafer's technique).
- New unique contributions of this book: the **Friendship Formula's four adjustable dials**, the **rapport-testing kit** (objective pre-close checks — nothing else in the library tests rapport, the others only build it), **lip-purse objection pre-emption**, and the **anger-cycle sequence** (the library's first structured de-escalation protocol).
