# F&I Reading List — Ranked Acquisition Guide

For Orlando, starting an F&I (Finance & Insurance) manager job imminently. Ranked most-useful-first. Every entry below was verified against a directly fetched publisher/retailer/library listing — nothing here is from memory. 3 candidates were discarded (see Discard Log).

## Is there a canonical F&I textbook? No.

There is no retail-available, ISBN-verifiable standard text for the F&I role itself. The actual job knowledge (menu structure, VSC/GAP/tire-and-wheel/prepaid-maintenance/credit-life-disability product mechanics, reserve, lender relationships, compliance) lives in:

- **AFIP** (Association of Finance and Insurance Professionals, afip.com) — the real industry certification body. Three tiers: Basic (CPFS), Senior (SPFS), Master. Compliance/ethics-focused. Pursue this directly rather than looking for a book equivalent.
- **NADA Academy** — National Automobile Dealers Association's training arm; $1,500–$2,500 in-person/live-online programs on menu presentation, TILA/RESPA disclosure, fair lending, documentation. Only useful if your dealer group sends you.
- **F&I and Showroom magazine** (fi-magazine.com, Bobit Business Media) — the standing trade press for this role.
- Dealership-proprietary training and private F&I course vendors (Reahard & Associates, Finance Manager Training, DealSpeak) — scripted courses, not books, and not evaluated here.

Everything below is **adjacent skill** — trust-building, persuasion, reading people — not F&I product or compliance knowledge. Treat it that way.

## Minimum viable list — if he only reads TWO things

1. **It's Not All About Me** (Dreeke) — the fastest, most directly applicable rapport skill for a stranger sitting across a desk from him under time pressure.
2. **Never Split the Difference** (Voss) — calibrated questions give him a non-confrontational way to handle price pushback, which is the single most common failure point in the box.

Everything else on this list sharpens those two skills; neither requires the others as a prerequisite.

## Skip these

- **Way of the Wolf (Jordan Belfort)** — a "Straight Line Persuasion" system built by a convicted securities fraudster, explicitly designed around manufactured urgency and frame control. In a regulated F&I environment (TILA, state UDAP statutes, FTC's 2024 CARS Rule targeting add-on misrepresentation), this playbook trains exactly the pressure and false-urgency tactics regulators are actively enforcing against. Skip entirely.
- **Pitch Anything (Oren Klaff)** — "frame control" mechanics lean on engineered time pressure and neediness-shaming, which sit close to the documented FTC/CFPB enforcement pattern around misrepresenting optional products as required or pressuring a fatigued buyer. Skip, or read only with a compliance officer's skepticism.
- **Classic hard-close car-lot sales scripts (era-specific "always be closing" material)** — as a category, these predate modern F&I compliance regulation and often teach the specific manipulative closes (fear-based urgency, refusal to take no) that current FTC/state AG actions cite by name. Skip the category; no specific title is recommended or was verifiable as a distinct book.
