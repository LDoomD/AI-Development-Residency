# Question bank

Pick the next question that fits the conversation. Ask one at a time. Skip any
question the user has already answered. Treat this as a list of ideas, not a
script. Follow the thread of what the user says.

## Phase 1: Explore

Good opening questions describe a real moment:

- What is the moment you find yourself wishing this existed?
- What is the idea, in your own words? Rough is fine.
- What made you think of it? Did something frustrate you?
- Walk me through the last time you ran into this problem.
- If this existed today, what would you use it for first?
- Is there anything similar out there? What would yours do differently?
- Why does this matter to you personally?

Make it concrete:

- Walk me through using this, step by step.
- You said it should do X. What does that look like on screen or in real
  life?
- Can you give me an example?

If the user has no idea yet:

- What problems do you run into often at work, at home, or in your hobbies?
- What are you good at that other people struggle with?
- What would you like to learn by building this?
- Offer two or three directions and ask which one feels most interesting.

## Challenge vague words

When the user says one of these, ask what it means for them:

- "Simple": simple for whom, and compared with what?
- "Fast": faster than what, and by how much?
- "Users": which person, in which situation?
- "Good" or "better": what would you notice that tells you?
- "Everyone": who would use it first?

## Phase 2: Define

### Problem

- What goes wrong today without this project?
- How do people handle the problem now?
- How painful is it: a small annoyance or a real cost in time or money?

### Users

- Describe one real person who would use this. What is their situation?
- What do they want to get done?
- How comfortable are they with technology?
- Is there a second type of user, such as an admin or a customer?

### Core value

- If this could only do one thing well, what would it be?
- If everything else broke but that one thing worked, would it still be
  useful?
- What would make you wince if it got this wrong?

### Goals

- If this works well in three months, what is different?
- How will you know? Can you put a number on it?
- What would make this a failure even if it technically works?
- Is this for learning, for a client, for a business, or something else?

### Features

- What are the three things this must do to be useful at all?
- What would be nice to have if there is time?
- What can clearly wait for a later version?
- Which single feature would you demo first?

### Out of scope

- What are you tempted to add that you should not build yet?
- What will this deliberately not do, and why?

### Constraints

- When does this need to be done, if there is a deadline?
- What skills and tools do you already have?
- What is your budget for time and money?
- Are there choices, like the platform or tools, that you want to make
  yourself? Which would you like me to suggest?
- Is there anything that must be used or avoided, such as a rule from a
  client or a course?

## Playing back

After each topic, say back what you heard in two or three lines. Mark
anything you guessed as a guess. End with: "What is wrong or missing?"

## Knowing when to stop

Offer to write the PRD once you know what the user wants, why they want it,
who it is for, and what done looks like. Do not keep asking just to fill the
template.
