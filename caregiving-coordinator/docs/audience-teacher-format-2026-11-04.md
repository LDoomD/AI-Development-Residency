# Audience goals and use cases, teacher format (2026-11-04)

Figma: section 2623:76 on the audience page (2378:7107). Built from 412 caregiver posts and threads: 303 Reddit posts (268 parent, 35 spouse) and 109 forum threads (55 parent, 54 spouse). Every list is most common first. Data and check scripts: `docs/sources/market-analysis-3/audience-v2/`.

## Audience 1: adult child caring for a parent

### Motivations

1. Keeping their parent safe and comfortable, and sparing them suffering: worry about falls, not eating, wounds, or a home that is no longer safe.
2. Doing the right thing and not carrying guilt or blame: duty to a parent, fear of not doing enough, being the one who has to step up.
3. Love, loyalty and promises: not wanting to abandon a parent, not ready to say goodbye, keeping a promise made years ago.
4. Letting the parent stay at home and keep making their own choices, even when it is risky.
5. Honoring the parent's wishes and making the time they have left count.
6. Taking the load off the other parent, who is the main caregiver.

### Situational goals

1. Understand what is happening and what to do next: is this dementia or a side effect, what is normal, how others handled it.
2. Get help, relief or services: respite, more in-home help, approval for long-term care, someone who can step in.
3. Get the parent's money, bills and legal papers under control: power of attorney, Medicaid, bills paid twice, whether the parent can still sign.
4. Keep the parent safe and know they are OK: alone at home, falls, scams, a new facility where no one speaks up for them.
5. Find, choose and pay for the right place: memory care that accepts Medicaid, moving a parent closer or across states.
6. Get medicines right: taken on time, refused, side effects, refills.
7. Get the parent to accept care and look after their own health.
8. Handle the parent's moods and behavior without a fight.

### Life goals

1. Keep their own health and sanity: sleep, stress, not falling apart.
2. Keep their own life, job and money: career, studies, savings, not losing their income.
3. Get time and breaks for themselves, without feeling guilty.
4. Protect their own marriage, children and family.
5. Not be alone in it, and be seen by others.
6. Have a life and a home of their own after caregiving.

## Audience 2: spouse or partner caring for a husband, wife or partner

### Motivations

1. Love, loyalty and their vows: staying by their partner's side and wanting to spend the rest of their life together.
2. Keeping their partner safe, comfortable and with dignity, and sparing them suffering.
3. Keeping their partner at home, with the friends and pleasures they still enjoy.
4. Doing right by their partner.

### Situational goals

1. Get help, relief or services: someone to stay with the partner, hospice, veterans' or other programs.
2. Understand what is happening and what to do: what to expect next, how others handle it.
3. Find, choose and pay for the right place: tour memory care, join waitlists, find a way to pay.
4. Keep the partner safe and know the care was done: walking off, driving, blood pressure, aides doing their job.
5. Make sense of money and insurance: keeping coverage and doctors, getting paid as a caregiver.
6. Keep medicines manageable: fewer pills, quality of life first.

### Life goals

1. Keep their own health and sanity: get through each day, cope, get help for their own anxiety and depression.
2. Keep their own life, job and money: keep working, have a life left afterward.
3. Companionship, closeness and someone who understands: they feel alone in the marriage.
4. Get time to themselves and real breaks.

## Limits

- One reader marked each post, and no person double-checked it. People who post online tend to have harder problems.
- Audience 2 has only 35 Reddit posts. 3 forum threads were filed under the wrong audience.
- The scenarios are our own wording of what caregivers want: open problems, not features or decisions.
- The box counts come from the earlier goals coding (269 Reddit parent posts, which includes one post that appears twice).

From the relief reading pass (`docs/market-analysis-segments-11-12-findings.md`). The table cells above are unchanged.

**Audience 1, use case 1 (adult children carry the care, many of them alone):**
- What blocks real time off: cost, the parent refusing, family not helping, eligibility rules for VA and Medicaid programs, rigid hours or minimum stays, and waitlists.
- Help they use: respite, paid aides, siblings or friends stepping in, adult day programs, therapy.
- 25% need a break and have no help in place (486 posts).

**Audience 2, use case 1 (spouses carry the care, many of them alone):**
- What blocks real time off: family not helping, eligibility rules (often the VA), cost, no reliable providers, and quality worries.
- Help they use: therapy and support groups more than adult children do, plus respite and paid aides. The VA is their most named provider.
- 30% need a break and have no help in place (98 posts, so these numbers are rough).

Neither audience uses burnout or meditation apps.


## CORRECTED 2026-11-05 — What stops caregivers from getting a break, under use case 1 (approved by the owner)

From 831 caregiver posts about getting a break (`docs/market-analysis-3.md`, "CORRECTED 2026-11-05" section). The table cells above are unchanged. **Respite** means a short break while someone else takes over the care.

**Audience 1, adult children caring for a parent (use case 1: carrying the care, many of them alone):**
- About 1 in 4 need a break and have no help in place.
- What stops them: the cost, the parent saying no, family who won't step in, paperwork and rules to qualify, and worry about the quality of care.
- Help they use: respite, paid aides, siblings or friends stepping in, adult day centers, therapy.
- Programs they name most: Medicaid, hospice, the VA.

**Audience 2, spouses or partners (use case 1: carrying the care, many of them alone):**
- About 1 in 4 need a break and have no help in place.
- What stops them: paperwork and rules (sometimes the VA), family who won't step in, the cost, not finding reliable helpers, and worry about the quality of care.
- Help they use: support groups more than adult children do, plus therapy, respite and paid aides.
- Program they name most: the VA.
- Based on 98 posts, so these are a rough guide.

Burnout and meditation apps almost never come up for either audience.
