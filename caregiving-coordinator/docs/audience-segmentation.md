# Audience segmentation definition (FigJam section)

Rewritten 2026-11-01 from the full AARP and NAC report, "Caregiving in the US 2025" (140 pages). The first version used news and blog summaries and contained errors; see "Corrections" at the end.

Source file: `docs/sources/aarp-nac-caregiving-in-the-us-2025/full-report.pdf`. Searchable text with page markers: `full-report.paged.txt` in the same folder. **"p." is the PDF page; printed page = PDF page minus 6.** The report was read in full by a subagent (all 6,930 lines); every figure below was then checked against the text by search, and the sole-caregiver chart was checked by viewing the PDF page.

`[Reddit file]` = `docs/qualitative-research-findings.md`. `[not in report]` = the full report has no figure for it.

## Geographic
- 40% of caregivers live with their care recipient (p.22). "More than 10 percent live an hour or more away"; the chart label is 11% (p.22, p.23).
- No breakdown of the long-distance 11% by age, income or race. [not in report]
- Urban 35%, suburban 44%, rural 20% (p.130). Rural was 12% in 2020; 2025 classifies it differently.
- Sibling circles spread across places: 15 of about 86 Reddit and forum sources are about updates, siblings and long distance. [Reddit file]
- Outside the US: Australia's Carer Gateway appears in our Reddit sources. Not assessed.

## Behavioral
- Remote monitoring 25% (13% in 2020); technology to track health records 41% (30% in 2020) (p.37).
- 44% are in high-intensity situations (p.33); the Key Takeaways box says 40%. Average 27 hours a week; 24% give 40+ hours (p.8). Average 5.5 years; nearly 30% five years or more (p.28).
- Almost two-thirds help with at least one daily-living activity (p.29). 55% do medical or nursing tasks, yet only 22% were trained (p.34).
- 70% monitor the recipient's health conditions (p.35). Only 58% say coordinating with health professionals is easy, down from 65% in 2015 (p.36).
- 34% have no other help, 30% unpaid help only, 12% paid help only (Figure 11, p.25).
- No question on how families share information with each other. [not in report]
- Siblings: the AARP/NAC report has no sibling figure (sibling appears only as a care recipient). A 2019 Northwestern Mutual press release (not the full study; method not shown) says 83% have siblings but only one in 10 say siblings share equally as a team; 40% say siblings do not help at all and 41% say they are the primary caregiver.
- Logins: JMIR 2025, 657 US adults (not only caregivers): 49% have had digital proxy duties; about one-third of proxies used informal access; about 10% of the sample logged on by knowing the account owner's credentials. Pharmacy and insurance logins specifically: not found.
- Group texts, WhatsApp, shared Google files and pill boxes; searches start after a hospital stay or new diagnosis. [Reddit file]

## Demographic
- 63 million US adults, 24.0% of all adults; 59 million care for an adult. Up 45% since 2015 (p.12, p.7).
- Average age 50.6 (the Executive Summary rounds to 51); 61% women, 38% men (p.15).
- 61% non-Hispanic white, 16% Hispanic, 13% Black, 6% Asian American, Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander (p.15).
- 40% care for a parent, 7% a parent-in-law, 15% a spouse or partner; 76% care for one person (p.17).
- 29% have children or grandchildren under 18 at home (p.24).
- Employed: 70% of caregivers aged 18 to 64 (p.38); 60% of all caregivers (p.130).
- Household income (p.130): under $50,000 30% (under $10,000 5%; $10,000 to $24,999 8%; $25,000 to $49,999 16%); $50,000 to $74,999 17%; $75,000 to $99,999 12%; $100,000 or more 41%. Median $68,770.
- Adult children caring for a parent: the project owner's own case and the focus of our Reddit sources. One case.

## Socioeconomic
- 47% had at least one negative financial impact, about two on average (p.43). About 1 in 5 report high financial strain (p.42).
- Stopped saving 31%; short-term savings used 24%; long-term savings used 13%; more debt 23%; bills unpaid or late 20% (p.43).
- 14% unable to afford basic expenses like food, up from 11% in 2020 (p.43).
- 9% gave up working entirely (p.41). 11% had to start working, work more or find a second job (p.43).
- 11.2 million caregivers were paid for care in 2025 (p.13).
- Dollar out-of-pocket spending: not in this report. [not in report]
- Willingness to pay (Caregiver Action Network Tech Insights Survey 2026, 272 people from its email list, self-selected): 32% said any helpful tool should be free; 28% would pay $11 to $25 a month for something that really reduces their stress; "Only 12% said they would personally pay out of pocket"; 64% believe Medicare, Medicaid or insurance should cover caregiving technology.
- Schulz et al. 2016 (512 caregivers aged 18-64, full text read in PubMed Central; `docs/sources/literature/schulz-2016-willingness-to-pay-PMC5019044.*`): about 20% would pay nothing for kitchen and self-care technologies; willing payers averaged about $50 a month for monitoring technology and $70 for monitoring plus assistance, "capped at around $70 per month". Not about coordination apps; web panel survey.
- Insurance type: not found.

## Psychographic (added)
- 56% felt they had no choice in taking the role; only 42% of them find purpose in it (p.50).
- 24% feel alone, up from 21% in 2020 (p.9). Nearly two-thirds report moderate or high emotional stress (p.48).
- One in five report fair or poor health (p.46). 23% report difficulty caring for their own health as a result of caregiving (p.47).
- The report does not measure guilt, overwhelm or burnout. Reddit does: "I feel lost. Angry. Guilty, and trapped." Burnout and choosing care are the two biggest themes (21 of about 86 sources each). [Reddit file]
- "They will text me things like 'thank you for all you do,' but they never actually step in." [Reddit file]
- Attitudes toward sharing logins and health data: not measured.

## By race and ethnicity (US caregivers; added 2026-11-01)

Source: AARP and NAC, Caregiving in the US 2025, `docs/sources/aarp-nac-caregiving-in-the-us-2025/full-report.pdf`. **p. = PDF page** (printed = PDF minus 6). Why this is here: the US market includes families from cultures with strong parent-care traditions. Where the report gives a number it is shown; where it says only "more often" no number is given. The report does not break these groups down by caring for a parent, long distance, or sharing information with siblings. [not in report]

- **Share of caregivers:** 61% non-Hispanic white, 16% Hispanic/Latino, 13% African American/Black, 6% Asian American, Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander (AANHPI) (p.7, p.15).
- **Living with the care recipient:** 40% overall; Hispanic/Latino 45%, African American/Black 43%. The report says this may reflect cultural comfort with multigenerational living, citing Guzman and Skow 2019 (p.22).
- **Caring for an adult and a child at home (sandwich generation):** African American/Black 36%, Hispanic/Latino 43%, more than overall (p.24).
- **Hours and difficulty of care:** African American/Black and Hispanic/Latino caregivers help with 2.2 daily-living tasks on average, vs 1.9 for all family caregivers of adults (p.30). A greater share of both groups are in high-intensity situations (p.33).
- **Medical tasks:** African American/Black caregivers more often do medical and nursing tasks (62%) and are more often trained for them (27%) (p.34).
- **Talking to providers and advocating:** African American/Black 70% and AANHPI 73% communicate with providers, vs all caregivers; advocating 65% and 69% (p.36).
- **Employment:** 66% of African American/Black family caregivers work, vs 57% of all family caregivers (p.38).
- **Money:** African American/Black, Hispanic/Latino, younger and lower-income caregivers more often report negative financial impacts (p.9).
- **Health:** difficulty caring for own health 27% among Hispanic/Latino caregivers (p.47); physical strain 28% vs 19% for other caregivers (p.48).
- **Meaning:** 58% of Hispanic/Latino caregivers say the role gives them a sense of purpose (p.50).
- **Paid family caregivers** are more often Hispanic/Latino (23% vs 15% of unpaid), Black (21% vs 11%) and AANHPI (6% vs 4%) (p.16).
- **Limits:** the survey used a web panel with added samples of Black, Hispanic and Asian American respondents (p.118); AANHPI is one broad group, with no sub-groups reported in the passages above. Group differences are from the report's text; I did not check each against its figures.
