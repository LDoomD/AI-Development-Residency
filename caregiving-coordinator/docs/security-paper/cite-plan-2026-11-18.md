# Cite plan for the Premium paper: which sources carry claims, by topic (2026-11-18)

**Status: Approved by the owner on 2026-11-18**, together with two additions (NIST SP 800-207 and the Vercel security docs). It narrows the reading plan. It does not remove anything from `caregiving-coordinator/docs/security-paper/source-list-2026-11-17.md`, which stays the list of approved sources.

**Why it exists.** The course sets no source count. The research rule says to read in full every source that is cited. So we read in full the sources that carry claims, read only the cited part of the others, and do not read what the paper will not cite. This follows what the proposed caregiving coordinator needs (`caregiving-coordinator/docs/clean-deliverables/non-functional-epics-features-tasks-2026-11-15.md` and `caregiving-coordinator/docs/risk-profile.md`): a Next.js app on Vercel with Supabase sign-in and row-level security, care circles with roles, consent records and an access log, health and money notes about another adult, invite emails through an outside email service, no trackers, no AI service and no data feeds, a public repository with fake data, backups, locked package versions and a 60-day breach plan. The idea is a hypothesis. Nothing here says it is secure or compliant.

## Verizon DBIR: which sections to read
Read in full: key findings and results (pages 1 to 27, done), System Intrusion, Social Engineering, Basic Web Application Attacks, Miscellaneous Errors, Privilege Misuse, Healthcare (pages 86 to 87), small and medium businesses, and the methodology appendix. Not read, not cited: denial of service, the North Korean IT worker section, the regions, the other industries, and the AI sections beyond one line saying the idea uses no AI service. Verizon's rule on paraphrasing is in `caregiving-coordinator/docs/sources/security-paper/notes/verizon-dbir-2026-notes.md`.
