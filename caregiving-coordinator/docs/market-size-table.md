## Not in this table

- **Segments 11 to 14** (respite and adult day care, caregiver mental health, telehealth, insurance and benefits navigation) were not researched this session. The earlier file `docs/market-research-segments.md` has figures for them that I have not re-verified, so I left them out.
- **Number of people.** The template defines market size as people and money. This table is money only. The caregiver count is documented separately: 63 million US family caregivers, up 45% in a decade (AARP and NAC, 2025: aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers). It is not split by segment.
- **No source here is a primary report.** All figures come from publisher summaries and press releases. The full reports are paywalled, so definitions and methods were not checked.

## Update 2026-11-02: what the new research changes

The table above is unchanged (publisher dollar figures). The new papers and forum read change how far to trust it.

- **Long-distance caregivers: no single number.** My earlier estimate of about 6.5 million long-distance caregivers applied AARP's 11% (all caregivers, PDF pp.22 to 23) to the 59 to 63 million total. A 2026 Gerontologist paper gives 13.2% for a different group: adult children of Medicare beneficiaries aged 71+ who live an hour or more away (NHATS round 11, 2021; 932 caregivers representing over 9 million). The two bases differ, so do not stack or average them. Say "about 11% of all caregivers (AARP) and 13.2% of adult children of people 71+ (NHATS)". Source: `docs/academic-findings.md`.
- **Dollar markets do not show families paying.** Two surveys put a ceiling on what families pay: Schulz et al. 2016 (512 caregivers aged 18 to 64) found about 20% would pay nothing and willing payers capped at about $70 a month, for monitoring, not coordination. The Caregiver Action Network 2026 survey (272 people from its email list, self-selected) found 32% say a helpful tool should be free and "only 12%" would personally pay out of pocket. In the 141 forum threads, 10 mention the cost of a tool or app, and none says they pay for a family coordination app (`docs/community-voice-findings.md`). So the Caregiver App row is what publishers say the category earns, not what families spend.
- **Who pays may be the unanswered question.** The employer row (Cariloop, Wellthy) is the only row where the buyer is not the family. The CAN survey also found 64% think insurance or Medicare should cover caregiving technology.
- **Still not measured:** how many families use any coordination app, and the size of the "paper and group text" alternative.
