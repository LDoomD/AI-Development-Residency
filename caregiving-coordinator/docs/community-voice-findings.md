## What was read

- **Forum threads: 141.** AgingCare 40 (2026, mostly about parents) and ALZConnected 101 (mostly spouses; first page of each thread only).
- **YouTube: 12 videos**, 500 or fewer top comment threads each (YouTube's relevance order). Readers counted only comments where someone describes their own situation: about 1,500 of the threads, hand-counted and approximate.
- All files were read in full by seven readers. All 1,121 returned quotes were checked word for word against the saved files and all matched (the checker was tested with altered and invented quotes and rejected them).

## Against the NotebookLM summary

`docs/qualitative-research-findings.md` (about 86 sources, summaries not saved) gave one main theme per source: choosing and paying for care 24%, burnout 24%, updates, siblings and long distance 17%, medications 13%, money and insurance 6%. This pass gives, for the 141 forum threads (primary theme, then "anywhere in the thread"): burnout 17% (39%), dementia behavior 15% (31%), choosing and paying for care 9% (26%), money and legal 8% (32%), medications 6% (14%), family coordination 4% (20%), long distance 1% (6%). On YouTube, family coordination is about 10% of themed comments.

What holds: burnout and guilt is the top or second theme in every source. What does not: choosing care, medications and coordination each rank lower here as the main topic than in the NotebookLM summary. Likely reasons, not tested: ALZConnected is a dementia community (dementia behavior is 21 of its 101 threads), and different readers assigned the themes. The NotebookLM relative weights are not reproduced, so they should not be quoted as findings.

## Limits

- Forum posters are frequent posters; ALZConnected is a dementia community with mostly spouses; AgingCare skews to parents and to people who write at length. Not a sample of all caregivers.
- Only the first page of long ALZConnected threads, and the visible replies of AgingCare threads, were saved. Six AgingCare threads have a title but no post body (the page itself has none).
- YouTube comments react to a video and are shown in YouTube's relevance order. Some commenters are outside the US, care for children or are paid caregivers; readers flagged this. One video's comments are mostly not about caregiving.
- Theme counts come from readers who assigned one primary theme per thread. They are approximate and were not double-coded. YouTube counts are hand counts.
- Spouses are the largest group in the ALZConnected half (49 of 101); this project's origin case is an adult child caring for a parent.
- The searches were not about coordination apps, so silence on apps is weak evidence either way.

## Not checked
