## Bottom line

This board is a **generic Tech Fleet / Community template**, prefilled in two very different ways:

1. **Instructional template content** — generic explainers, example AI prompts, empty fill-in-the-blank tables. This is reusable for any project, including caregiving-coordinator.
2. **Actual filled-in research data** — and every single piece of real, filled-in research on this board is about the **old ADHD learning app project**, from before the pivot. None of it is about caregiving-coordinator. There is no caregiving market data on this board at all.

So the honest answer to "what does the market research suggest we start with" is: **nothing on this board is caregiving market research yet — it's all leftover from the previous project.** What's genuinely useful here is the *template/prompt structure* that already worked once, which we can reuse by substituting caregiving-coordinator's own segments.

## What's actually on the board

- **SAMPLE AI PROMPTS TO PERFORM MARKET RESEARCH**: two example prompts built around a hypothetical "reduce food waste" app. Prompt 1 asks for the top 10 market segments; Prompt 2 asks for the top 5 market channels, top 5 direct/indirect competitors per channel, market size, and CAGR, with a specified output format and an explicit requirement to cross-reference data and cite sources. **This is the one genuinely reusable artifact on the board** — a proven prompt shape we can reuse for caregiving-coordinator's own market.
- **OUTCOMES OF THE ANALYSIS / PROBLEMS TO SOLVE / SCOPE OF THE ANALYSIS**: generic framing questions with example sticky notes from the instructor (Morgan Denner) — e.g., "The business is not sure what the unique value proposition is yet." Not filled in for either project; just the template's own examples.
- **Tech Fleet's own AI-prompt example** (a large text block): a worked example of competitor analysis, but it's Tech Fleet analyzing *BetterUp* as a competitor to itself in the leadership-development/agile-coaching/life-coaching space — this is the instructor's own illustrative example, not related to either of our projects. Good as a model for how detailed a competitor writeup should be, nothing more.

## What this means for caregiving-coordinator

- **We cannot reuse any of the filled-in data.** None of the market sizes, competitors, or opportunity framing apply — a caregiving coordination app is a different market entirely (closer to CareZone, CaringBridge, Lotsa Helping Hands, digital caregiver platforms — none of which appear on this board at all).
- **We can reuse the prompt template and table schema.** The "SAMPLE AI PROMPTS" structure (ask for top market segments → then ask for channels/competitors/market size/CAGR per segment, with sourcing required) is a good, already-proven shape to reuse for our own research. Same for the Market Size table's column structure (Market, Channels/Segments, Audience Fit, Today's Size, Projected Growth, Top Companies, Source, Notes/Caveats) and the Competitor table's column structure (Competitor, Direct/Indirect, Market Segment, Website, Features, Differentiation, Strengths, Weaknesses, + follow-up questions).
- **Nothing here tells us what to build.** It tells us how the previous round of market research was structured and sourced — which is a useful process template — but the actual starting point for caregiving-coordinator's market/competitor analysis still needs to be done from scratch, the same way the old research was done for the learning app.

## Suggested next step

Reuse the proven two-prompt structure (top market segments first, then channels/competitors/size/CAGR per segment with required sourcing) and the same table schemas, but pointed at caregiving-coordination's actual market — likely segments like digital caregiver coordination platforms, family care-management apps, and medication/health-tracking apps, with real competitors like CareZone, CaringBridge, and Lotsa Helping Hands as a starting list rather than assumed.
