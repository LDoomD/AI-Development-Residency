# Audience summary for Claude (2026-11-02)

Purpose: one short, plain input for the next steps (problem selection, vision board, product work). It says who the two audiences are, what they want, and what goes wrong for them, with how well each point is supported. **Nothing here is a design or a solution.** Sources: AARP and NAC, Caregiving in the US 2025 (full report, page numbers in the board and in `docs/audience-goals-use-cases.md`); 117 caregiver forum threads confirmed by a strict check (`docs/sources/forum-relevance/`); every forum quote was checked against the saved thread.

## How to read the counts
A "thread" is one forum conversation (a post plus replies). "10 of 55 threads" means 10 of the 55 conversations from that audience mentioned the theme. Counts are minimums and approximate: one reader sorted each quote, and the forums (AgingCare, ALZConnected) skew toward dementia caregivers. They show how often a theme came up, not how many caregivers share it.

## The two audiences

### Audience 1: adult children caring for a parent (55 of 117 threads)
- Who: caring for a parent (40%) or parent-in-law (7%) of all caregivers; average caregiver age 50.6; 61% women; 70% of those aged 18 to 64 are employed (AARP pp.15, 17, 38-39).
- Where they live (only 24 threads say): 16 apart, 8 together.
- Help from others: in all 117 threads, family helps 13, does not help 14, conflict 10, not mentioned 59, unclear 21.
- Own case from the project owner (told in chat, not research data): siblings pooled money for gadgets for their father.

### Audience 2: spouses and partners caring for a husband, wife or partner (54 of 117 threads)
- Who: 15% of caregivers care for a spouse or partner (AARP p.17). The report has no separate figures for spouse caregivers.
- Context for caregivers who live with the person (40% of all): less unpaid family help (p.22); 44% in high-intensity care, average 27 hours a week, 34% with no other help (pp.33, 8, 25); 56% felt they had no choice and 24% feel alone (pp.50, 9).
- Where they live (only 16 threads say): 12 together, 4 apart.

## Audience 1: what drives them and what they want

**Motivations (what drives them)**
- Keep them safe and comfortable, spare them suffering (7 of 55 threads)
- Do the right thing, avoid blame or guilt (6 of 55 threads)
- Keep them at home and in control of their own life (5 of 55 threads)
- Love, loyalty and promises (3 of 55 threads)

**Situational goals (what they want to get done)**
- Get help, relief or services (11 of 55 threads)
- Find, choose or pay for a place (memory care, assisted living) (6 of 55 threads)
- Manage money and legal affairs (3 of 55 threads)
- Understand what is happening and what to do (2 of 55 threads)
- Keep them safe and watch over them (2 of 55 threads)
- Medicines (1 of 55 threads)

**Life goals (what they want in their own life)**
- Time and breaks for themselves (4 of 55 threads)
- Keep their own life, job and money (3 of 55 threads)
- Their own health and sanity (1 of 55 threads)

## Audience 1: problems, most common first, with the outcome we assume

### Exhaustion, guilt and no life of their own (23 of 55 threads)
- Example: “Right now I’m resentful and really don’t understand why I can’t get over it and just be there for her the way a daughter should be.” (thread agi-499699)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499616, agi-499798]): We want the caregiver to have breaks and support so caring does not cost them their health, job or relationships.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver can step away for a few hours and knows the person is looked after [supported by agi-499801, agi-499890]; The caregiver can find support at times that fit a working life [supported by alz-77398]; The caregiver is not the only one carrying the load [supported by agi-498946, agi-499890]

### Dementia behavior and hard conversations (14 of 55 threads)
- Example: “We have tried many times to take her to lunch, shopping etc. but it always ends with her request to be taken "home" to see her dogs.” (thread alz-77399)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-76756, alz-77460]): We want caregivers to know what to say and do when the person is anxious, angry or confused, so visits and calls do not end in distress.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): A caregiver gets through a hard call or visit without a meltdown [supported by alz-77399, alz-77460]; A caregiver knows when a behavior is the illness and when it needs a doctor [supported by alz-77399]; Repeated calls do not take over the caregiver's workday [supported by alz-76894]

### Money, legal papers and facility bills (11 of 55 threads)
- Example: “the house needs several repairs before we can put it on the market, including interior painting, ceiling and window frame repairs from previous water leaks, and basement mold remediation.” (thread alz-77396)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499298, alz-77373]): We want families to understand and control money, legal papers and facility bills, so nothing is signed under pressure or paid wrongly.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): A caregiver knows what a facility charges and why [supported by agi-499732]; A caregiver can apply for benefits without being rushed [supported by agi-499298]; The person with legal authority and the relatives who help can see the same account information [supported by agi-499820]

### Relatives do not help, or block each other (10 of 55 threads)
- Example: “When I broke down and begged them to help me, younger sister told me, "We all make our choices."” (thread agi-499771)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-77412, alz-77423]): We want relatives to share the load and the information, without conflict or going through lawyers.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): A sibling who lives far away knows what is needed and does their part [partly supported by alz-77366, alz-77386]; A relative with legal authority cannot keep others from the person without a reason they can see [supported by agi-499818]; Decisions about the parent are made together [partly supported by alz-76894, alz-77401]

### Medicines, eating and personal care (8 of 55 threads)
- Example: “It was a lot of work every morning of cleaning him up, and doing all the laundry.” (thread agi-499448)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-498744, agi-499433]): We want the person to take their medicines, eat and stay clean without a daily fight and without the caregiver doing it all alone.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver knows whether a dose was taken or missed [partly supported by agi-498744]; Morning and night personal care does not exhaust the caregiver [supported by agi-499448]; The person eats enough [supported by agi-499433, alz-77401]

### Choosing and arranging a place for them (6 of 55 threads)
- Example: “My Mom had agreed to move into AL and then turned around yesterday (about a week before it was time to move) and says 100% no way.” (thread alz-77401)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-77399, alz-77401]): We want families to arrange a move to assisted living or a nursing home with confidence, without the person being forced or tricked.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): A family can compare places and costs before a crisis [partly supported by agi-499762, alz-77385]; The person has a say in the move [supported by alz-77386, alz-77401]; A move is planned for a time when it can go well [partly supported by alz-77401]

### Staying safe at home (6 of 55 threads)
- Example: “She has walked, "jogged", alone, up and down the street we live on every day and only recently, and on only one occasion (that we know of) gotten disoriented and lost.” (thread alz-77129)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499659, alz-77417]): We want the person to stay safe at home without constant watching.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver knows if the person left home or fell [supported by agi-499433, alz-77382]; Hazards such as the stove do not cause harm [supported by agi-499659, alz-77417]; A person who wanders is found quickly [partly supported by alz-77129, alz-77393]

### Getting answers from doctors and hospitals (4 of 55 threads)
- Example: “His diagnosis was missed by several doctors and with HIPPA, if it was caught, no one knew so we could start getting help sooner.” (thread alz-77385)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499762, alz-77005]): We want families to get clear information and quick callbacks from doctors, hospitals and programs.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): A caregiver gets a diagnosis sooner [supported by alz-77385]; A caregiver gets a callback within days, not weeks [no supporting quote]; Privacy rules do not leave the caregiver without answers [supported by alz-77385]

### Being far away and not knowing what is happening (2 of 55 threads)
- Example: “I’m POA to my elderly mother who lives out of state and I have been dealing with missing information from people close to her who live in her town which is causing me a great deal of stress” (thread agi-499712)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499712]): We want relatives who live far away to stay informed and able to help.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): A caregiver far away has the information the people near the parent have [supported by agi-499712]; Calls and visits from far away still help the parent [partly supported by alz-77460]

## Audience 2: problems, most common first, with the outcome we assume

### Exhaustion, loneliness and own health (22 of 54 threads)
- Example: “I am tired of doing it all - cooking, cleaning, daily laundry, paying bills, grocery shoping, doctors appointments, I’m even tired of crying everyday” (thread alz-77470)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499222, alz-77379]): We want the spouse caregiver to keep their own health and have someone to lean on.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver gets regular breaks [supported by alz-77390, alz-77406]; The caregiver has someone to talk to [supported by alz-77379, alz-77427]; The caregiver's own medical needs are not put off [partly supported by agi-499378, alz-77375]

### Dementia behavior and hard conversations (18 of 54 threads)
- Example: “She is determined to stay independent and takes offense to any questions or reminders about medications and other normal daily questions or reminders.” (thread alz-77457)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-77348, alz-77377]): We want caregivers to handle agitation, repeated questions and refusal without anger or exhaustion.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver finds something that calms the person when the usual tactics stop working [supported by alz-77414, alz-77449]; Evening restlessness does not end the day in conflict [supported by agi-498938, alz-77406]; The caregiver knows when to ask a doctor about behavior or medicine [partly supported by alz-77414]

### Personal care and medicines (12 of 54 threads)
- Example: “She also forgets when she has a glass of wine if we go out to dinner and comes home and pours another.” (thread alz-77112)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499873, alz-59049]): We want bathing, toileting, medicines and eating to be manageable at home.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): Night-time incontinence does not wreck the caregiver's sleep [partly supported by agi-499222, alz-59049]; The person takes medicine without a fight [supported by agi-498938]; Doctors can see the readings or notes the caregiver keeps [no supporting quote]

### Safety when they are alone or out (9 of 54 threads)
- Example: “when there’s no family restroom and my DH has to go into the men’s room by himself and there’s no other men going on or coming out” (thread alz-77263)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-77407]): We want the person to be safe when the caregiver is not with them and when they go out.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver can leave the house briefly knowing the person is safe [supported by alz-77407]; Public places work for a couple, for example restrooms [supported by alz-77263, alz-77410]; A conversation about driving can happen in time [supported by alz-77437]

### Aides, hospice and trusting others (8 of 54 threads)
- Example: “when I was gone for a few hours at a time, she had nothing to hold her accountable, she didn't do anything at all.” (thread agi-499829)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-499829]): We want caregivers to trust paid help and know what happened while they were away.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver knows how an aide's visit went [supported by agi-499829]; A hospice or aide visit happens as promised [supported by agi-499829, alz-77395]; The caregiver can leave the person with others for a few hours [supported by alz-77263, alz-77410]

### Placing them and care costs (7 of 54 threads)
- Example: “It is a huge decision for us, as what will I have after he’s gone? And living on my income if/when he is in MC will be tight!” (thread alz-77354)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by agi-498938, alz-77392]): We want families to place a spouse in memory care at the right time, in a good place, and to be able to afford it.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver can judge when home care is no longer enough [partly supported by alz-77354, alz-77375]; The caregiver can find a good place and pay for it [supported by alz-77364, alz-77392]; Care in the new place meets the spouse's needs [supported by alz-77375, alz-77471]

### Money, scams and bills (6 of 54 threads)
- Example: “I am so overwhelmed with his bills- left so unintended- and the lack of addressing so many things when he walked out of his job” (thread alz-77428)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-77379, alz-77411]): We want the spouse's money, bills and exposure to scams under control.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver can take over bills that were left behind [supported by alz-77411, alz-77428]; The person cannot be scammed or overspend [supported by alz-77379, alz-77411]; Care costs can be planned [partly supported by alz-75137, alz-77473]

### Diagnosis and doctor delays (4 of 54 threads)
- Example: “DW finally getting actual sustained treatment after nine months of insurance changes, delays, one rogue neuro PA, etc.” (thread alz-77371)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-77371]): We want a diagnosis and treatment without long waits.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): A person with new symptoms sees a specialist within weeks [supported by alz-77086, alz-77371]; The family understands what is wrong [supported by alz-77409]

### Relatives and others not helping or criticizing (3 of 54 threads)
- Example: “the bigger problem for me is his daughter and exwife that have given me more grief than anyone else. They aren't supportive, they degrade me” (thread alz-77427)
- Outcome we assume (ASSUMPTION, not tested; evidence [supported by alz-77427, alz-77470]): We want family and friends to support the caregiver, not criticize.
- Assumed scenarios (ASSUMPTIONS): The caregiver has family who step in [supported by alz-77470]; Criticism from relatives does not add to the stress [supported by alz-77374, alz-77427]

## Where the two audiences differ (share of threads; full table in `docs/audience-goals-use-cases.md`)
- Adult children: relatives not helping or blocking (18% vs 6%), money and legal papers (20% vs 11%), mostly live apart.
- Spouses and partners: love, loyalty and promises as a motivation (17% vs 5%), own health and sanity as a life goal (13% vs 2%), trusting aides and hospice (15%, not a theme for the other audience), dementia behavior (33% vs 25%), medicines and personal care (22% vs 15%), mostly live together.
- Same: exhaustion and loneliness is the top problem for both (42% and 41%).

## Limits
- Forum threads are not a sample of all caregivers, and they lean toward dementia.
- No caregiver was interviewed; nothing here shows who would pay or what people would do.
- The outcomes and scenarios are assumptions; the tag after each says which threads back it and how well. One draft line with no supporting quote was removed.
- Audiences were chosen because they are the two largest groups in both sources (AARP Figure 3: parent or parent-in-law 47%, spouse or partner 15%), not by value to a business. That choice still has to be made at the vision board.
- Caring for your own adult child (6% in AARP) was not analyzed.
