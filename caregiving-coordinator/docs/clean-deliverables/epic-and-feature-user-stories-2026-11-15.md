# Epic and feature user stories (approved 2026-11-15)

**What this is:** the user story statements for the 6 epics (10 stories, one per audience) and the 10 features in `epics-features-tasks-numbered-2026-11-15.md`. This file is the master copy. Notion and any other copy must say "same as this file, [date]" and match it word for word.

**Why they exist:** the instructor's board, in the "User Story Statements" section, says: "User stories can be created for all levels... epics and features also should have user story statements (and not acceptance criteria)." The Notion "Epics and Features" template has the heading "Feature-Level User Story" under each feature. Acceptance criteria belong to tasks only.

**Rules used (instructor's board):**
- One sentence: "As a [who], I want [goal] so that [problem or value]." The goal is never the solution.
- At epic and feature level, the "so that" states the value to the person. At task level it states the problem to avoid.
- Each person who benefits gets a separate story, so an epic that helps both audiences has two stories.

**Where the wording comes from:**
- The audience in each story is the epic's or feature's audience in the numbered file.
- Each feature story's "so that" comes from the approved scope table row's "Associated Problem" in `vision-and-scope-boards-2026-11-10.md`. Example: Audience 1 breaks: "Today, adult children carry the care, often alone, and can't get a break even when help exists."
- Each epic story's goal comes from the epic's business goal. The Epic 4 "so that" comes from competitive criterion 1, "Knowing what to do," as the 2026-11-11 epics file records.

**Tags:** the two Epic 3 feature stories were approved earlier on 2026-11-15 (the wording is Claude's). The other 18 are Claude's wording, approved by the owner on 2026-11-15 as a set.

A1 = Audience 1, adult children (or children-in-law) caring for a parent. A2 = Audience 2, spouses or partners.

## Feature stories (10)

| # | Feature | Story |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Getting real breaks they can afford (A1) | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want real breaks I can afford, even when help exists but I can't reach it, so that I keep my own health and life. |
| 1.2 | Getting real breaks and a life outside caring (A2) | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want real breaks and a life outside caring so that I keep my health, my sleep and my closeness with my partner. |
| 2.1 | Having siblings share the care and costs (A1) | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want my siblings and other relatives to share the care and costs so that I'm not carrying it alone. |
| 2.2 | Having relatives who support, not criticize (A2) | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want relatives who support me instead of criticizing so that I'm not alone with the care. |
| 3.1 | Getting the parent to accept doctors or help (A1) | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want my parent to accept the doctors and help they need, with their own choices respected, so that their care happens instead of stopping at a refusal. |
| 3.2 | Bringing in outside help the partner accepts (A2) | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want to bring in outside help my partner accepts so that care continues without every talk turning into a fight. |
| 4.1 | Calming outbursts and calls, and staying calm themselves (A1) | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want to handle my parent's anger, confusion and anxiety and stay calm myself so that visits, calls and hard talks don't fall apart. |
| 4.2 | Understanding the behavior and how to respond (A2) | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want to understand my partner's behavior and how to respond so that daily life and talks stop turning into fights. |
| 5.1 | Having legal authority and papers sorted in time (A1) | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want the legal papers, benefits and bills sorted in time so that care costs and paperwork don't pile up into a crisis. |
| 6.1 | Protecting their money and avoiding debt (A2) | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want to protect our money and avoid debt so that we keep our home, food, coverage and healthcare. |

## Epic stories (10)

| Epic | Audience | Story |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Remove the barriers that keep caregivers from getting a real break. | A1 | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want to get a real break from caring so that I keep my own health and life. |
| 1 | A2 | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want to get a real break and keep a life outside caring so that I keep my health, my sleep and my closeness with my partner. |
| 2. Make sure caregivers don't carry the care alone, with relatives sharing the work. | A1 | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want my relatives to share the work so that I'm not carrying the care alone. |
| 2 | A2 | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want my family and friends to support me, not criticize me, so that I'm not alone with the care. |
| 3. Increase how often a parent or partner accepts outside help. | A1 | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want my parent to accept the outside help they need so that their care continues instead of stopping at a refusal. |
| 3 | A2 | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want my partner to accept outside help so that care continues without every talk turning into a fight. |
| 4. Make personal, ongoing guidance reachable without an employer or a high hourly bill. | A1 | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want personal, ongoing guidance without an employer or a high hourly bill so that I know what to do as my parent changes. |
| 4 | A2 | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want personal, ongoing guidance without an employer or a high hourly bill so that I know how to respond as my partner's behavior changes. |
| 5. Make the money, papers and accounts manageable, even when nothing was prepared ahead. | A1 | As an adult child caring for a parent, I want the money, papers and accounts in order, even when nothing was prepared ahead, so that care costs and paperwork don't pile up into a crisis. |
| 6. Protect caregivers' own health, job and money while they care. | A2 | As a spouse or partner caring for my partner, I want to protect my own health, job and money while I care so that we keep our home, food, coverage and healthcare. |
