**Scope table rule:**
- **Value:** the board's value stickies.
- **Associated Problem:** "Today, " + the board's problem sticky.
- **Outcomes:** the other "What we want to change" items in table 2b for that problem.
- **Resulting Scope Items:** "We will bring the value of … by:" + the same outcomes.

There's one wording change: the outcomes in table 2b start "We want them to have…", and the scope tables say "Having…". Nothing else was changed. "Build" from Morgan's example was replaced with "bring the value of", because the lecture calls "build" a solution (1:43:15 to 1:43:37).

## Shared boxes (the same on all 4 boards unless noted)

**Business Goals (6)**
1. Remove the barriers that keep caregivers from getting a real break.
2. Make sure caregivers don't carry the care alone, with relatives sharing the work.
3. Increase how often a parent or partner accepts outside help.
4. Make personal, ongoing guidance reachable without an employer or a high hourly bill.
5. Make the money, papers and accounts manageable, even when nothing was prepared ahead.
6. Protect caregivers' own health, job and money while they care.

**Competitive Advantage (4; Audience 2 has the first 3)**
- Unlike others, this focuses on getting caregivers to the real breaks that already exist.
- Unlike others, this focuses on getting a parent or partner to accept help, and relatives to share the work.
- Unlike others, this focuses on personal, ongoing guidance without an employer or a high hourly bill.
- Unlike others, this focuses on the money, papers and accounts, even when the parent didn't prepare ahead. *(Audience 1 only)*

**Barriers to Product Success (8; Audience 2 has 7)**
- We don't know who pays for help: families, employers or Medicare. This is still unproven.
- Few caregivers would pay out of pocket, and those who would pay only a small amount.
- Health data and consent are a risk. Today's tools have shown medical details to invited friends, and let parents lock what relatives see.
- A parent who refuses to go is a top reason relief goes unused. *(Audience 1 only)*
- Cost, eligibility rules, waitlists and rigid hours keep caregivers from relief.
- The help that exists differs by state and by local agency.
- Some data is still missing: some places can't be scored yet for relatives sharing the work.
- Our evidence comes from online posts, read by one person and not double-checked, so it leans toward hard moments.

**Costs (2)**
- Keeping the vision and scope up to date as new research comes in.
- We don't know the money and time costs of the release yet.

**KPIs (6), one per goal.** Format: "We have a goal to <goal>. We will define success with the following KPI: **<KPI>**"
1. % of caregivers who get a real break
2. % of caregivers whose relatives share the work
3. % of caregivers whose parent or partner accepts outside help
4. % of caregivers who get personal, ongoing guidance without an employer or a high hourly bill
5. % of caregivers who get the money, papers and accounts in order
6. % of caregivers who keep their own health, job and money while caring
