# Questions to ask about competitors in the caregiving niche (2026-11-02)

DRAFT for the project owner to choose from. This is a list of questions, not answers. Problems only, no solution framing. Nothing here has been added to Figma.

## Part 1. What standard guides say to ask (documented)

Four public guides were saved and read in full (copies in `docs/sources/competitor-questions/`, entry 2v in `docs/sources/INDEX.md`). Common questions, in my own words:

| Theme | Questions the guides ask | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Product | What does it do and which problems does it aim to solve? Which problems does it not solve? | Aha! (lines 272-274), Productboard (line 88), Salesforce (lines 41-44) |
| Money | How do they monetize (pricing tiers, packaging, contract terms)? Who pays? | Productboard (line 90), Coursera (line 60), Aha! (line 290), Salesforce (lines 50-53: what is in the base cost, free trial) |
| Customers | Who are they selling to? How do their personas differ from ours? | Aha! (lines 282-285), Productboard (line 92) |
| Acquisition | How do they acquire customers (self-serve or sold through organizations)? Which channels? | Productboard (line 96), Coursera (lines 65-69), Salesforce (lines 56-58: which segments and channels) |
| Customer experience and retention | What are the touchpoints and where is the friction? What happens after signing up (support, upsells, community)? | Coursera (lines 83-88) |
| Reviews | What do users love and struggle with? What needs does it fail to meet? | Aha! (lines 300-302), Productboard (line 94) |
| Strength of the company | Who is behind it, how well funded, how many customers? | Productboard (lines 98, 102) |
| Direction | Moving upmarket or downmarket? Where is the market heading? | Productboard (lines 117-119) |

The template's own three questions (business model and how they make money, how they retain customers, how they approach customer experience) match the Money, Customer experience and retention rows above.

Limit: the guides are general business guides, written for products in general. None of them is about caregiving.

## Part 2. Questions specific to the caregiving niche (my reading, each tied to our evidence)

These are my suggestions, built from what our own research found. They are not from the guides.

| # | Question | Why it matters here (evidence in our files) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who uses it and who pays: the caregiver, the older person, an employer, a health plan or a care company? | The paper research found no study on willingness to pay for coordination, only for monitoring (`docs/academic-findings.md` line 27). Nobody in the forum sample named paying for a coordination app (`docs/community-voice-findings.md`). |
| 2 | Is the person being cared for a customer, a user, or neither? Can they say no? | The "older person cannot or will not use it" problem appears in the caregiving-only reviews (`docs/app-review-findings.md`). |
| 3 | Can a second or third relative join without the account holder handing over a login? What does it take (invite, permission, legal document)? | Access for another person is one of the most common problems in reviews and forums (`docs/app-review-findings.md`; 17 of 141 forum threads mention logins or accounts). |
| 4 | How does it treat health data and consent (who sees what, whether it says it is covered by health privacy law, how a person is removed)? | Health data and shared credentials are this product area's main risks (`docs/risk-profile.md`). |
| 5 | What happens when it fails during an emergency (a missed fall, a wrong location, no alert)? How does support respond? | "A person in danger and the tool did not help" and "alerts missed or late" are among the problems in the caregiving-only reviews (`docs/app-review-findings.md`). |
| 6 | What does setup take, and who does it (the caregiver, the older person, a technician)? Does it work on the phones the family already has? | Setup and phone compatibility trouble appear in the caregiving-only reviews (`docs/app-review-findings.md`). |
| 7 | What does it do when a family has no tech skills, or the person refuses to use it (paper, phone call, a person who answers)? | Simple tools and paper work for many families (23 counter-evidence quotes, `docs/community-voice-findings.md`). |
| 8 | Is it sold to families directly, or through employers, health plans, or care companies (so the caregiver never pays)? | The scoring draft notes that the choosing and paying problem is mostly sold through employers and plans (Figma 3rd Version scoring, problem 7 note). |
| 9 | What does it cost after the free period or free tier, and what is behind the paywall? | Price and paywall complaints appear in the reviews (6 of 192 problem reviews). |
| 10 | Does it serve the caregiver's own wellbeing, or only the care tasks? | Burnout and guilt is the top or second theme in every source (`docs/community-voice-findings.md`). |
| 11 | How does it handle a change of situation (a move to a care home, a death, a new caregiver)? What happens to the data and the people with access? | Not tested in our evidence yet; this is a gap question. |
| 12 | What do customers do instead when they stop using it (paper, group texts, a different app)? | Paper notebooks and group texts are the most common tools in the forum sample (`docs/community-voice-findings.md`). |

## What this does not show

- It does not show which questions the teacher or the client expects. The template says to write the questions based on the unique needs of the project (Figma Section 8).
- Questions 4, 8 and 11 may need information a company page does not state, so the answer in the table may be "not found."
- Retention data (how long customers stay) is rarely public. The answer will usually be a signal, such as reviews about cancelling, not a number.
