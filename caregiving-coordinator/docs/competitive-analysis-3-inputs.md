## 2. What the audience work says

### Audience 1 (adult child caring for a parent), the main audience

- **Use cases, seen in (top 7):**
  1. Exhaustion (138)
  2. Dementia behavior (82)
  3. Money, papers, accounts (74; 132 accounts posts in the targeted search)
  4. Medicines and hands-on care (62)
  5. The parent refuses help (60)
  6. Relatives don't help (48)
  7. Choosing a place (46)
- **Situational goals:** understand what's happening and what to do (73); get help or services (58); money and legal papers under control (49).
- **Life goals:** their own health and sanity (73); keep their job and money (52); time and breaks (23).
- **Motivations:** keep the parent safe and comfortable; do the right thing without guilt; love and promises; let the parent stay home and choose for themselves.

### Audience 2 (spouse or partner)

A smaller sample. Its top use cases: exhaustion (45), dementia behavior (24), personal care and medicines (24), and **aides, hospice and trusting helpers (14)**. New life goal: companionship.

### Segmentation boxes (AARP/NAC 2025 and other sources)

- 70% of working-age caregivers are employed.
- 55% do medical or nursing tasks, and only 22% were trained.
- 34% have no other help.
- Median household income is $68,770, and 30% earn under $50,000.
- Only 12% would pay out of pocket (CAN 2026), and willing payers cap near $70 a month (Schulz 2016).
- More than 10% live an hour or more away.

## 3. How these lead each step of the competitive analysis

1. **Problems to solve (step 1):** from the problem list and audience goals above.
2. **Problems to cover (step 2):** Audience 1's top 7 use cases, sized by the market analysis.
3. **Competitors (step 3):**
   - **Start from the segment table above.** Every company named in a segment that touches a top-7 problem is a candidate.
   - Add candidates for the problems with no segment (2 and 5).
   - Add what families use instead.
   - Show every candidate, and give a reason for each one left out.
   - Label each one: business, nonprofit, government program, or workaround.
   - The owner chooses 5 to 7 direct, plus indirect.
4. **Questions (step 4):** from the audience goals and the segmentation facts.
5. **Table cells (step 5):** **one short sentence** that answers the column's question for a caregiver (what job it does, and why it works or fails). Counts and quotes go in the evidence file. The model is the owner's Competitive Analysis 2 table.
6. **Gaps and value opportunities:** measured against the problem sizes, the use case counts and the market opportunity verdicts (for example, segment 10 is the strongest unmet need, and segment 12 is the biggest need but crowded).
