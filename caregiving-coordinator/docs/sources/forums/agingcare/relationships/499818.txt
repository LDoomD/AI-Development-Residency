=== COMMENT 3 (2026-10-21T09:03:30.773-0400) ===
If there was a Restraining/Protective Order against you, you would have been served with court papers by a marshal to appear in court. Judges don't just grant those because a family member wants to be nasty to another family member. Also, the person seeking a Restraining/Protective Order against someone has to convince the court of why they want it. A sibling power-trip and it sounds like your sister is on one, really isn't grounds to get a judge to sign off.

Most definitely you should talk to the Ombudsman about it. Yes, your sister as POA can prevent you from seeing your mother. I'd say go to the nursing home and visit her. If there's a problem and you're not allowed access, ask them why. Then go from there and contact your sister and the Ombudsman.

=== COMMENT 6 (2026-10-20T07:43:49.007-0400) ===
We get this issue a lot on this forum and it's always difficult to answer since we're only getting one side of the story. I'm not sure why your sister would think it's better for your Mom to be in a facility rather than being cared for by her own child. Nonetheless, your Mom assigned her as her PoA and not you... there may be a reason for this. 

If you want to see your Mom I would ask your sister for a supervised visit so that you can prove you're not doing anything to undermine the decision (assuming she truly is the PoA). 

Your other option is to consult an elder law attorney who will send a letter demanding your sister produce the PoA document. If she's actually the PoA then you don't have much recourse. If she isn't the PoA then your Mom needs a legal representative and she cognitively may be beyond being able to do this.
