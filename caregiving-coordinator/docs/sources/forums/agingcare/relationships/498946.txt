In Florida, an ex-wife's authority as your Power of Attorney (POA) automatically ends. [1, 2]
Under Florida Statutes Section 709.2109, the moment a petition for dissolution of marriage is filed with the clerk of court, the spouse's authority as an agent under a financial POA is legally terminated. Once the divorce is finalized, her rights are permanently revoked.

=== COMMENT 3 (2026-06-16T19:53:36.000-0400) ===
I have been granted POA in Florida by five people at various times. I have also granted my POA to others multiple times. Florida has strict rules about valid POA documents. First of all, the document has to be signed by the principal (the person granting it) in the presence of two competent adult witnesses. It must then be notarized by a notary public, who can sometimes serve as one of the witnesses. All of these people must be together at the time of signing, and they sign sequentially. The rules must be followed exactly. If they aren't, the POA is not valid. {Chapter 709, Florida Statutes}

Perhaps you have been misinformed about what happened.
