=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-14T18:05:41.860-0400) ===
My husband has 4 siblings. They were all from the same biological parents. Yet, DH always says, "we all had different parents." Why would he say such a thing? Because parents tend to treat their children differently, that's why. The troublemaker and instigator winds up being handled differently than the quiet, studious child who was no trouble at all. I saw it myself in my own two children. My son was hell on wheels. We treated him differently because he was always in trouble or cooking up trouble and giving us gray hairs prematurely. My poor daughter didn't get half the attention my son did, even though the attention he got from his father was negative most often. They can each say they had a different father.

Nowadays, my son has little to do with his father. My daughter is devoted to him. 

Do you see where I'm going with this? Your siblings don't want to help their mom for reasons you may not comprehend. Or they may feel sensitive to her new personality after the stroke. That's a very real thing you know. So many adult children simply cannot handle dealing with a parent who's changed cognitively. We view them as super heroes and now they're old and frail, babbling about nonsense and spitting food all over the place. Don't write that possibility off. Your sister helps people she has no emotional attachment to. That's a neck of a lot easier than helping a mother who is no longer your mom, mentally. You are obviously strong enough to withstand the job, but not everyone is.

I suggest you let go of the bitterness and anger and chalk this lack of helpfulness off to their inability to deal with mom. Their relationship with her is not the same as yours. And, even you are close to burn out now, after 4 long years with little help. 

Place mom in managed care so you can go back to your life and so she can be cared for 24/7 by teams of caregivers who work in shifts. Or, hire caregivers to come into the home and help you. Because if you break down, then what? Then mom goes into managed care anyway. 

Look at the situation realistically instead of emotionally. I was an only child who KNEW I was unable to care for my very elderly parents at home, one with a brain tumor and one with mobility issues and dementia. So I didn't. And they had autonomy in Assisted Living and I got to sleep with my husband every night, undisturbed and w/o having to change adult briefs and fight about showers. It worked out just fine.

Good luck to you.
