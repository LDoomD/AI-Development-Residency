=== COMMENT 10 (2026-08-04T18:02:31.000-0400) ===
The myth of how rewarding it is to be a caregiver comes from misguided AARP propaganda, TV commercials where people with expensive teeth smile and smile and smile at attractive gray-haired elders, and ads where old people are encouraged to come for a wine tasting at a castle in their town where they will get a free month's rent if they sign up before whenever. Happy, happy, happy all the time! and there are never any pix of dirty diapers, or panicky conversations about how we're going to get mom to her doctor appointment when she refuses to get in the car, or picking dad up from his 35th fall this month when you threw your back out picking him up last Thursday. So let's declare the truth: CAREGIVING IS NOT REWARDING. We do it because we love them (or maybe we hate them but they make us do it by promising their house) or because we can't figure any way out of it. Plus all families are not happy, all caregivers are not perfect, and we've been duped. The myths have caused depression and anxiety for lots of family caregivers. We feel that we have to live up to the cheery expectations. Speak up, speak out, and tell the truth. That's what I am doing right now.

=== COMMENT 17 (2026-08-01T12:16:28.000-0400) ===
I overwhelm easily so I end up taking things one at a time. 

If Mom does not or will not have enough money for her care in LTC, then you need to apply for Medicaid. In my State you should do this 60 to 90 days before the money runs out. I started in mid April with the application. The caseworker gave me a list of things he needed to complete the application. Mom had 20k left. I placed her in LTC on May 1st. Her 20k paid for May and June. By June I had emailed everything needed for the application to the caseworker. I confirmed this with him. July 1st Medicaid started. 

Yes, my Mom had a house that was up for sale. It must be sold at Market rate to satisfy Medicaid. If sold while she is on Medicaid, Medicaid stops, your spend down the house proceeds and then go back on Medicaid. Once my Mom was on Medicaid, I stopped paying her taxes, they are high where I live. I unplugged all her appliances. Had one light that came on at dark and went off at 11. I set her heat at 55. I was paying those bills so tried to keep them low. My Moms house did not sell while she was alive. Since she was only on Medicaid for 3 months, she owed them 6k so a lien was placed. With that lien the tax and water liens on the house, I was able to sell it for what I could get as long as I covered the liens which I did. The bills that I paid were reimbursed at the house closing.

You are not responsible to pay anything towards your mothers care. Her house, the realtor is handling the sale. Now, you clean it out, one room at a time. I stared by cleaning out trash first. Each room had 3 piles....keep, throw out and give away. Give away went to thrift shops and friends if they wanted something. I did tackle the kitchen first because of the food. 

You have to prioritize. I had a disabled nephew who lived with Mom. He could fend for himself so I was able to put him on the back burner while I concentated on Mom. It has to be done so you just keep going. When its all done it feels so good.
