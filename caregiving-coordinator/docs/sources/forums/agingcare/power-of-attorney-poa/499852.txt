=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-24T09:54:27.800-0400) ===
Your friend should go over to the PoA Uncle's house with the forms that allow him to resign from his PoA authority. Hopefully he won't be a jerk and will sign them. 

Then I would contact the county and start talking to them about getting him a court-assigned legal guardian, so she'll need to endure until this is able to happen (or call in APS to discuss).

Otherwise there is the "ER dump" strategy: you call 911 and tell the responders that he is suddenly not acting like himself and that he may have an infection but isn't cooperating with care. Do NOT mention cognitive impairment or dementia, as these are not medical emergencies and nothing can be done about it in the ER. 

Once he's there, ask to talk to the hospital social worker right away. Tell them he cannot come back into your home as you are not his caregiver and he no longer has a PoA and he is unsafe there. FYI your home is his legal residence unless you go through the eviction process. Depending on where you live (state and county) the hospital may try very aggressively to get you to take him back. Some hospitals have been known to but impaired elders into taxis and send them back. You need to impress upon the discharge planner that you are unable to care for him, he is not cooperative with care and is an "unsafe discharge". Do not bring him back to your house no matter what the hospital promises or threatens. Give them the name and contact info and address for the PoA Uncle and let them harrass him instead. Make sure they know you're not the PoA. Do NOT give them yours.

I had an ER do this to me with my 6'5" step FIL with Parkinsons who fell in a parking lot and was brought to the ER. I was at work 30 miles away with 3 kids in school and the Sheriff called me up and threatened me that he was going to drive my SFIL home and I'd better be there to let him in. You can't make this stuff up, sadly. But back then I didn't know about "unsafe discharge" or anything else or I would have never done what the Sheriff threatened. I was NOT the PoA. No one was. He refused to assign anyone. But the medical staff will latch on to anyone who looks in their direction. 

You may want to consider starting the eviction process just so they can't force him back in to your home. It will be rocky for a while until there's a permanent care solution in place for him. 

You can probably find the PoA resignation document online for the state in which that elder lives. Drive it over the the Uncle and demand he sign it and legally finalize it (whatever that takes, maybe needs to be notarized at a bank). 

I wish you success in getting through this mess.

=== COMMENT 8 (2026-10-23T20:57:08.520-0400) ===
Grandpa can cancel the existing POA and write a new one. That’s IF he is mentally competent to do it, and willing to do it. My suggestion would be for your friend to find a helpful lawyer. Lawyer’s take on ‘mental competence’ is normally quite low – ‘does he seem to understand what he is doing and agree with it?’.

Your friend could usefully contact the current POA, say that the intention is to cancel the POA appointing him and appoint a different POA. Contact IN WRITING and ask for a reply IN WRITING if the current POA has any problems with this. Keep copies of this (and of any reply). I’d recommend dating it and having it witnessed and signed by the witness. I’d find somewhere in it to record the “kill him” comment.

For the new POA, it would be best to have that signed in front of the lawyer, but if that is impossible it’s still worth doing. Pick the best time of day to ask Grandpa to sign it, and take the witness to him to make it easy.

A new more appropriate POA will make it possible for GP’s care to be kept in the family. If it can’t be done, your friend should probably contact the health authorities or the police, and have his care taken over by the state.
