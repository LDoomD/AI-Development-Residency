Yes, a Personal Care Assistant (PCA) can hold a [member], but it is heavily regulated and can present significant legal and ethical challenges.

How this is handled depends entirely on whether the PCA is a professional hire or a family member.

Scenario 1: Professional or Agency-Hired PCAs

If the PCA is a professional hired through an agency or acting as an independent contractor, they generally cannot or should not be the POA. [

Conflict of Interest: Most professional care agencies explicitly forbid employees from accepting fiduciary or legal roles like a POA. It creates a massive conflict of interest where the person providing the care is also managing the money that pays for that care. [1, 2, 3]

Exploitation Risks: Courts and financial institutions closely scrutinize situations where a paid, non-family caregiver is granted a POA. If it looks like undue influence was used to get a vulnerable person to sign over control, the POA can be legally challenged and invalidated. [1, 2, 3, 4]

Scenario 2: Family Members Acting as PCAs

If the PCA is an adult child or relative who is acting as the primary caregiver, it is very common and completely legal for them to hold the POA. 

Personal Care Agreements (PCA Contracts): Often, families will draft a formal contract called a Personal Care Agreement (also abbreviated as a PCA). This contract allows the family caregiver to be paid legally out of the elder’s funds without violating Medicaid look-back rules. [1]

Signing the Agreement: If the care recipient is still mentally competent, they must sign the PCA contract themselves. If they are already incapacitated, a family member who already holds the financial POA may have the authority to sign the contract on their behalf—essentially hiring themselves to be the caregiver. 

Critical Precautions for Caregivers with POA

If you are acting as both the PCA (caregiver) and the POA (legal decision-maker), you must take steps to protect yourself from allegations of financial elder abuse:

Keep Meticulous Records: Log every hour worked, care tasks performed, and keep every single receipt for expenses paid out of the principal's accounts. 

Charge Market Rate: Do not overcharge. The pay rate specified in the Personal Care Agreement must match local averages for professional caregivers. 

Be Transparent: Share the arrangement openly with other family members or siblings to ensure there are no surprises or accusations of hidden asset transfers.
