=== POST ===
Most days the aide comes while I am at work, and by evening I realize I honestly do not know how the visit went. A quick "everything is fine" does not tell me much. I feel guilty double-checking on people, but I feel worse not knowing.
How do the rest of you handle this? Do you call your parent, text the aide, keep a notebook at the house, check cameras? And has there been a time you found out a visit did not happen the way you thought it did? I would love to hear how other people figured that out.

=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-24T20:19:38.500-0400) ===
A visit log kept at the house worked for us. A simple sheet on the fridge or in a notebook: date, time in, time out, what was done (meal, bath, walk), anything unusual, aide's initials. Ask the aide to fill it in before leaving, and add a line for the parent to initial if they can. You then read a page instead of asking "how did it go?", and gaps show up fast. Keep the agency's phone number on the same sheet so a missed visit gets a call the same day, not a week later.

=== COMMENT 3 (2026-10-23T01:15:25.467-0400) ===
We started with the notebook system in which the caregivers logged their notes at the end of the shift. The problem was it could be tough for me to read the writing.

We shifted to an electronic note system. I had an old cell phone and loaded the free care Plan app to the cell phone.. the caregiver logged their notes to the Care Plan app. I could login from my house to read the notes. They could also read notes from prior shifts.

We use this system for years. It worked really well.. if I left town for a week I could login and read the notes each day.

The caregivers took pride in their notes. Most were CNA‘s and were used to already doing this on their other jobs at skilled nursing facilities or [member].

=== COMMENT 6 (2026-10-22T09:43:14.807-0400) ===
Homecare agencies use an app installed on the caregiver's phone which also has GPS. The caregivers check in when they arrive at the client's home and they check out when they leave. I have a homecare agency so I know where my employees are at all times during work hours. If you're using an agency, you can call the office to 'check up' on the aide any time and they will. How I do things, is if an aide has to leave early for some reason or there's a situation with the client and they feel they must leave, they are to call it in. They receive full pay for the shift even if they leave.

If you hired privately, that's a different story. You could install a doorbell camera on the doors. I have a professional aquaintance in the homecare business. She sent a caregiver onto a job at a home that had two doorbell cams front and back. The aide would come in through the front door. The client was invalid from dementia. She would change and wash them then feed them breakfast. She'd exit the house through the kitchen window when she was finished and go to her other caregiver job. Her shift was six hours five days a week. She was working about 90 minutes five days a week. She got back in the house the same way and would leave through the front door when her shift was over.

 She claimed she didn't have a car, and for that client she didn't need one. She had a car and would park it on the street out of range of the doorbell cam. This went on for several months. One of the neighbors came by for a visit and was ringing the back doorbell and no one was answering. The neighbor literally saw the aide climbing in the kitchen window. She called the police because she didn't know who this was. Cops came and that aide was fired. There are ways to get around doorbell cams.

If the person has a landline and many older people so, you could have the aide checking in with you from the landline. You could insist she check in every half hour from the landline too if she's there all day. 

If the aide is through a homecare agency, it's their responsibility to keep her in line and properly doing the job. The boss or a supervisor is supposed to make regular visits to a clients' homes to make sure everything is running smoothly. If you're using an agency, go down there and talk to a supervisor. Tell them your concerns. 

Ask the aide to write a few lines in a notebook on how the day went. Don't expect nurses' notes. If the aide tells you everything is fine and you have no reason to believe otherwise, believe it. If you think something isn't right, call it in right away. Tell the caregiver too.

=== COMMENT 10 (2026-10-22T02:13:21.710-0400) ===
If you just want to be sure they are showing up on time and staying for the contracted time, get a ring camera or something similar. These are not traditional “hidden” cameras, they are very commonplace today, and you can set up an alert whenever it is triggered or check the feed daily. If you are worried they are showing up but not doing the job, I would use a camera which you can choose to hide or not. I have one now in my mom’s apartment facing the door so I can see when the aides come and go (she’s in [member]) and I can report to a supervisor if they don’t show up as scheduled to help her in the morning or at bedtime. It is motion triggered and frankly I don’t have time to watch every clip; I delete each day after checking the times aides are expected in the mornings and evenings. Compliance has improved. And no, I didn’t tell anyone I was placing a camera. It’s right there on her shelf. I don’t micromanage the aides but if they don’t show up at all, we shouldn’t be charged for that visit.

=== COMMENT 11 (2026-10-22T00:00:15.917-0400) ===
This is one of the reasons I did not return to work, but quit my job to stay home and care for my husband full time. I was worried about counting on an aide coming every day and being reliable.
I'm usually home when the aide is here, and sometimes we work together as a team, but I had an aide a couple years ago who I knew was lazy and avoided doing work, and I found that when I was gone for a few hours at a time, she had nothing to hold her accountable, she didn't do anything at all. I could tell because when I got home, things weren't done, or she was just starting the usual tasks that should have been done hours earlier. I thought to myself, "what have you been doing all this time?"

For me, personally, I would use cameras focused on where your parent usually is, like in the living room and one in the bedroom (or kitchen). But, you would have to let the aide know that there are cameras and you may be checking in on them occasionally. 

I like your idea of keeping a notebook. That is always a good way for family members and care providers to keep everyone informed, also good for tracking the person's health, diet, medications, activities, mood and bowel movements.

There can be a lot of "down time" in a job like this, and I think there are a lot of aides who really don't want to do anything but sit, watch TV, and babysit. This job draws people who don't want the structure and accountability of a 9 to 5, and they think this will be easier. Not saying there aren't some caring, hard working care providers, but I think this job appeals to people looking for a way to "coast".
