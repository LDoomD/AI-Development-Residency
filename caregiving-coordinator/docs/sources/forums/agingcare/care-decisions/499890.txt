I have a very good grasp on what financial situation my dad is in. Been handling his affairs for several years now. I am an only child, all this falls on me and I am good with things when I know where to turn. I need direction on this and would greatly appreciate any and all opinions and comments.
The basics:
My dad is 85 and lives alone. I, along with his doctor, believe he should not be living alone and should be somewhere with social interaction, good food and stuff to do. He has taken to sleeping most of the day. I have a girl come in 2x per week for a couple hours each time, but that is not enough.
He does not own his home. It is in an irrevocable asset protection trust. I hate that my parents created this trust 18 years ago, but that is water under the bridge. I have to work with what IS.
He is not a veteran.
He has an annuity (RMD taken about 5K annually), $26K IRA, long term care insurance policies that will pay about $2K per month for 50 months, approx.. $60K in the bank, a couple life insurance policies (Cash value about $50K). His social security is $2,900 per month.
I visited a personal care home yesterday that I really like... I actually said I would move in myself and help take care of the older people. Anyway, I won't know the exact pricing until my dad is assessed, but a quick figure on what pricing they gave me, it will be between $5-$5,500k per month for the care that he needs now.
Who is the best person to help me with his financials and what I can do with them to provide a stream of income, so I can see what he can afford and for how long?
Accountant, lawyer, financial professional or all three? I do have a client-attorney relationship established with an elder law attorney, but I don't know if they can help with financial things to give me the guidance I need.
I just need a little direction.
Thank you in advance.

=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-29T17:31:50.257-0400) ===
A financial planner, a fiduciary, is the person you want to turn to now for help planning your Dad's financial future and how to best manage his expenses. 

It wouldn't hurt to have the elder law attorney review everything, just to give their take. In particular, regarding the irrevocable trust. What happens to the house when your father moves, or when he dies? Your parents were planning ahead, and apparently wanted to protect the house. That was smart. If Dad's money runs out, you can help him apply for Medicaid assistance.

=== COMMENT 2 (2026-10-29T14:34:50.700-0400) ===
Your dad would benefit from a fee-only Certified Financial Planner (CFP) who specializes in retirement income and long-term care planning. Look for someone who charges an hourly or flat fee to evaluate his finances and develop a plan, rather than someone primarily interested in selling investments.

The goal should be to determine how much monthly income he can reliably generate, how long his savings will last at different [member] costs, and what happens when his long-term care insurance benefits run out.

Because his house is in an irrevocable trust, an elder law attorney should also review the trust before any financial decisions are made. The trust provisions and state Medicaid rules could significantly affect his options.

You can find fee-only planners at NAPFA.org and elder law attorneys at NAELA.org.

=== COMMENT 4 (2026-10-29T12:30:34.850-0400) ===
Financial planners (which by the way there is no set standard to call yourself a financial planner) make money by selling you products (investments) so they’re unlikely to be much help to you. You might look into a fiduciary to help you strategize how best to make his money last as long as possible. A fiduciary will
be fee-based, mine charges a percentage of the total assets per year, so all the advice they give me and strategies they suggest are based on helping me achieve my goals. You might look around your area and see if there are any.
