=== POST ===
My 92-year-old brother lives in Nevada. I live in California. I am trying to help him with his finances and need some advice. He does not have a computer and has never used one. His wife always handled the finances but now she suffers from dementia. I recently visited them and discovered a bunch of financial problems including false credit card charges, auto payments on the checking account which included his credit card, etc. I helped a bit with cancelling one credit card, transferring some funds out of the checking account etc. I am trying to see, going forward, how to help him manage his finances which would include checking his credit card expenses to make sure they are valid, reconciling his checking account and keeping track of when he needs to renew his CDs, pay his home and car insurance etc. He has good cash flow from social security and his pensions after many years of hard work. He is still very independent, otherwise.I am thinking that perhaps hiring a bookkeeper would be a solution. However, I am concerned about how much I can trust an outside bookkeeper not to misuse the accounts. Note: my brother, for now, is lucid and takes care of himself. He drives. He just always worked (physical labor), never used a computer and never dealt with his finances. Any suggestions or opinions?

=== COMMENT 1 (2026-09-30T17:04:10.717-0400) ===
You don't need a bookkeeper to do any of that.

You can be added to your brother's bank accounts as a joint owner even if you aren't his PoA (you will need to do this in person). Once you do this, you open an online portal and manage everything digitally. You can actually pay expenses with bank-generated checks using his bank's online Bill Pay feature if you need to. But just have as many things as possible on auto withdrawal/auto pay.

Same with his cc: have him add you as an authorized user. Then open an online portal where you can see and manage everything. Have the cc paid automatically from his bank account. 

Have his SS and pension funds deposited automatically into his checking account. Keep most of his cash in the savings account and only the minimum to pay bills in the checking. 

Whatever institution the CDs are issued from, you can probably set up online access to those too (once you are his PoA). 

It would be a good idea for him to make you his durable PoA (and for his wife, too).

What is it that you think a bookkeeper will do that you can't?

=== COMMENT 2 (2026-09-30T16:38:00.390-0400) ===
If he doesn’t have a computer he has presumably never set up online access to his accounts. If you have his permission and cooperation you could bring your laptop the next time you visit and set up the online portals on your laptop. 

I’m sure there’s some legal gray area about how you’d be “pretending” to be him, but if he asks you to do it and provides you with the account numbers etc this seems ethical to me. This is the route I went with my mom’s finances, even though I actually have POA. I set up the online portals with her thanks and kept and eye on everything from the comfort of my own computer for years before I had to step in and make POA level changes.

Random piece of extra advice would be to help him put a freeze on his credit. This keeps any bad guys from taking out new credit cards or worse in his name.

=== COMMENT 4 (2026-09-26T21:01:46.817-0400) ===
I've known some retired female bank employees who had a steady income from taking care of the finances of elderly folks like your dad. Usually they had been tellers or clerks. They managed the checkbook, filed receipts, etc. They knew the elderly person because they'd served them when they came into the bank. It created a nice little retirement income for the retiree, and the elder already trusted them from dealing with them at the bank. Ask someone at your dad's bank if they know of anyone who does this.

=== COMMENT 5 (2026-09-26T13:48:14.083-0400) ===
Banks and other financial institutions give people online access to their accounts. You could have your brother give you his financial POA and online access to his accounts. This way you could check the accounts regularly to see what transactions have been done by the bookkeeper and that they are all legitimate, without having to spend the time doing the transactions yourself.
