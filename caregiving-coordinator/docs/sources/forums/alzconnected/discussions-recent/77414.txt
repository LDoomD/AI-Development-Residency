=== POST (2026-10-24T11:21:55+00:00) ===
I have always been a minimalist when it comes to medications. As my husband's mixed dementia got worse and his behavior became more aggressive I consented when his doctor suggested more drugs.  The drugs helped in that we were able to care for him without getting punched or kicked. 

The drugs likely shortened his life, but Alzheimer's and vascular dementia are both fatal and there is no upside to living longer in stages 6 and 7 and dying slower.  He didn't appear to be happy and seemed to be suffering greatly. 

I recently got beat up pretty badly on a forum on Facebook for suggesting that a medication adjustment might help with a loved ones behavior problems . The group is for people who have loved ones with dementia. The comments I received were pretty mean suggesting that "drugging" him was wrong. 

It is true drugs can be "chemical restraints", but it is also true that we need to keep people with dementia safe, clean and fed. Sometimes none of those things are possible without medications.

I'll never comment on that forum again.  Don't judge me. Walk a mile in my shoes before you tell me what to do. I did the very best that I could when it came to caring for my DH.

=== COMMENT 17 (2026-10-26T04:55:18+00:00) ===
My DW was not mean or aggressive, but she became increasingly confused, agitated and anxious, along with delusions and hallucinations. I agreed with her neurologist that some medications that would calm her would essentially improve the quality of her life for the time she had left, even if this meant she might be mildly “chemically restrained“. Why would we want our LO’s to suffer?

In other cases, I declined drugs that might treat other issues she was having when those drugs made her miserable. In late Stage 6 she began to have silent seizures and was prescribed Keppra. This drug made her very morose (a known side effect) and in discussions with her neurologist I thought it would be kinder to discontinue the drug and give her a more pleasant life despite the possible affects of the silent seizures, which in the end, didn’t seem to be of any consequence. She passed away in very late Stage 7f and I have no regrets about the medications she took or those that were withheld.
