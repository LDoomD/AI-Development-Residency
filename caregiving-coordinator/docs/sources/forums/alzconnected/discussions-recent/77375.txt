The time has come. Wednesday I’ll drive to the best possible memory care I could find that’s also commutable, and I’ll walk in with my DW of 43 years. I know I’ll walk out and she will stay.  She’ll be angry with me, eventually she’ll forget that she is.  But will she remember the love we have, and the humor between us, at least for a little while before she disappears completely? I’ve been getting her room ready for the last month, and very lucky, four best friends have been helping me.  I was there the other day and had to find a caregiver to let me out. She was running a bingo game, and one of the residents responded to me by saying “ once you’re in here honey, you can get out” and then she chuckled and cracked a wry smile. My DW will like her.  I’m glad there are some higher functioning residents there, meaning there’s a chance for my DW to connect with others.  I am sick and worn out (Lupus, vestibular migraines with chronic vertigo and dizziness, low blood pressure…. Louise Hay would have a hay day)  and I’m doing what I have to do to stay in this game.  I am grateful we have the resources to choose this option, as so many are not able to. So utterly unfair! My heart is broken in so many ways. Though I’ve been hard pressed to find time to post much here, I’ve learned more from the collective experience, wisdom, and humor here than any other source of support I’ve had over these past years. I thank one and all for making this what it is. Extraordinary.  Thank You and please wish us well on Wednesday🙏🏽.  Talk with you soon Gi!                                      Karen

=== COMMENT 2 (2026-10-19T15:14:51+00:00) ===
All the best to you and your DW on Wednesday. 

I am glad you have found support on this forum.  

Memory care is a new turn on the journey, and the caregiving does not end when you LO is placed. It just changes. You will worry, maybe a lot, but it is normal. You will feel moments of relief too, and it is OK to then just breathe.   

One thing I wanted to mention that nobody told me is that your DW's experience in memory care will be greatly impacted by your presence there and interaction with the staff. They need to know someone is looking over their shoulder. When you visit your DW, take notes of things that you question or find concerning. Schedule routine (monthly?) care meetings with the staff and raise the items you noted earlier at those routine/scheduled meetings. Cross them off your list once you speak to staff about them. Follow up afterward with the staff if you do not see they got tended to. 

It seems to me that the staff mostly did just enough to "get by" until I pressed them on things. My standards were certainly NOT the same as the caregiver's standards. Sometimes the staff seemed a bit irritated with me, but I remained polite and kept at it.  

You will learn as you go and adapt and probably keep tweaking things here and there. I am so glad you have the support of friends.
