Hi Mona, and welcome to the best support for the worst disease. I was your age, when my dh was diagnosed, and it was difficult. At some point I had to decide whether to quit my job and care for him full-time, or place him and continue working. I knew if I quit my job I would be miserable and it would not change the trajectory of the disease. I would also find myself in my early 60s with no job, no current experience, and no contacts. 

I chose to place him in memory care and continue to work. Others have chosen differently, but this was the best choice for us. He was a very social guy and was happier in MC than he would have been at home with me all the time. I was 63 when he died, although older onset typically moves more slowly.

While he is able, make sure to get legal affairs in order if this is not already done: Durable Power of Attorney and POA for Healthcare, and wills. That is the critical time-sensitive step while he is still able to sign.

Come often, read a lot, and ask the questions you have. There is someone here that has been through anything.

Welcome. I am sorry that your husband has been diagnosed with Alzheimer’s. I am giving you the best advice I was given. As others have stated, consult with an Elder Law attorney immediately to get your legal documents in order while your husband can still sign. Get HIPPA forms signed with all of his doctors. Get a notebook and take notes of everything you learn here and from making calls. Go to all doctor appointments with your husband and take notes in this notebook. Note new symptoms and behaviors to share with his doctors on a portal or private note. Do not talk about it in front of your husband. His brain is broken. His reality is not yours. You will need to take over all finances. Get a list of all accounts with log in and passwords. Do the same for bank and brokerage accounts, computer, phone. If he is still driving, call your insurance company and ask if an accident will be covered with the diagnosis. Most won’t leaving you open to getting sued for everything you own. Buy TILE gps trackers and place on wallet, keys, phone. Buy the book The 36 Hour Day. It will help you prepare for what is coming next. Call your local Council for the Aging and ask what is available including adult day care. This is especially important since you are still working. Call local in home care agencies and get information and pricing. At some point, your husband will not be able to be left alone. Tour assisted living/memory care facilities and put him on a waitlist with a refundable deposit in the event you can no longer care for him at home. Travel and make as many memories as possible now. Come back here for support and advise. The people here are extremely supportive and knowledgeable.
