My wife has advanced ALZ.  She is reasonably mobile but in severe cognitive decline.  She still recognizes me and depends on me for everything.  She has no interests, can't read, can't follow a movie plot, can't play games, is not conversant and grunts and points instead of speaking.  She needs to be doing something all the time and expects me to provide her with something to do.  I take her on walks several times a day and we say nothing.  It's driving me crazy.   I have her in adult daycare 6 hours/day 5 days/week.  I couldn't survive without this help but it's not enough.  I can't believe I am the only one with this problem.  How are others handling it.  I would love to hear from you.

=== COMMENT 9 (2026-10-13T12:49:16+00:00) ===
I think this is another "symptom" of this disease that most people, other than caregivers, don't recognize. It hits me harder than my DW. The entertainment and engagement effort is exhausting to me. The other work of caregiving is frustrating and difficult also, bathing, toileting, feeding, wet bedding, on and on. It however has specific goals I can aim for and find a method that will work. I know when each task is done with or without DWs help. This engagement effort is just endless and DW will not participate in anything anymore. She is at a stage now where if I am not directly speaking to her or interacting directly with her she just sits there zoning out. When I can keep her involved the light comes back in her eyes and for short periods she is more aware. It's exhausting, but I guess it's the cost to keep the lights on.
