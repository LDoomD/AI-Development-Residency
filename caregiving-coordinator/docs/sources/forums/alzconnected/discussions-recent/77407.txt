This is a very difficult phase to navigate. I always rule out any medical causes for behavior - UTi, other infection, etc. before contemplating any medication. Behavioral approaches first too like you mentioned. Antipsychotics are a medicine of last resort for me. They carry many, many side effects, can worsen cognitive issues, etc. When behaviors were becoming an issue for my mother, I requested alternatives to antipsychotics. My mother did well for years without taking antipsychotics. She did eventually need them at one point, so the least amount needed was used. Also, you may want to consult a geriatric psychiatrist who may better be able to manage the intricacies. All medicine - antipsychotics in particular - need to be handled very carefully. In my opinion, there's no reason for the person to suffer, so if medication can alleviate that, I have no issues with it. The only regret I have is having to use a medication that had undesirable side effects because other medication wasn't effective. Unfortunately, there isn't always a good fit. 

Taking medication wasn't an issue for us but, as mentioned, if the pill can be crushed, it's helpful. 

The right aide would work with you as you're dealing with behavioral issues but they are few and far between. You would need to be upfront with the agency so the caregiver knows what they are walking into. The caregivers will not deal with anyone who may harm they though. So, if that's an issue, you may need to wait. From what you have described, it doesn't appear your wife is safe to be left alone anymore.

I wish you the best of luck.

Beachwalker, I have been taking montelukast multiple times a day for the last 10 years. One effect has been that I don't have the anxiety and agitation that I was experiencing before I started taking it. Montelukast lowers chronic inflammation (also chronic neuroinflammation). Because it has a short half-life of about 4 hours, it requires at least 10 mg twice a day.

Montelukast is a cheap generic available drug and you can buy it without a prescription across the border in Mexico. I live outside the US and I don't have any problem buying it.

I just went to a doctor a few weeks to get a treatment for a skin fungus infection (common where I live) and he had a liver function test done to see if I could tolerate a certain oral medication. The doctor said my CR protein test came back very low indicating very low chronic inflammation. He said it is unusual to see a number that low for a person of my age group. Your DW appears to be about the same stage as mine. According to the DBAT (Dementia Behaviorial Assessment Tool) she is a solid stage 5 with all but a couple of boxes checked. Mine completed Kisunla a couple of months ago with plaque fully cleared but the disease has not abated. I use the DBAT to track the disease and date each box when I check it. I have checked a couple of more boxes since the infusions were finished. I send an updated DBAT to the neurologist ahead of each appointment.

While my DW's mind is lost, she is physically capable (golf and pickleball)  with no balance problems so I have been very cautious in agreeing to any drugs which can affect her balance. She has been on Donapazil for 3 years. She suffers from depression and I agreed to start her on the lowest 25mg dose of Zoloft a few months ago which she has tolerated well. She has always taken vitamins and the generics for cholesterol and thyroid so I just added the very tiny pill to the morning pills with no comment and no questions. 

Her neurologist is suggesting we double the Zoloft to 50mg and add Memantine. Both can affect balance. I suggest reading the side effects of any drug very carefully and weighing them against the patients' strength and weaknesses.

We live on a high floor in an independent living complex so wandering is less of a concern than it would be if we were in a SF home. Caregivers are readily available here and day care with transportation is nearby. My DW will not consent to either so that is a remaining hurdle. I'm trying to get help on dealing with that issue.
