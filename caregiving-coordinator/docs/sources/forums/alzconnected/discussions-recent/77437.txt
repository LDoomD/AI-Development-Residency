=== COMMENT 3 (2026-10-27T19:32:13+00:00) ===
Taking away driving privileges from our LO’s as dementia progresses is definitely the hardest issue I see discussed on this forum (with the possible exception of end-of-life issues). More than one member has left us in a huff when forum members suggested or even insisted that their LO should no longer be driving. 

My opinion is that driving by PWD should be stopped as soon as dementia becomes evident. Any number of arguments can be raised in support of this.  For example: Your insurance likely will not cover an accident if there is a dementia diagnosis.  The PWD has reduced cognitive abilities, reduced spatial awareness, reduced reaction time.  Getting sued can result in a court case many months later when the PWD has progressed and will appear even more incapable of safely driving. The list goes on.

It can be difficult and cause anger and significant pushback from the PWD. This issue is a major problem particularly for men. It often helps to blame someone else like a doctor for the need to take away driving privileges. A doctor can also help by providing medication to reduce any anger that results.

Caring for our LO’s with dementia absolutely sucks but it is our job to keep them safe and to prevent them from potentially harming others. This is one of the most difficult decision you will have make as a caregiver. I’m sorry that this decision falls on you.

=== COMMENT 12 (2026-10-28T12:08:55+00:00) ===
@[member] 

Hi and welcome. I am sorry for your reason to be here but pleased you found this place.

The driving piece is hard. It seems to be especially hard for men for whom driving is intrinsically linked to independence, agency and masculinity. It was hard for my dad who had been a real gear head his entire life. This was the hardest part of dementia for all of us. The very last conversation I had with dad, hours before he died, was about me promising to bring him his car (at the MCF) in case he wanted to go somewhere hours before he died.

The driving issue is something of a "unity horse" here and folks— me included— tend to project our own experiences. It's hard, because so often doctors will initially diagnose a PWD with MCI (even when they seem to be more impaired than that) before "upgrading" it to Alzheimer's or some other dementia 6-12 month later. So he might meet the criteria for dementia and be unsafe to drive now. 

His driving behavior sounds unsafe now. My dad did this, too. He'd slow down randomly to give himself time to process situations and make decisions. Later, his loss of empathy led him to miss or not care about what another driver's next move might be. Dad's spatial reasoning was bad too. His car was banged up from him having several parking mishaps including 2 garage doors he damaged. It was terrifying. There are specially trained OTs who do driving evaluations which could provide more information in making decisions. Caveats around this are that 1) they're not covered by insurance and 2) they're a snapshot in time in a progressive disease. 

Another concern is his insurance. Many policies are voided by driving AMA or if there is a history of dementia or cognitive impairment in his medical record. If he were to have an accident and was sued, this could cost you everything you own. There can also be a civil liability for his POA for allowing him to drive impaired. My mom, who has vision issues not dementia, was sued after an accident. The insurance company represented her and covered costs, but the experience dragged on for a couple years (during which she aged into a very frail looking octogenarian) and was just awful all around.
