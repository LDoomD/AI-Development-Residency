Hi everyone! I (27F) have recently made the decision that my dear mama (62 w/Alzheimer’s) requires full on assisted living. I am now showering her, wiping her after bathroom visits, etc. I have been her full time caretaker for 5 years, and have come to the cross roads of continuing to care for mama, or continue my own life. I’m currently in school, and as hard as I try I can’t focus in school the way I need to. My grades are slipping not due to lack of care, in fact it’s the opposite I care so much! But my mom takes any and all energy I have. I am flooded with guilt and horrible thoughts of how heartbroken she is going to be. I already understand that it’s best to not give her a heads up, and that none of this is my fault. But the idea of leaving my mom somewhere and her begging me to not leave her, I get sick whenever I think about it. I don’t know how to cope with this, and I keep going back and forth saying she doesn’t need assisted living it’ll be fine i’ll just drop out of school again, or mama doesn’t need assisted living and I’m just being dramatic. I’m not sure how to handle all of these feelings that feel so big in my body.

=== COMMENT 3 (2026-10-26T17:43:35+00:00) ===
So sorry you are going through this. Assisted living still requires a fair amount of independence. From your description I wonder if memory care may be more appropriate. Some Al may accept people who need help showering and wiping, but they will probably charge you considerably more. My opinion is that Al staff is not as well trained for this kind of care even if they say they will provide it. They will do an evaluation before she is accepted, but I would still be very cautious to make sure they can give her the care she needs. Try not to feel guilty. I am guessing your mom (if she was able to think clearly) would want you to be able to finish college and live your life. Maybe look into counseling or talk with your doctor about medication to help with your anxiety/depression. Medication has helped me a lot. As others have said, you will still have a lot to do. Weekly visits, buying supplies and snacks, managing finances and coordinating care with doctors and nurses is a lot.
