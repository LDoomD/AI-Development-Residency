=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-24T04:46:12+00:00) ===
My DW began to do this in late Stage 6. She would try to pick up the flowers printed on a tablecloth or items in photos or book illustrations. She would also try to grasp things in the air that were actually well out of her reach, in some cases very far away. 

The lack of proper spatial and depth perception seems to be somewhat common in dementia patients, but not universal from my very limited encounters with others at my DW’s MCF. Many of the dementia patients there frequently saw dark floor mats at the doors as holes in the floor and did not want to step on them.

=== COMMENT 2 (2026-10-24T06:28:31+00:00) ===
Changes in vision and the ability to process visual information do happen in dementia. As well as the changes the previous poster mentioned you can also have peripheral vision loss (tunnel vision). These changes often lead to a greater fall risk.

I think PWDs sometimes have difficulty interpreting the images their eyes bring to them.  My wife and I visited a grocery store with such highly polished concrete floor that the overhead lights and roof supports were reflected in it, and it looked like a chasm.  She walked very gingerly where I led her, only because she trusted me more than her eyes.  We left and never returned.
