=== COMMENT 4 (2026-10-17T15:36:11+00:00) ===
I placed DH in a memory care facility in November, 2021. He was there 17 months before he passed in April, 2023. If there was anything “easy” about placing him, it was the fact that he did not know me, he did not know our kids, he did not know where he was most of the time, he was just here, but he wasn’t here. He was not aggressive, he was not incontinent, (I wouldn’t allow it),  he did not wander. I cared for him at home, 24/7, for 11 1/2 years. I investigated a memory care facility for a 30 day respite, just to  get a sense of what it was like. On the day that we finalized the plan for the 30 day respite, my three kids announced,  by way of their spokesperson , my giant retired Marine Colonel son, that after 30 days, we were not bringing pop home. They argued that I didn’t go anywhere, I didn’t get to see the grandkids’  sporting activities, concerts, activities; I did nothing but care for DH, forfeiting any semblance of a normal life. Although it saddened me, I knew in my heart that they were right and that if I carried on in this manner, I might come to resent DH and what his loss was doing to me and our family.

DH was well cared for, content, and well loved in the memory care facility. I was able to enjoy my grandkids again, and enjoy being his wife and not his caregiver. You will do what you need to do, when the time comes. And don’t worry about what anyone else thinks. I wish you well.💕

=== COMMENT 12 (2026-10-19T04:21:00+00:00) ===
I think there are a lot of reasons to move to MC and some of them depend on what kind of relationship you had in your marriage. My wife has been in MC for about 2 1/2 years now. It is a small unit with only about 14 residents. Of those there are 5 or 6 that were there when my wife moved in and the others have come and gone. Some have been very difficult when they first get there but adjust. Don't limit yourself to a preconceived idea as to when is right. There are too many variables and you can get over stressed with second guessing yourself. The only ones to seriously listen to are the ones that are there doing the work living the life of a caregiver.

=== COMMENT 17 (2026-10-23T21:50:00+00:00) ===
please have plans A & B ready just in case. After my cancer diagnosis I had no choice but to place my DH in memory care. I had not planned for that. Caring for someone with dementia is usually more than one person can handle. Soon they become incontinent and need 24/7 care. Unless you can hire caregivers, Memory Care facilities are better equipped to provide that care. Once placed, you are still the primary caregiver just in a different role. I made sure he was well cared for until he passed in August 2024.
