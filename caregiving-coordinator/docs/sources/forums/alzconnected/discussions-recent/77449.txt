=== POST (2026-10-29T19:02:20+00:00) ===
My wife, who has Alzheimer’s, is constantly saying she wants to go home (we are living at home), she doesn’t want to be here anymore, and she can’t do this anymore. I’ve tried telling her she’s safe here, asking her to tell me about home, and other tactics I’ve seen here and elsewhere, to no avail.

Does anyone have any other ideas?

=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-29T22:43:19+00:00) ===
Not sure what ideas I can add to what you already may have tried.  When my DW was really into this stage I would try excuses as to why she couldn’t leave.  I tried telling her that I couldn’t drive her, it was to dark, to early, to late, to close to dinner or whatever and I would take her first thing tomorrow.  Sometimes I would just take her for a ride to the store or call one of my kids to talk to her about staying.  I did assure her everyone knew where she was and that it was okay to stay.  I would even show her her clothes for added assurance.  It did seem to ease the anxiety about it…..for a while then we would start all over in a day or two.   Now, though it is not as bad, I still get her saying she can’t stay and has to go.  It seems easier to to distract her from the idea now.  It’s just a terrible disease.  Good luck.

=== COMMENT 2 (2026-10-29T22:50:34+00:00) ===
I think we have all been there. It’s so difficult when you are confronted with your LO saying “I want to go home” “I hate it here” and be prepared the next one is “who are you”. I found these conversations very confronting and for most of this time I was in tears, distraught. Others on this wonderful site will have their own solutions how to deal with this but mine is this. Your LO’s mind is breaking down, it is no longer capable of reasoning and working out why they feel like they do. It is very real and confusing to them. I found it is best to agree with whatever they say, be sympathetic “I can understand that”, “I don’t want to be here either” etc and that makes sense because nobody wants to be in this compromised situation. Then if possible use distraction “come and help me do the washing, plant a tree, water the garden” anything really that takes them away from the situation and gives them something else to do.

It’s a land of discovery, trial and error, hit and miss but done with love, care, gentleness and peace another world can exist on the other side. Good luck.

My DH (84 yrs old) still voices this a lot. Stage 6 and can’t talk very well these days, but still says almost daily that he needs to go home or needs to see his mother or his family or needs to “get down to the town.” It’s simple anxiety, and no amount of reasoning can comfort him. He is sadly still somewhat aware of what he has lost. 

Just this evening we were sitting on the back patio trying to have a conversation about mundane things and he said “I should just kill myself.” I’m not sure where that came from, he’s never said it before, but yes he is somewhat aware of what’s happened to him. My heart breaks, he was such a great intelligent guy, it’s so sad. 

I did last week get some CBD gummies (50 mg, that I cut in half) and give to him when needed, as a “vitamin” to ease his anxiety. I think it helps.
