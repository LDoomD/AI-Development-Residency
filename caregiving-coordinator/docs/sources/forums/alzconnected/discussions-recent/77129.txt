I am so grateful for this space. My mother came to live me and my husband 4 years ago. She is in the moderate stage of Alzheimer's dementia. I moved her from her beloved home and her independence in South Carolina. This is a humble business, and we've learned so much and have so much to learn.

We are entering a new stage that I would love some insight and practical advice. 

She has walked, "jogged", alone, up and down the street we live on every day and only recently, and on only one occasion (that we know of) gotten disoriented and lost. We know this because our neighbor reached out. Thank God for community. 

I initially spiraled, as I do whenever something seems to signal a new threat level and need for greater care. Can she no longer be left alone? Do I hire a caregiver? For how many hours per day? Is it really "that time"? Or. In this specific case, use a GPS tracker in the form of a watch that has a lock and can be set for a "green zone". Even though not perfect, is there some acceptable amount of risk?

=== COMMENT 3 (2026-09-18T04:37:49+00:00) ===
One gets used to their LO's voice. You've been able to gage ,usually, if they are not telling the truth etc.

That only works if their brain is functional- you really can't trust their recollections of where they were , what they did or when after a certain point. Before I knew what was going on I would get stories of what a store or repair shop had told my LO. I would call to "straightened it out" and WHOA totally different take on the events. My LO didn't understand or recall what had happened. Yet everything else seems "fine."

All a tracker does is tell you where they are- not if it is safe for them  as they cross streets etc.

=== COMMENT 8 (2026-10-22T00:08:48+00:00) ===
I think getting lost is only part of it. Some other things to consider- Is she remembering to watch for cars pulling out of driveways? Is she looking for cars before she crosses the road?  Is she able to pace herself so she has the stamina to get back to your house? If she were to fall does she carry a phone with her and would she know to call for help or would she be so flustered and confused she didn’t know what to do. It’s hard. You can only guess at the answers.
