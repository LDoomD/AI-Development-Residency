=== COMMENT 2 (2026-10-24T17:14:25+00:00) ===
Yes and no. It is never wrong for you to want your mom to make her own decisions. The condition is that she is able to make decisions independently. If she has cognitive loss, it is reasonable and often necessary for her caregiver/guardian to make decisions.

However, from your post, it's not clear whether there is cognitive loss. I guess we might be able to assume that if the guardianship has taken effect, that there must be some sort of clinical documentation to support taking decision-making away. Contradicting this is that she has been moved to assisted living where there is independence.

If you feel your mom is well cared for and well placed, I would suggest you show flexibility to arrange the field trips to close lunch places, i.e., don't rock the boat. Buy a bunch of yarn, bring it to lunch, have her choose, and then return the rest.

If you have concerns that your mom is not well placed, I would discuss with your attorney and perhaps they can ask your sister for documentation on mom's clinical status. I believe this could rock the boat. I'm not saying you should never do this; however, I would recommend picking and choosing where to challenge as this is a long journey and you don't want your mom to suffer from any of this.

=== COMMENT 6 (2026-10-24T21:16:36+00:00) ===
Iv never heard of a poa vs guardian situation. I can definitely see why an over night at your house might be a problem. Routine is so important to a person with dementia and an  overnight stay could disrupt that. While it might seem straightforward that someone in mid sage dementia should still be able to pick out yarn, I don’t think it’s a given. My mom is about stage 4. If I took her  to pick out yarn, she might know the kind she needs and the colors and all that. The problem is she would want to buy 10 things of yarn for a huge project  she would never be able to finish. Part of the problem might be her ability to actually do the project (she can’t thread her sewing machine, but believes she can sew like she did 29 years ago)but also because she has no ability to stick with something til it’s done. The next week she would want to pick out 5 yards of material for another project and the week after that she would want craft beads and glue. All these things would clutter up her living space (it happened ).  But every person with dementia is different. I would consider is she will be able to follow through and actually make something. Maybe buy 1 or 2 skeins and see how she does before getting enough for a big blanket. If you really think she has the ability to use the yarn, I think one of the above suggestions (let her pick it out on line, or buy several and take back what she doesn’t want). It sounds like your sister has all the power and you might not want to push your luck and end up with her cutting you off altogether (if that’s even possible). Yarn is not worth rocking the boat in my opinion. I have a difficult sibling and I know it can be incredibly tough.
