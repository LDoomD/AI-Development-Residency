Hi everyone,
 This is my first post. Recently, my mum was diagnosed with early-to-mid-stage Alzheimer's. She is 67. This has hit our family pretty hard, especially my dad, who has taken on the mantle of primary caregiver.

 I still live at home, so I am exposed to the daily realities of the condition. Lately, she has been suffering severely in the evenings with sundowning and hallucinations. For example, she will believe an ambulance is coming to take her away, or most recently she becomes convinced she can hear someone breaking into our car and starting the engine. This triggers a "loop" where she repeatedly says the same phrase over and over.

 We have tried several techniques to navigate these episodes, but recently, nothing seems to work:

 - Reassurance (telling her she is safe)
 - Validation (taking her outside to check the car and show her it's okay)
 - Distraction (changing the topic or moving to another room)

 If anyone has other suggestions, recommendations, or simply some words of advice, we would appreciate it so much.

 On a personal note, it is incredibly hard to see her regress daily. The reality is that her symptoms are progressing far quicker than our ability to learn how to care for her. I'd be lying if I said I wasn't scared of what the future holds if things keep moving at this rate.

Hello and welcome.  Yes, it is so hard to watch a LO decline like this.

Do tell her doc what is happening. She may need an adjustment on meds to calm down some. There are meds that won't cause a zombie effect. Also, if this new behavior is fairly sudden, also get her checked for any UTI.

=== COMMENT 7 (2026-10-29T12:55:47+00:00) ===
If you haven't already, have her checked for a UTI.  My mom (88YO…year 8 since diagnosis) has delusions, paranoia and agitation.  Like your mom, mine does not respond to distraction, validation or reassurance.  She  started a new anti psychotic (Rexulti) last month and it seems to be tampering down the symptoms.  It's not a magic pill but she has not had the daily meltdowns that she was having regularly.  She still loops relentlessly but she's more calm. I hate that medication seems to be the only thing that helps my mom but I know if it were me I would rather try medications than be confused, scared and angry all of the time.   Keep coming to this forum for support and information, it has been a life saver for me. Your dad would benefit as well.
