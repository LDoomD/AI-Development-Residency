I have a question about care in a nursing home.  I visited my 94 year old neighbor who has been in a nursing home for two months after living at home with 24 hour paid caregivers for a year and a half.  She had had a stroke leaving her paralyzed on her right side.  She had been speaking but now doesn't speak, but she will move her mouth as if she wants to speak.  She is also of low vision due to Macular degeneration and has hearing loss.  When I visited her, her paralyzed leg was on the floor and her body was scrunched down in the bed with her neck bent down.  Her gown was up and I could see that the diaper was wet.  I called an aide who repositioned her.  I was told that side rails are considered a restraint and are not used.  The aide said she would change her after lunch.  She had been eating soft food on her own with her goid hand but now is fed pureed food.  

I would like to suggest to her POA that she hire a personal attendant for at least eight hours for better care.  Do you think this would be doable in a nursing home?  BTW, my neighbor does not have dementia.  She is just a paralyzed senior.

Iris

=== COMMENT 2 (2026-11-01T19:37:46+00:00) ===
Did you visit her on the weekend? My DH is in a nursing home and the weekend staffing level is not as good as the weekdays. I have to visit him most days to make sure he is ok.

If your neighbor doesn’t have family to visit her and has the money then a hired aide would be doable in a NH.

=== COMMENT 3 (2026-11-01T19:56:36+00:00) ===
It’s possible she is in a nursing home because money ran out for in home care and she had to go on Medicaid. If this is the case, the cost of a caregiver for 8 hours a day would need to be covered by family. That’s not doable for a lot of families and if there are only one or two children it’s not as manageable as it might be if the cost could be split between 5 or 6 siblings. I would still let the family know what you noticed, but be careful. They may already be very upset about their inability to help her more. It’s a shame that nursing homes are not able to offer more/better care.
