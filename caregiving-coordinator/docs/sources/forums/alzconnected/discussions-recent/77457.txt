My (65f) wife (62f, early onset Alzheimer’s, diagnosed Feb 2026) told me I’m not a good caregiver, not like she was to her dad, who had Alzheimer’s. She is determined to stay independent and takes offense to any questions or reminders about medications and other normal daily questions or reminders. I’m backing off and steeling myself for possible sarcastic answers when I do feel I need to check in - like asking if she is having any reactions to a new anxiety med. I’m glad she’s focused on being independent, but the conversation was rough.

I found a new de-escalating measure that helped during this conversation - I sat on the floor, softened and slowed my voice, said little, and agreed. All while repeating to myself - her brain is broken. 

This changing relationship between us is so painful and frustrating. We just cannot get along, and I can’t find reactions or lack of reactions that work with her. She is going to move in with my sister and BIL between trips we are taking together. I’m trying to prioritize her comfort and at the same time keep myself sane and healthy. As I’ve mentioned before, she is opting for a Switzerland clinic for assisted death, so I am trying to embrace the fact that it really is all about her for now. 

I am taking some comfort in knowing that while we can’t change the past and fix previous actions or issues, we also can’t delete the past happy and fun events.

=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-30T23:59:33+00:00) ===
It sounds like she is not able to recognize her symptoms or limitations. Because of this she she thinks she is still able to do things herself, she resents any reminders or help. It’s tough to manage as a caregiver. In some instances you might try doing things for her giving her the impression you are just being a kind and caring husband trying to spoil her with your attention. In other situations you might claim you really find a particular chore (she is not capable of) relaxing or enjoyable. A pill dispenser might allow you to just check that rather than asking her (if you could get her to use it). Maybe if you tell her you will be using a pill dispenser she might be willing(even if you’re  just throwing a multivitamin in it). Subtle work arounds can  make a big difference, there just very hard to figure out.
