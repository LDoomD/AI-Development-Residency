Looking for other people who have very recently been diagnosed and are early in their Alz journey. In addition to first line therapies such as the new Anti Amyloid drugs, what else are people doing to stave off further cognitive decline aside from working out and challenging their brains. The two most promising things I have come across appear to be supplementing with 5mg of Lithium Orotate and the cholesterol lowering drugs Ezetimide (Zetia). In a retrospective study, people who were taking Zetia for lowering cholesterol, were 86% less likely to developed Alzheimer's related dementias.  Has anyone been taking these and if so has anyone seen any improvement in their beta amyloid biomarkers or Ptau biomarkers; or experienced any measurable cognitive improvement?  This is all very new stuff because the Zetia connection was only discovered in 2024 and the Lithium Orotate potential described in 2025. Both of these have the potential to be game changing because they address very fundamental Alz disease pathways and because the information is so new there is little to no track record in humans.

=== COMMENT 7 (2026-10-05T23:46:20+00:00) ===
Thanks to everyone for sharing your experiences. My husband was diagnosed in December 2024, and one thing I learned very quickly was that you sometimes have to be relentless in navigating the medical system.

I did not rely solely on our PCP’s referral. I asked for a general referral, kept the appointment that was months away, and then started calling neurology offices myself.

At one office, the scheduler told me they had no openings for several months. I asked to be placed on the cancellation list—and while we were still on the phone, a cancellation came in for the very next day!

So my encouragement is: be persistent, stay polite, and keep pushing.

A few things that may help:

Ask your PCP whether they can speak directly with a neurologist if symptoms are progressing quickly or there are safety concerns.

If possible, request referrals to several neurologists rather than waiting on only one office.

Keep the first appointment you can get, even if it is months away, and ask to be placed on every cancellation or wait list.

Call the specialist’s office after the referral is sent to confirm they received it and have everything they need.

Keep copies of medical records, labs, brain imaging, and cognitive testing so missing paperwork does not slow things down.

Be willing to consider another location, telehealth, or an initial appointment with a PA or NP if that can get you into the practice sooner.

Let schedulers know if you can come in on very short notice. Flexibility can make a big difference.

My husband began Kisunla treatment in June 2025 and is expected to finish in December. Kisunla has not been a silver bullet for us, and we have not seen the kind of improvement we prayed and hoped for YET. But we continue to move forward, stay informed, and take things one step at a time.

Most importantly, do not live this journey in fear or shame. Start identifying your support and care team early. Let trusted family and friends know what is happening and allow them to walk alongside you.

Stay positive. Stay connected. Keep asking questions. And keep advocating for yourself or the person you love. ❤️
