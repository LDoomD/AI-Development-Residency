=== COMMENT 1 (2026-10-25T14:33:47+00:00) ===
Hi MarshalL - welcome to 'here', but sorry for the reason.

There are others on here who definitely deal with the same. Regardless, you are not alone. 

Have you looked into adult daycare a few days or more a week? Also, check out some memory care centers that offer a week or two respite service. 

Have you checked with a CLEC (elder care lawyer). Because you should not have to use your own funds to pay for her care.

Also - do you have DPOA and HIPAA accesses? Important.

Way, way too often, siblings, other family, and even well-meaning friends have no clue of the burden the caregiver has! A lot have no desire to learn, either. I'm sorry you are dealing with it.

Welcome. When mom was first diagnosed I accepted that my brother was probably not going to be much help. Eventually I realized he was going to be no help at all. Finally I realized he was going to actively work against me to make the whole process even worse than it had to be. I now have very little contact with my brother. I expect him to help with nothing. To top it all off mom can be angry, mad and demanding with me, but my brother can do no wrong. It is wrong and so unfair, but life is not always fair. They are who they are and there is probably nothing you can say or do to change that. Stressing yourself by trying to get them to change is not worth it. 

From their prospective, in home care is a lot of work. If my sibling insisted on care in the home and I thought a facility was a better option I would not be ok with putting in excessive hours to make in home care work. 24/7 in home care while still working doesn’t seem sustainable. For most families it would be financially impossible. You deserve to have a life outside of work and caring for your mom. A facility offers a chance to make friends,  socialize and do activities that’s not available with in home care. Maybe your siblings are more aware of their limitations and boundaries. I’m just trying to give you a different point of view. Family dynamics are so complicated and I understand there is a lot here that I’m not aware of. 

Have legal matters been addressed? Are you DPOA? Have you looked into Medicaid? You might try contacting your local commission on aging to see what support services are available. Is it time to consider assisted living, memory care or a nursing home? Even with placement she will still depend on you for so much. So sorry you are going through this.

=== COMMENT 3 (2026-10-25T15:06:18+00:00) ===
Previous poster is wise. I similarly went through the no help and eventual working against.

It is important the legal poa be in place. If you are providing the care, I would think it would be you but it's uncertain from your post. 

Family is critical in a situation like yours. What you'll find though is that family may not end up being your biological siblings. Family may end up being your best friend from high school, the neighbor across the street,  the council on aging respite care worker who comes to your home free of charge, and/or the facilitator of your local dementia support group. 

You absolutely need to ask for help from your sisters. Provide options as direct care or grocery shopping may not be a fit for everyone. Assuming you have asked and there is no response or a hurtful one, move on. My mom has passed and after about ten years, I have forgiven my siblings but do not have a relationship with them as I won't open myself to being hurt yet again.

A support group will help a lot! I went in person when my mom was diagnosed and I had to move her closet to me.

Re: siblings - she lived out of state near family who my parents had lived close to for exactly this reason. They just couldn’t deal. I was furious, but also able to step up. None of family are close to each other at this point. We get by. 

I will say this: when I agreed to be primary caretaker of my mom I knew I had to do it willingly. My mom IS in a MC facility and it’s still a huge amount of work for me since I visit often. glad you found this site - I’m five years in and it’s been a lifesaver.
