=== POST (2026-10-17T22:48:30+00:00) ===
My DW is declining steadily, but slowly. I thought a year or so ago that she had hit a serious decline, but it was another "altitude drop" where things suddenly changed a bit but stabilized after a pattern of such things. She is in a fantastic MC that is run by our county, not a for-profit facility. She was originally in a for-profit, but the care level declined more rapidly than she did. When they started leaving her in her room all day in the same dirty clothes, I found another place as quickly as I could. She's been in the county facility for a little more than two years. She no longer recognizes me, cannot speak a coherent sentence, and has to eat chopped food. On the positive side, she is out of bed every day, has plenty of activities, and interacts with other residents to some degree.

I have no problem with her care, and I trust the facility. They call me at the slightest sign of difficulty, and I always attend the quarterly care conferences in person. My problem is me: I cannot seem to force myself to visit with her, and I couldn’t, even to save my life, say why. I have no idea. When I do go, it is generally at mealtimes because a) I can find her without staff having to look all over the large unit to see whose room she has wandered into and b) because I can interact more easily with her. The visits last about an hour, but she disconnects long before I leave. 

I love DW dearly and wouldn't consider seeing anyone else while she remains alive. I live alone with two dogs for company. I work from home and really only drive anywhere significant on Sundays. I often decide on a day to visit, but by the time I plan to go, I've convinced myself I need to take care of something important that cannot wait. I know I am lying to myself, but I just can't convince, guilt, or even force myself to go most days. The best I can come up with is that I don't think there is a point in visiting: she doesn't know who I am, and there's no communication. After a long struggle, she is now very happy with her circumstances.

Has anyone experienced anything like this, and if so, what did you do about it?

My DH is stage 6, still home with me, usually doesnt know I’m his wife but shadows me closely and knows I’m his “person.” And so my goal is to keep him with me until such time as he no longer knows who I am or who he is or where he is, and as such he would not be needing me or missing me if I wasn’t with him. 

Your DW may be at that stage now that she is well cared for, and has some activities, and is able to interact with the other residents to have some socialization, and so she doesn’t really know you or need you - and your visits are not adding to whatever enjoyment of life she still is capable of. 

So it may just be too hurtful to you to visit with her at this point in her disease, since she doesn’t need you and you add nothing positive to her current level of existence. 

I can see the same thing happening to me in my future when I must place my DH. Im sure it will be quite difficult to visit a man who used to be my husband for many decades, but is no longer that same person, doesn’t recognize me as anyone significant to him and has no need for me. This is a sad, cruel disease.

=== COMMENT 5 (2026-10-18T12:03:37+00:00) ===
Dear @[member] 

I am not in the same situation as you, so can only speak from my heart.  

It sounds to me that you have been an exceptional spouse and caregiver.  You found a ‘home’ where your DW is well cared for and is safe and content.  If it were me, I would take this respite time to re-group and and decide how you can be there for your DW when the time comes.  Take all the time you need and do not feel guilty for not visiting because you think that is the expectation.  Perhaps at the next quarterly care conference you can take a few moments to observe her interacting with the staff or other residents to assure yourself that she is safe and happy?  You will know when it is time to be present, and I think that will be when the visit is meaningful for both of you.

God bless you and be with you.
