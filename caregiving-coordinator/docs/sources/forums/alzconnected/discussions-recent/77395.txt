=== COMMENT 2 (2026-10-21T18:22:06+00:00) ===
If you are going to go the agency route, contact the agencies directly by phone. Call more than one. If you have a schedule that you would like to have an aide for, let them know it. Tell them you want one aide if possible. if not, no more than 2. Tell them you want consistency. They know this is important for someone with Alzheimer's. Also, tell them exactly what you want the aide to do. When the aide is selected, go over those responsibilities with the aide and agency both present.

Agencies do not normally send a different person all the time. However, they can't guarantee anything like they claim either. I've dealt with caregivers and agencies for many years. It is an unfortunate fact that they are unreliable. 

The agencies will tell you they match the aide to the person. They try but they send whoever they can get. I used to meet the caregivers before hiring. 

Do a search for home health agencies and review some websites. Pick a few that look good to you, have been in business a while, run all the appropriate background checks, etc. A few names that pop into my mind are Home Instead, Senior Helpers, Right at Home, Visiting Angels, etc. This will vary by your location. I wouldn't contact anyplace that will find agencies for you.

=== COMMENT 3 (2026-10-22T13:32:52+00:00) ===
The employee time an agency uses to schedule is non productive/non billable  time so they are just as interested as you in getting a steady good match. I've found that when a long term person quits or gets ill it typically takes trying 1-3 people for a good fit . 

But its priceless when you get a 9 am call and they say - XX is unavailable- they are sick, car trouble ,whatever — we can offer MM - do you want her/him today ? Better than radio silence .  And saves you the tax prep, background checks, workers comp insurance etc etc . 

"She knows not to use the stove or touch her medication unless I give it to her. Yet, our neurologist is insistent on me having her supervised 24/7. "  Sounds like your wife is in the stage where she sounds and acknowledges things normally like in the past BUT based on the Dr's opinion I think you maybe overestimating her memory and ability to process complex thoughts.  Their voices still sound like normal for a long time and us humans are optimistic sorts -we are wired to assume that things are fine behind that voice. Alas, the brain is slowly doing its erratic unpredictable decline . You can't rely on it anymore.

 Set things up for safety first [locked meds , stove locks etc] . A house fire or worse and then you'd be looking at MC for her and an insurance nightmare for you—and losing your home & its memories .

Agencies can often offer the same individual if 1) you are flexible about the schedule and 2) you can offer said individual the number of hours/times they're looking to work. A day program would likely be a great option— she'd get socialization at a more cost-effective price point.

IME, a lot of primary caregivers look at their LOs through loving eyes and tend to dwell on their PWD's "best". Mom certainly had rose-tinted goggles with my dad longer than she should have. It would never occur to him to cook or go for a walk. She once asked me to come to the house to answer questions of the tech crew installing a new HVAC system because she didn't want dad mixing it up with them. During the install, the techs warned us the smoke detectors would go off. Two minutes later they did. Dad sat for at least 20 seconds before asking what the noise was. I told him. After another 20+ seconds, he got up to look for mom forgetting she was out. He did not call 911 or ask me to. In would have died in a real fire.

=== COMMENT 5 (2026-10-22T17:58:28+00:00) ===
My only experience with an in home caregiver is through an agency but our first person has been exceptional. In our case the VA chose, and pays for, care through a local agency. I know they will provide a substitute if the need arises but we have the same person each week. We’re in a very rural area so I was worried about finding someone but my husband’s “person” lives just minutes from us. I had hesitated to bring someone in but it’s been a great experience so far. 

My husband doesn’t attempt to cook, hasn’t driven in years, and is physically able. My biggest concern was if he’d know what to do in an emergency. I’m much more at ease having someone with him. Having the agency handle payroll is a huge plus.
