Same here—I’m caring for my dad in the advanced stages of Alzheimer’s. Sadly, it became too overwhelming to manage at home. I tried in-home care, but he made it very challenging for them to do their jobs. He would yell and threaten to hit them—not because they were mistreating him (I had cameras everywhere), but because, unfortunately, 30–50% of Alzheimer’s patients experience behavioral issues such as unpredictable aggression or inappropriate behaviors. On top of that, my dad struggled with severe insomnia.

As his care needs grew, caring for him became a 24/7 responsibility. That was my breaking point and the reason I transitioned him to memory care—though that, too, has its challenges. Even now, caring for my dad remains a big part of my life.

Everyone’s journey with Alzheimer’s is different, and we shouldn’t judge the decisions families make. What matters most is being supportive and kind.

Tip: If you ever reach the difficult decision to move a loved one into memory care, I highly recommend choosing a facility dedicated solely to memory care, as they are often better equipped to handle the complexities of dementia.
