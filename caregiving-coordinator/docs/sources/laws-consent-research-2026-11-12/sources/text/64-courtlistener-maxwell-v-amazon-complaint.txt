 2          59.     The Federal Wiretap Act (“FWA”), as amended by the Electronic Communications
 3

 4
     wire, oral, or electronic communication.
 5
            60.     Under the FWA, no person may intentionally intercept, endeavor to intercept, or
 6

 7   procure “any wire, oral, or electronic communication.” 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(a). Similarly, it is

 8   unlawful for any person to intentionally disclose, or endeavor to disclose, to any other person or

 9   to intentionally use, or endeavor to use, the “contents of any wire, oral, or electronic
10   communication, knowing or having reason to know that” the communication was obtained in
11
     violation of the FWA. 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(c) & (d).
12
            61.     The FWA confers a private right of action for any person whose wire, oral, or
13
     electronic communication is intercepted, used, or disclosed in violation of the FWA. 18 U.S.C.
14

15   § 2520(a).

16          62.     The FWA defines “intercept” as “the aural or other acquisition of the contents of

17   any wire, electronic, or oral communication through the use of any electronic, mechanical, or other
18
     device.” 18 U.S.C. § 2510(4).
19
            63.     The FWA defines “electronic communication” as “any transfer of signs, signals,
20
     . . . data, or intelligence of any nature transmitted in whole or in part by a wire, radio,
21
     electromagnetic, photoelectronic or photooptical system that affects interstate or foreign
22

23   commerce.” 18 U.S.C. § 2510(12).

24          64.     The FWA defines “electronic, mechanical, or other device” as “any device or
25   apparatus which can be used to intercept a wire, oral, or electronic communication.” 18 U.S.C.
26
     § 2510(5).
