# Support groups, government programs and online communities: what caregivers say

Group: support groups, government programs, online communities. Competitive analysis 4, step "relief for worn-out caregivers".
Date: 2026-11-06. Written for the owner, plain English. Nothing here is decided from raw counts. Every mention in the table below was read, or the section says it was not.

How to read the evidence tags (skeptical-audit): **observed** = I read it in the saved text this session; **inferred** = I reasoned from observed facts; **documented** = an earlier saved file says it; **reported** = a caregiver wrote it (not checked against anything else); **not-assessed** = I did not check it.

## 1. The short answer

1. **The big names on your list barely show up as relief.** I read 756 mentions. Caregivers describe a real *break* in only a few places: the hospice respite stay (18 of 23 reports are about a break), PACE (12 reports), and a few IHSS reports (4).
2. **Most government-program talk is about getting paid, not resting.** Medicaid waiver, CDPAP and IHSS reports are 155 of the caregiver reports. They are about hours, pay, paperwork and denials. The biggest blocker is paperwork and eligibility rules. 48 of the 78 Medicaid-program reports are one event: New York moving CDPAP to a new payroll company (PPL) in March and April 2025. That is not "how caregivers feel about Medicaid waivers" in general.
3. **Support groups and online communities give "I'm not alone" and information, not a break.** Reddit and ALZConnected writers praise their own community (34 of 55 and 19 of 28 say it worked). That is circular: they are writing on the site. **Facebook groups are the only community type with mostly bad reports**: of 12 caregivers using a Facebook group for support, 2 said it worked, 4 mixed, 5 did not (judging, toxic tone, a relative in the same group). Another 6 used Facebook groups to find an aide, a lift or local help, and none got it.
4. **When the parent refuses, hospice respite fails.** In 5 of 23 hospice respite reports the person being cared for refused or would not go. That is need 4 on your list, and no program in this group solves it.
5. **Caregivers ask for peer groups and cannot find a fit.** 16 caregivers asked for a support group, 8 asked for a Discord and 4 for a WhatsApp group. Only 2 said they use a Discord server and 1 a WhatsApp group. The rest of the Discord and WhatsApp posts are organizers or researchers recruiting (12 and 28).
6. **Several names you listed have almost no caregiver evidence:** Family Caregiver Alliance (0 caregiver reports), Caregiver Action Network (0 posts), Savvy Caregiver (0), Lotsa Helping Hands (0 real), Cozi (0 real), Meal Train (1 used it; friends never followed through; 2 more asked), Well Spouse Association (1, an online event), Wellthy (2 asks, no users), Medicare GUIDE (3 mentions, no users).

## 6. Findings by candidate (what the reports say)

**Medicaid waiver / HCBS / paid family caregiver (incl. CDPAP)** (78 reports from 101 read; 255 raw hits, 155 not read)
- Used for: getting paid to care for a family member, or hours of paid help. 0 reports ask for rest.
- Top blockers (keyword groups): paperwork and system problems (38, mostly the NY PPL payroll switch), waitlists (9), eligibility (9), too few hours or low pay (5).
- Only 1 caregiver says it worked clearly. Example quote: "I'm currently working for CDPAP which has helped me a lot".
- 48 of the 78 are the NY CDPAP to PPL change, March and April 2025. 30 reports are about other waiver programs (Florida, Alabama, Kentucky, Iowa, South Carolina, Washington, New Mexico, Pennsylvania and others).
- Parent refused: 2 reports (`p1ib912j`, `p1i7k19o`): the parent signed up, then refused to sign or to let help in.

**IHSS (California)** (77 reports from 101 read, all hits read)
- Used for pay (73 of 77). 4 mention a break (2 worked).
- 18 of 39 "considering or asking" reports are denials: income too high, wrong residency, assets, "share of cost" (24 eligibility blockers in all).
- 24 hits are not caregiver use: custody-dispute posts about "IHSS fraud" (4), IHSS workers writing about clients (8), ads (2), duplicates (3) and passing mentions (7).
- Typical hours quoted: "2.5 hours a day", "about 2 hours a day", "30 hours a month". Several caregivers say the hours are too few to live on while they also cannot work another job.

**PACE** (41 reports from 62 read, all hits read)
- 19 of the 62 hits are one repeated weekly link-list post (not a caregiver story) and are excluded.
- Worked: quick clinic visits, found 3 days of respite for a sick spouse, day center and rides. Mixed or bad: only 3 contracted respite facilities (2 poor), age rule (a 61-year-old did not qualify), "PACE is only as good as the care team you are assigned", a facility not under contract.
- A2 (spouse) reports are 7 of 41 and 5 of them are positive or mixed. A1 reports are 22.

**Hospice respite** (23 reports from 27 read, all hits read)
- Used a break: 14 caregivers. 1 worked clearly, 3 mixed, 3 did not, 7 do not say. 9 are considering or asking: 5 had the person refuse or it was not available.
- Example of a good one: "I am glad they offered respite during the time on it." Example of a bad one: "Dad came back from respite Wednesday nonverbal and agitated."
- Guilt shows up (the caregiver "didn't take the offer until our social worker told me").

**Alzheimer's Association** (19 reports, 25 read, all hits read plus 3 found later)
- Used it for information (14 of 19). Helpline: 1 helpful (a peer counselor), 2 not (one had "the hardest time" explaining dementia, one was never told of a benefit), 1 only got a list of facilities.
- One Zoom group run by Association-trained facilitators: "I did and have found it to be invaluable." (spouse caregiver).
- The forum files are the ALZConnected site, so I counted only members who talk about the Association or its services, not the site's own boilerplate.

**Support groups, any sponsor** (37 of 41 read, 114 coded posts)
- Used and worked: 7 of 15 who used one. Did not work: 4 reports (hearing others' stories made one caregiver feel worse; one could not find a group; a hospital group where the writer was the only dad; one caregiver refuses to go, as reported by a relative).
- 16 caregivers are asking for a group. Their blockers: meeting times (4), group does not fit the illness or age (2), not knowing where to look.
- I only read 41 of the 114 support-group posts. The other 73 are covered only by the earlier reader's codes (documented, not re-read).

**Reddit communities** (sample of 100 from 959; 55 caregiver reports)
- 34 say the community helped, 2 did not (e.g., "y'all made me borderline suicidal"), 18 give no outcome. Feeling "not alone" is the common phrase.
- Circular: these writers are posting on the source we are studying. 8 of the 100 were app makers or researchers.
- 859 more posts match the filter and were not read.

**ALZConnected forum** (28 caregiver reports, the data source itself)
- 19 worked, 9 no outcome, 0 negative. Same circular limit as Reddit. It is a mix of "this forum saved me" and advice to newcomers.

**Facebook caregiver groups**: see section 1. 64 narrow hits, all read. 14 are people promoting their own group.

**Discord and WhatsApp**: 37 and 84 hits, all read. The real signal is **demand without supply**: 12 caregivers asked for a Discord or WhatsApp caregiver group. Examples: "We need a chat room or discord. Anyone know if one exists?" and a spouse asking "Anyone want to start a private WhatsApp group to vent/support each other?" One member wrote "I just joined yesterday and already enjoying it." WhatsApp is mostly used for family group chats (5 caregiver reports, 4 did not work, for example "which is seldom used").

## 11. Files

- `MENTIONS-READ.jsonl`: one line per mention read (756 lines). Fields: id, name, label, used_for, cared_for (and where that came from), outcome, blockers, quote, note (for labels 4 and 5), source, url, quote_checked.
- `SYNTHESIS.md`: this file.
- `scripts/`: the scripts I used (`corp.py` loads all saved items, `pats.py` and `partb.py` and `named.py` hold every filter, `chk.py` checks quotes, `compile.py` builds the file, `stats.py` and `build_md.py` build the tables). `decisions/` holds my label files (one per candidate, pipe-separated). They read from the saved sources by absolute path. Run `build_cache.py` first: it builds `U.pkl`, `packs.pkl`, `rel_ids.json` and `sg_ids.json` that the other scripts load. Run the scripts from one folder that holds the scripts and a `dec/` folder (copy `decisions/` to `dec/`). `rawhits.json` is made by the loop at the top of `partb.py`'s companion: run `python3 -c` over `pats.C` as in `build_cache.py`.
