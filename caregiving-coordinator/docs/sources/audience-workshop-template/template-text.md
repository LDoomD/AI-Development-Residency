## Instructions section (verbatim)
GOALS OF THE WORKSHOP: "Build semantic labels around audience segments. / Agree to the audience segment descriptions your team wants to align on. / Brainstorm motivations, goals, and scenarios to support that will be important to audience segments."
DELIVERABLES OF THE WORKSHOP: "Identify semantic labels for audience segments based on what you think are the common traits / Identify situational goals that are important to support in the release planning / Identify use case scenarios that are important to support in the release planning"
STEPS TO RUN THE WORKSHOP: "Identify potential traits of segments based on: Geography / Behavior / Demographics / Socioeconomic status. Pick the most impactful traits and describe your audiences you want to focus on. Identify the motivations of the audience segment. Identify the situational goals and life goals that audience segment has in the end-to-end experience. Identify the use case scenarios that are the most impactful to support for each audience segment."
WHO OWNS IT: "The Product Manager hat owns the audience segmentation that should be focused on. As teams do more research over time, the segmentation of audiences changes and evolves."
WHERE KEPT: "Keep it in Figjam, or some other place where you can keep it over time"
Segmentation definition box prompt: "describe the possible contributing segments about your target audiences that may play a role in your product. This may just be a guess, but it's important to document as you keep doing research so that you can further refine your intended unique value proposition."

## Audience block (one per audience; the template has two)
- "AUDIENCE 1 LABEL: ____ / AUDIENCE HAS THE FOLLOWING SEGMENT TRAITS:" with an example paragraph of traits.
- MOTIVATIONS: "What drives the audience to take action? What do they desire that determine their goals?" (sticky notes)
- SITUATIONAL GOALS: "What are the outcomes that people want to achieve in our experience based on their motivations?" (sticky notes)
- LIFE GOALS: "What are the outcomes that people want to achieve in their life based on their motivations?" (sticky notes)
- USE CASES THAT WE SHOULD SUPPORT FOR THIS AUDIENCE: a table with four columns:
  1. SCENARIO-BASED PROBLEM ASPECT: "Describe an aspect of a challenge for a user today"
  2. CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE: "Describe when this problem happens today"
  3. BUSINESS GOALS: "In what ways would you want this challenge to change? How would it be different?"
  4. FUTURE SCENARIOS TO SUPPORT: "Write a list of scenarios that we would need to support for the audience in order to change the experience so that people don't face the problems that they face today"

## Prioritization
- Method: "MoSCoW" (Must, Should, Could, Won't), on risk and certainty, or on effort and value.
- Instructions: "Take the problems you identified for each audience in the first part and place each problem into the appropriate category of priority."
- Matrix (effort and value): 1 Top Priority = low effort + high value; 2 High Priority = high effort + high value; 3 Medium Priority = low effort + low value; 4 Low Priority = high effort + low value.
- Part 3: "As a team, agree to the number of votes you get. Copy the star x times. Everyone put your star on your sticky notes as a 'vote'." Outcomes: "A list of 'backlogged' work / A list of the top priorities of work".
- "How do you know which work to do first? In short: you don't, and no one does. It's all just a guess and a judgment call based on what you know."
