Aging populations push demand for elderly care services

The global elderly care services market was valued at USD 804.70 billion in 2025 and is projected to increase from USD 849.65 billion in 2026 to USD 1,385.78 billion by 2035, registering a CAGR of 5.59% during 2026–2035. Demand comes from longer life expectancy, rising rates of dementia and chronic disease, and smaller families that can no longer provide all support at home. North America held the largest regional share, 36.0% in 2025, because public programs and private payers fund large home health and residential networks there. Asia Pacific is the fastest-growing region at 6.87% a year, as China, Japan, Singapore and India expand services for very large older populations. Policy also shifted in late 2025, when Australia started its new aged care law and the Support at Home program on Nov. 1. As a result, governments and families are moving spending away from hospitals and toward home and community support.

Which senior support services does this analysis count?

The analysis counts paid personal care, companion care, homemaking, rehabilitation and related support delivered to older adults at home, in day centers and in public or private residential facilities. Families, public insurers, private insurers and local governments buy these services through hourly visits, monthly packages and residential contracts.

Hospital acute treatment, prescription drugs and medical equipment sales sit outside the scope of this analysis. The value chain runs from training providers and staffing agencies through home care agencies, nursing homes and day centers to the older adults and families who receive daily support.

Home care leads as companion care grows fastest

Why does home care take most facility spending?

Based on service facility, the market is categorized into home care, private facilities and public facilities. Home Care led with a 46.0% share of 2025 value, because most older adults prefer to age in their own homes. Home Care also grows fastest at 6.10% a year, as governments fund home packages to reduce hospital and nursing home costs. Private facilities attract families seeking specialist dementia support and higher comfort levels.

Which service type grows fastest through 2035?

Based on service type, the market is categorized into personal care, companion care, homemaking, rehabilitation and recovery, and others. Personal Care led with a 38.0% share of 2025 value, because bathing, dressing and mobility help are core needs for frail adults. Companion Care grows fastest at 6.34% a year, as loneliness among adults living alone now drives demand for regular visits and social support.

How do public insurers shape spending on senior support?

Public Insurance led with a 52.0% share of 2025 value, because Medicaid, national long-term support schemes and local programs fund most home and residential support. Private Insurance grows fastest at 6.49% a year, as more working adults buy long-term support cover for their later years.

Population aging and public funding lift demand

How does rapid population aging raise demand for support?

Population aging is the main driver, because the number of adults who need daily help rises with every year of longer life. According to the World Health Organization fact sheet on aging and health, one in six people worldwide will be aged 60 years or over by 2030. The number of people aged 80 years or older is expected to triple between 2020 and 2050 to reach 426 million. As a result, providers in every region face steady growth in clients who need personal care and supervision.

Why do public funding reforms favor paid home support?

Public funding reforms favor home support, because governments want older adults to stay out of hospitals and nursing homes for as long as possible. In April 2024, CMS finalized a Medicaid access rule that requires at least 80% of payments for homemaker, home health aide and personal care services to go toward compensation for direct care workers. States have several years to comply with this payment requirement under the rule. In turn, agencies that pay and retain staff well are better placed to win public contracts.

How do Asian government programs expand community support?

Asian governments are expanding community programs, because their populations are aging faster than health budgets can absorb hospital care. In January 2024, the Chinese State Council issued a guideline on the silver economy, which aims to expand services and products for senior citizens and to build around ten industrial parks nationwide. Singapore is also adding Active Ageing Centres and home personal care capacity across the island. As a result, operators in Asia Pacific gain new public and private clients.

Why do dementia and chronic illness lift demand for skilled help?

Dementia and chronic illness lift demand for skilled support, because these conditions require supervision, medication reminders and help with daily tasks over many years. Families often cannot provide this level of support while also working full time. Many households therefore combine paid home visits with personal emergency response systems and day programs. In turn, providers that offer specialist dementia training and longer visit hours can charge higher rates.

Care shifts toward homes, communities and technology

How is support shifting from facilities toward homes?

Support is shifting from residential facilities toward homes, because older adults and payers both prefer lower-cost care in familiar settings. Australia started its new Aged Care Act and the Support at Home program on Nov. 1, 2025, replacing the Home Care Packages Program and the Short-Term Restorative Care Programme. The change sets one framework for home support and short-term recovery services. As a result, home care agencies in Australia are adjusting pricing, staffing and reporting.

Why is remote monitoring rising in home support?

Remote monitoring is rising in home support, because sensors and connected devices let small teams follow more clients between visits. Agencies now pair visits with remote patient monitoring for blood pressure, falls and medication adherence among frail adults. Families receive alerts and updates through simple phone apps, which builds trust in paid support. In turn, providers can offer tiered packages that combine visits, monitoring and on-call help.

Can specialist dementia homes open a growth opportunity?

Specialist dementia homes offer a clear opportunity, because many general facilities cannot manage memory loss and behavioral symptoms safely. In November 2025, Epoch Elder Care said it is scaling capacity from about 200 to more than 500 beds and aims for 1,000 beds within five years. The company is opening new facilities such as Monet House in Pune. As a result, operators with dementia training and secure buildings can win families who pay out of pocket.

Where can providers win clients in community programs?

Providers can win clients in community programs, because governments now fund day centers and social activities that delay the need for residential care. Singapore had 214 Active Ageing Centres in 2024, and the Ministry of Health planned $140 million to enhance and expand the network. The ministry also planned an island-wide rollout of enhanced home personal care by the end of 2025. As a result, operators with community centers can add companion, rehabilitation and respite services.

Workforce gaps and funding pressure limit capacity

Why do workforce shortages limit provider capacity?

Workforce shortages are the main constraint, because personal care work is physically demanding and often poorly paid. In July 2025, the United Kingdom closed the overseas route for care worker visas, so new entry clearance applications are no longer accepted. Providers in England that relied on international recruitment must now hire and train more local staff. As a result, many agencies turn down new clients or raise hourly rates to retain workers.

How does public funding pressure squeeze provider margins?

Public funding pressure squeezes margins, because public insurers set rates that often rise more slowly than wages and building costs. Providers that depend on public contracts then have little room to raise pay or invest in training. Rules that direct a fixed share of payments to worker pay can help staff but also limit spending on management and technology. In turn, small agencies often merge with larger groups to share costs.

Why do quality and safety concerns slow family trust?

Quality and safety concerns slow family trust, because reports of neglect in residential settings receive wide public attention. Regulators respond with stricter inspections, reporting rules and staffing standards for facilities. Families then compare providers more carefully and often delay moving a parent into residential support. As a result, operators must invest in training, audits and clear communication to keep occupancy rates high.

How do high out-of-pocket costs limit access?

High out-of-pocket costs limit access, because many families in Asia, Latin America and Africa receive little public support for long-term care. Households then rely on unpaid relatives or informal helpers who lack formal training. Private insurance cover for long-term support remains limited in most emerging markets. In turn, demand for paid services grows more slowly than the older population, and providers must offer lower-cost packages to reach middle-income households.

Why does North America hold the largest regional share?

North America held a 36.0% share of 2025 value and grows at 5.17% a year through 2035. The United States drives this position, because Medicare, Medicaid and private payers fund large home health, personal care and residential networks. People aged 65 and over made up 17.9% of the U.S. population in 2024, according to World Bank data. Federal rules on worker pay and growing demand for assisted living keep providers investing in staff and new capacity.

What shapes demand across Europe?

Europe held a 31.0% share of 2025 value and grows at 4.87% a year through 2035. Italy, Germany and France have some of the oldest populations in the world, with people aged 65 and over making up 24.6%, 23.2% and 22.1% of their populations in 2024. National long-term support schemes fund most home and residential services in these countries. However, staff shortages and tight public budgets keep growth below the global rate.

How fast is Asia Pacific adding clients?

Asia Pacific held a 24.0% share of 2025 value and is the fastest-growing region at 6.87% a year. Japan has the oldest population among large economies, with 29.8% of residents aged 65 and over in 2024, while China reached 14.7%. China's silver economy policy and Singapore's community programs expand services for very large groups of older adults. India adds demand from urban families that now buy professional home support and specialist dementia homes.

Where is Latin America adding demand?

Latin America held a 5.0% share of 2025 value and grows at 6.07% a year through 2035. Brazil leads regional demand, and people aged 65 and over made up 11.0% of its population in 2024. Private operators are adding home support and residential homes in large cities.

What limits growth in the Middle East and Africa?

The Middle East and Africa held a 4.0% share of 2025 value and grows at 6.17% a year through 2035. Gulf states add higher-value demand from private home support, while family care remains the norm across most of Africa.

Monitoring, telehealth and scheduling tools lead change

  • Connected sensors and alarms detect falls, inactivity and wandering at home, which lets small teams respond quickly between scheduled visits.
  • Telehealth visits connect older adults with nurses and doctors from home, which reduces travel and supports earlier action on health changes.
  • Scheduling and care planning software matches workers to clients and records each visit, which supports public reporting rules and billing.
  • Rehabilitation technology such as guided exercise programs and stroke rehabilitation tools helps older adults recover mobility after illness or surgery.

Local operators compete on staff, trust and coverage

Competition is fragmented, because most services are delivered by local operators that serve one city or country. In Singapore, United Medicare Pte Ltd., St Luke's ElderCare Ltd, SASCO Integrated Eldercare Centre, Pacific Healthcare Nursing Home and Econ Healthcare Group run nursing homes, day centers and community programs that work closely with public agencies. In India, Epoch Elder Care, Samvedna Senior Care and ApnaCare focus on assisted living, dementia support and home visits for urban families. Rosewood Care Group Inc., RIEI Co. Ltd., Cascade Healthcare, Millennia Personal Care Services and GoldenCare Group Private Limited add residential and home support in their local markets. Providers compete on staff training, dementia expertise, family communication and the ability to win public contracts. As a result, buyers often favor operators that combine home visits, day programs and residential beds under one brand.

Home and community models set the growth pace

Credence Research's view is that home and community models will set the pace of growth through 2035, because they cost less than residential beds and match what most older adults want. The overall market grows at 5.59% a year, supported by aging populations, public funding reforms and rising rates of dementia. Personal care keeps the largest base, but companion and recovery services grow faster as loneliness and post-hospital needs rise. Operators that recruit and retain trained staff, use remote monitoring and win public contracts will gain the most share over the decade.

How Credence Research sized senior support spending

The report uses 2025 as the base year and 2026 to 2035 as the forecast period. Market values come from the Credence Research model, which builds spending by service facility, service type, payment source and region. Regional and segment splits are Credence Research estimates informed by World Health Organization and World Bank statistics, government policy releases from the United States, China, Singapore, Australia and the United Kingdom, and company announcements. No primary interviews or surveys were used in preparing this edition of the report.

Senior support spending outlook to 2035

By 2035, the elderly care services market is projected to reach USD 1,385.78 billion, with growth led by home support, companion visits and recovery programs. Personal care will remain the largest service type, although payers will expect better reporting and outcomes. North America keeps the highest spending per client, while Asia Pacific adds the most new clients through public programs and private operators. More support will move into homes and community centers over the forecast period. As a result, advantage will depend on staff supply, dementia skills and the ability to combine visits with remote monitoring.
