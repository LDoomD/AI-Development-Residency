Sample data tables & charts Research methodology Download Free Sample Population aging and public funding lift demand How does rapid population aging raise demand for support? Population aging is the main driver, because the number of adults who need daily help rises with every year of longer life. According to the World Health Organization fact sheet on aging and health , one in six people worldwide will be aged 60 years or over by 2030. The number of people aged 80 years or older is expected to triple between 2020 and 2050 to reach 426 million. As a result, providers in every region face steady growth in clients who need personal care and supervision. Why do public funding reforms favor paid home support? Public funding reforms favor home support, because governments want older adults to stay out of hospitals and nursing homes for as long as possible. In April 2024, CMS finalized a Medicaid access rule that requires at least 80% of payments for homemaker, home health aide and personal care services to go toward compensation for direct care workers . States have several years to comply with this payment requirement under the rule. In turn, agencies that pay and retain staff well are better placed to win public contracts. Asian governments are expanding community programs, because their populations are aging faster than health budgets can absorb hospital care. In January 2024, the Chinese State Council issued a guideline on the silver economy , which aims to expand services and products for senior citizens and to build around ten industrial parks nationwide. Singapore is also adding Active Ageing Centres and home personal care capacity across the island. As a result, operators in Asia Pacific gain new public and private clients. Why do dementia and chronic illness lift demand for skilled help? Dementia and chronic illness lift demand for skilled support, because these conditions require supervision, medication reminders and help with daily tasks over many years. Families often cannot provide this level of support while also working full time. Many households therefore combine paid home visits with personal emergency response systems and day programs. In turn, providers that offer specialist dementia training and longer visit hours can charge higher rates. Care shifts toward homes, communities and technology How is support shifting from facilities toward homes? Support is shifting from residential facilities toward homes, because older adults and payers both prefer lower-cost care in familiar settings. Australia started its new Aged Care Act and the Support at Home program on Nov. 1, 2025, replacing the Home Care Packages Program and the Short-Term Restorative Care Programme. The change sets one framework for home support and short-term recovery services. As a result, home care agencies in Australia are adjusting pricing, staffing and reporting. Why is remote monitoring rising in home support? Remote monitoring is rising in home support, because sensors and connected devices let small teams follow more clients between visits. Agencies now pair visits with remote patient monitoring for blood pressure, falls and medication adherence among frail adults. Families receive alerts and updates through simple phone apps, which builds trust in paid support. In turn, providers can offer tiered packages that combine visits, monitoring and on-call help. Specialist dementia homes offer a clear opportunity, because many general facilities cannot manage memory loss and behavioral symptoms safely. In November 2025, Epoch Elder Care said it is scaling capacity from about 200 to more than 500 beds and aims for 1,000 beds within five years. The company is opening new facilities such as Monet House in Pune. As a result, operators with dementia training and secure buildings can win families who pay out of pocket. Where can providers win clients in community programs? Providers can win clients in community programs, because governments now fund day centers and social activities that delay the need for residential care. Singapore had 214 Active Ageing Centres in 2024, and the Ministry of Health planned $140 million to enhance and expand the network. The ministry also planned an island-wide rollout of enhanced home personal care by the end of 2025. As a result, operators with community centers can add companion, rehabilitation and respite services. Workforce gaps and funding pressure limit capacity Why do workforce shortages limit provider capacity? Workforce shortages are the main constraint, because personal care work is physically demanding and often poorly paid. In July 2025, the United Kingdom closed the overseas route for care worker visas, so new entry clearance applications are no longer accepted. Providers in England that relied on international recruitment must now hire and train more local staff. As a result, many agencies turn down new clients or raise hourly rates to retain workers. How does public funding pressure squeeze provider margins? Public funding pressure squeezes margins, because public insurers set rates that often rise more slowly than wages and building costs. Providers that depend on public contracts then have little room to raise pay or invest in training. Rules that direct a fixed share of payments to worker pay can help staff but also limit spending on management and technology. In turn, small agencies often merge with larger groups to share costs. Why do quality and safety concerns slow family trust? Quality and safety concerns slow family trust, because reports of neglect in residential settings receive wide public attention. Regulators respond with stricter inspections, reporting rules and staffing standards for facilities. Families then compare providers more carefully and often delay moving a parent into residential support. As a result, operators must invest in training, audits and clear communication to keep occupancy rates high. How do high out-of-pocket costs limit access? High out-of-pocket costs limit access, because many families in Asia, Latin America and Africa receive little public support for long-term care. Households then rely on unpaid relatives or informal helpers who lack formal training. Private insurance cover for long-term support remains limited in most emerging markets. In turn, demand for paid services grows more slowly than the older population, and providers must offer lower-cost packages to reach middle-income households.
