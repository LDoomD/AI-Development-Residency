Who Can Help | DSHS 
 Who Can Help
 Connect with agencies and partners for help with long‑term care options and help with next steps.
 Long-term care services help people understand their options and make informed decisions. Local partners can guide you to programs in your community and support you as you plan for what comes next.
 Area Agency on Aging
 Area Agencies on Aging help adults age 60 and older remain independent. Find your AAA office .
 AAA staff can help:
 Plan for long-term care.
 Find services in their community.
 Learn about programs that support living at home.
 Find help for family members or friends who provide unpaid care.
 Community Living Connections
 Community Living Connections offers free help to older adults, people with disabilities, caregivers, and families. Staff listen to your needs, explain your options, and help you find local services. 
 Find your local CLC support   
 Call  1-855-567-0252 
 Home and Community Services
 Home and Community Services helps adults who may need Medicaid to help pay for long-term care. If someone qualifies for Medicaid, an HCS case manager works with them to decide what care is needed and creates a care plan. Local HCS staff know the services available in each community.
 Find your local HCS Office 
 Senior Information & Assistance
 Senior Information and Assistance is a free service for adults age 60 and older and the people who care for them.
 Local offices can help people find:
 Transportation.
 Meals.
 Housekeeping.
 Personal care.
 Housing.
 Public benefits.
 Health insurance and prescription drug information.
 Aging and Disability Resource Centers
 Aging and Disability Resource Centers offer free long-term care information for people of all ages. They help people plan for care, understand benefits, explore housing options, and get referrals for legal or financial guidance. 
 Pierce County is Washington’s pilot ADRC site. 
 Family Caregiver Support Program
 The Family Caregiver Support Program helps unpaid caregivers who care for an adult family member, friend, or neighbor. 
 Local staff can help caregivers find:
 Support groups.
 Counseling.
 Training.
 Respite care.
 Other local resources.
 Adult Protective Services
 Adult Protective Services helps protect vulnerable adults from abuse, neglect, abandonment, and financial exploitation. APS reviews reports and investigates concerns. There is no charge for an APS investigation. Some protective services may also be available at no cost. 
 Report Abuse and Neglect 
 Long-Term Care Ombudsman
 The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program supports people who live in licensed care settings, including:
 Adult family homes.
 Assisted living facilities.
 Nursing homes.
 Ombudsmen listen to residents, explain their rights, and help address concerns. They may also work with families and facility staff to support the resident’s safety, well-being, and quality of life.
 Find your local Ombudsman Office 
 Developmental Disabilities Administration
 The Developmental Disabilities Administration supports people with developmental disabilities and their families. Visit How to enroll with DDA. 
 DDA can help people learn about:
 Long-term support services.
 Community programs.
 Residential care options.
