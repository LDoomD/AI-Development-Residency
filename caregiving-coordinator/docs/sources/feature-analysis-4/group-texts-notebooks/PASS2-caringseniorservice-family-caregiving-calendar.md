Organizing a Family Caregiving Calendar

 by Rachelle Ehlert | Mar 12, 2026

Caring for a family member is a rewarding but challenging responsibility. When multiple family members are involved, tasks can quickly become disorganized. This can lead to missed appointments, forgotten medications, and added stress.

A family caregiving calendar can help streamline communication, ensure responsibilities are clearly assigned, and help your loved one receive consistent care. Using the right tools, families can create an efficient caregiving system to keep everyone informed and reduce burnout.

Learn how to set up and organize a caregiving calendar, what to include, and the best tools to use.

What To Include in the Calendar

A caregiving calendar should cover all essential tasks that your loved one needs help with. Being as specific as possible will help everyone understand expectations and avoid confusion.

Here are some key responsibilities to include:

Medical Appointments: Doctor visits, physical therapy, lab tests, etc.

Medication Management: Prescription pickups, medication reminders, or administering medications.

Household Chores: Cleaning, laundry, meal prep, and basic maintenance.

Errands & Grocery Shopping: Stocking up on food, household supplies, and prescriptions.

Personal Care Assistance: Home care duties like bathing, dressing, and hygiene-related support.

Social Activities & Companionship: Family visits, church events, or community outings.

Transportation Needs: Rides to appointments or social engagements.

Check-Ins & Well-Being Updates: Quick phone calls or home visits to monitor their condition.

Respite Care: Scheduled time off for primary caregivers to rest and recharge.

Pro Tip: Be as detailed as possible by including the time, location, and estimated duration of each task.

Best Caregiving Calendar Tools 

A shared caregiving calendar helps coordinate responsibilities, keeps everyone informed, and ensures tasks don’t fall through the cracks. Digital tools update in real time, making them especially helpful for families with multiple caregivers.

Here are some of the best caregiving calendar tools to consider:

1. Google Calendar (Free, widely used shareable calendar)

Best for: Families already using Google products.

Pros: Free, simple to use, and integrates with other apps.

Cons: Not caregiving-specific; may require customization.

With Google Calendar you can create a calendar and share it with your family members. Schedule care tasks and appointments, and assign them to specific family members. Family members can get alerts when tasks are coming up. However, it may not be the most straightforward option since it is not specifically designed to help with family caregiving.

2. Lotsa Helping Hands (Caregiving-specific)

Best for: Families needing a structured care schedule.

Pros: Easy task sign-up system with automatic reminders.

Cons: Requires all family members to sign up for the platform.

This care management app is designed specifically for families caring for loved ones. Families can coordinate care by creating a shared care community where tasks like appointments and errands can be assigned. It automatically sends reminders and includes a notes section for health history, medications, and other key details.

3. Carely (Caregiving & communication in one)

Best for: Families who want care updates & group chats in one place.

Pros: The chat feature keeps everyone on the same page.

Cons: Fewer scheduling options than Google Calendar or Lotsa Helping Hands.

Carely is like a social media network for caregiving, making coordinating with your family unit easy. Share updates on your loved one’s health with real-time messaging. Post photos and memories to keep everyone emotionally connected. Plus, Carely lets you organize and track appointments, errands, and caregiving shifts within the app.

4. Caring Bridge (Private website for caregiving)

Best for: Families looking for a secure, private way to share updates.

Pros: Custom website with journals, schedules, and fundraising options.

Cons: Requires setup; better suited for long-term care management.

CaringBridge provides a private website for sharing updates, documenting your loved one’s journey, and coordinating support. It includes a journal, planner, and fundraising tools, allowing families to stay connected and organize care efficiently.

5. Caring Village (Comprehensive care hub)

Best for: Families seeking an all-in-one caregiving platform.

Pros: Secure document storage, built-in messaging, and customizable wellness tracking.

Cons: Some features require a paid plan, and the interface has a learning curve.

Caring Village helps families coordinate care with task assignments, medication tracking, shared to-do lists, and secure messaging. Its document center stores medical and legal records, while the wellness journal tracks mood, symptoms, and health updates for better oversight.

6. Medisafe (Medication & health tracking)

Best for: Families needing a medication management tool with real-time alerts and tracking.

Pros: Easy-to-use, real-time caregiver notifications, and medication interaction warnings.

Cons: Primarily focused on medication management, lacks broader caregiving task coordination features

Medisafe helps individuals and caregivers manage medication schedules with dose reminders, drug interaction warnings, and refill alerts. It also allows family members to track adherence and syncs with healthcare providers for seamless care coordination.

How to Share & Manage the Calendar Effectively

Once you’ve chosen the best caregiving calendar, set clear guidelines for family members to follow.

Schedule a Family Meeting: Discuss responsibilities and expectations before rolling out the calendar. Include nuclear family members as well as extended family members who want to be involved.

Assign a Point Person: Have one family member oversee updates and scheduling. Assigning a point person ensures accountability, prevents scheduling conflicts, and provides a central contact for updates and coordination.

Encourage Participation: Even long-distance family members can help (handling finances, scheduling appointments, or ordering groceries).

Respect Privacy: Respect the privacy of your aging loved one. Not all health details need to be shared with every member of the family care team. Set permissions accordingly.

Stay Flexible: Things change; caregivers and your care planning structure should be able to adjust as needed.

Pro Tip: Regularly review the calendar every 2–4 weeks to adjust tasks and accommodate changes in your loved one’s care needs.

Creating a caregiving calendar is an effective ways to coordinate care, prevent burnout, and ensure your loved one receives the support they need. By using the right tools, families can work together to provide high-quality care for their aging loved ones.
