[text 2843:15133] 1. No one handles a parent who refuses help or gets relatives to share the work.

[text 2843:15134] Gap: every competitor gets Partly or No on both. A Place for Mom's advisor even told a family to lie to the parent. Caring Village can't get relatives to join. Opening: help that works around a refusing parent and splits real tasks among relatives.

[text 2843:15136] 2. Personal, ongoing guidance is mostly locked behind an employer or a $150-an-hour bill.

[text 2843:15137] Gap: Cariloop comes mostly through employers; keeping it after leaving a job costs $249 a month. Care managers cost $150 to $300 an hour. Free and public help is limited: Medicare GUIDE (a care navigator and up to $2,500 a year for respite) covers only people with dementia; the VA covers only veterans; the Alzheimer's helpline gives free advice, though a few caregivers found it general; Area Agencies on Aging mostly give referrals. Only 12% of caregivers would pay out of pocket, and willing payers cap near $70 a month. Opening: coach-level guidance under $70 a month, or sold through employers, since 70% of working-age caregivers have jobs.

[text 2843:15139] 3. Help with money and accounts works only if the parent prepared ahead of time.

[text 2843:15140] Gap: Everplans is useless unless the parent filled it in. Caring Village stores documents but can't get the caregiver into bank or insurer accounts. The market analysis found 132 posts about getting into a parent's accounts. Opening: getting caregivers into a parent's accounts after the parent can no longer set it up. Risk: health data and consent. Caring Village shows medical details to invited friends, and Everplans lets parents lock sections.

[text 2843:15141] Also noted: • Burnout and meditation apps (segment 12) stay out of the comparison: caregivers almost never use them (9 of 49,315 posts). • Medicines and hands-on care, and choosing a place, stay in the competitor rows but aren't gaps.

[text 2843:15174] 4. Relief exists, but caregivers can't reach it.

[text 2843:15175] Gap: 39% of caregivers say respite would help, but only 13% have ever used it (AARP/NAC 2025, PDF pp.26-27). In caregiver posts, 128 used respite, 78 couldn't get it and 81 were still looking. Cost, the parent refusing, family not helping, eligibility rules, waitlists and rigid hours block it, for adult children most of all. Spouses face the same blocks, in a slightly different order. Respite services and the VA give real relief once a family gets in. Opening: helping caregivers find, qualify for, pay for and actually use relief, including getting the parent to agree and relatives to cover.
