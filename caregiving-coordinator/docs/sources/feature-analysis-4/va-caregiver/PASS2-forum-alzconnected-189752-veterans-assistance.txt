 Hi Denise, My husband was 100%% service connected and the VA provided any and everything we needed. He was diagnosed with Vascular Dementia in 2008 and I lost him January 15, 2015. I was able to keep him at home and was his sole caregiver- no nearby relatives to help. No hassle at all to get help from the VA. 
 We had both been employed at our fairly local VA Hospital but this had nothing to do with the care and supplies provided. You need to talk with a social worker at your VA Hospital and a Service Officer. We used the Oklahoma Department of Veterans Affairs. There are many organizations you might use. If your husband is service connected, there shouldn't be a problem. What can be provided depends on the percentage of service connection. 
 First, talk with the Outpatient Social Worker and they will guide you. There is help out there and a veteran has earned any help they can provide. Don't hesitate to ask for help. Good luck.

 My DH is a vet and not service connected. I receive excellent help from the VA. I live in Phoenix AZ. The VA supplies all hygiene items, hospital bed, wheel chair etc... even installed safety bars and a bidet. My DH also attends a Day Program, home health aide to assist in getting him up and ready for the day and evening help, transportation on para -transit, 180 hours of respite care a year and two weeks of inpt respite care. All this with out charge. They offer lots of education on dementia as well. If you have a service connection there are more benefits. 
 I just called and talked to a social worker and found it very easy to get things started. I believe with the help the VA offers I will be able to keep him at home.

 My LO receives care from the VA Geriatrics Team and they have been tremendous. They have provided all the necessary equipment for his care (ie.... walker, wheelchair, grab bars, adult diapers, pads for bed, PT, OT, and so much more). Yes contact the social worker for Geriatrics and they will provide the resources that you need. I was recently approved for the Caregiver Assistance Program so now I am considered his legal caregiver. I am authorized 30 days of respite care thru the VA at no cost along with unrecalled weeks of respite care in a skilled nursing facility. Also, as being a part of the Caregiver Assistance Program, I am given a stipend which I will use to pay for additional home health care as needed. 
 Since your husband is 100% disabled, you will qualify for many benefits. 
 Good luck

 I would ask the primary Dr. for a referral to the geriatric social worker. I initially was not aware of all the benefits the VA provided until the primary Dr. (NP) approved scripts for bed, adult depends, wchair etc... and then did a referral to the social worker. The social worker contacted me the next day. 
 I also know that some of the speciality providers at the VA are not aware of all the services provided.
 If you have issues with the claim for compensation then I would discuss with one of the service organizations.
