                                   Executive Summary
The Veterans Health Administration’s (VHA) Caregiver Support Program oversees the Program
of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC). PCAFC offers benefits such as a
monthly VA stipend and healthcare coverage to primary family caregivers of eligible veterans
who have incurred or aggravated a serious injury or illness during active military duty and need
in-person personal care for at least six continuous months.1 Veterans and caregivers must meet
an established set of requirements to be eligible for the program (detailed in appendix A).2
During the review period, if they disagreed with a program decision related to enrollment or
benefits, veterans and caregivers could appeal the decision through a higher-level review or a
supplemental claim (referred to collectively in this report as decision reviews) or an appeal to the
Board of Veterans’ Appeals.3 These three options are provided by the Veterans Appeals
Improvement and Modernization Act of 2017 (also known as the Appeals Modernization Act or
AMA).4 Additionally, they can submit a clinical appeal, which according to VHA
Directive 1041(2) is a request for higher review of one or more medical determinations.5
The VA Office of Inspector General (OIG) conducted this audit to assess how the Caregiver
Support Program processes and tracks PCAFC AMA decision reviews to ensure compliance with
statutory requirements and applicable VHA policy. To provide a complete picture of all the
available appeal options, the OIG team also reviewed the timeliness and outcomes of clinical
appeals. The OIG found that PCAFC AMA decision reviews took an average of more than
400 days to complete and these wait times were not publicly reported, limiting the information
veterans and caregivers need to make appeals decisions and possibly delaying benefits.
In October 2025, as well as January and March 2026, the audit team briefed the Caregiver
Support Program’s executive director and the director of review and appeals on preliminary
results and recommendations. The OIG made three recommendations to improve PCAFC AMA
decision review timeliness and transparency. VHA concurred with all recommendations and
provided acceptable action plans.

What the Audit Found
The OIG found that despite VHA Notice 2025-09 reiterating VHA employees’ duty to decide
claims for benefits quickly and accurately, the average completion time for PCAFC AMA
decision reviews was about 416 days from February 11, 2022 (the date the program began
issuing AMA decision reviews), through March 31, 2025.6 Clinical appeals took, on average,
36 days to complete from October 15, 2020, through March 31, 2025. The date ranges for these
two data points are different because clinical appeals have been offered longer than AMA
decision reviews. The OIG also found similar outcomes for both types of appeals through the end
of calendar year 2025 (April 1 through December 31, 2025).
PCAFC AMA decision review completion times were lengthy partly because the Caregiver
Support Program has not established timeliness goals, like those that apply to clinical appeals,
for AMA appeal options. On review of the Centralized Mail Portal—the system used to receive
digital and paper AMA-related claims mail—the audit team observed a backlog of 703 AMA
decision reviews awaiting action in July 2026. Some had been submitted as far back as
October 2025. During the course of this audit, the team discussed its preliminary finding related
to the backlog with the program office often, most recently in March 2026. While this backlog
has decreased, because it remains and new claims are regularly submitted, the Caregiver Support
Program continues to face challenges with minimizing decision review wait times.
In addition, the OIG found information provided to veterans, caregivers, and program staff does
not clearly communicate that a supplemental claim must be filed within one year from the date of
the decision notification letter to maintain eligibility for the earliest effective date for any granted
benefit. As a result, veterans and caregivers could be missing out on earlier effective dates,
resulting in fewer benefits being received.
If the timeliness and transparency concerns raised by this report are not addressed, veterans and
their caregivers may continue to make uninformed decisions about appeals, which could result in
limited or delayed benefits.

                                         Introduction
The Veterans Health Administration’s (VHA) Caregiver Support Program provides education,
resources, support, and services to caregivers of eligible veterans enrolled in VA health care
through two programs, one of which is the Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family
Caregivers (PCAFC).7 This assistance program offers enhanced clinical support for family
caregivers of eligible veterans who have incurred or aggravated a serious injury or illness in the
line of active military duty and require in-person personal care for a minimum of six continuous
months.8 Veterans and their caregivers must apply for the program together and are enrolled if
they meet an established set of specific requirements (detailed in appendix A).9 Once enrolled,
the veteran’s primary family caregiver receives a monthly VA stipend and can also receive other
benefits, including
    ·   healthcare coverage,
    ·   certain travel benefits when traveling with their veteran to receive care,
    ·   at least 30 days per year of respite—short-term relief for someone else to care for
        the veteran, and
    ·   access to military commissaries, exchanges, and recreation retail facilities.10

During the review period, if a veteran or caregiver disagreed with program decisions regarding
enrollment or benefits, such as the stipend amount, they could appeal the decisions through
options provided by the Veterans Appeals Improvement and Modernization Act of 2017 (also
known as the Appeals Modernization Act or AMA) or through clinical appeals.11 VHA
Notice 2025-09 specifies that benefits decisions—an official determination of one’s entitlement
to a benefit—are appealable through an AMA higher-level review or supplemental claim
(collectively referred to in this report as decision reviews) or through an appeal to the Board of
Veterans’ Appeals.12 VHA Directive 1041(2) specifies that a medical determination—a decision

Appeal Options
In April 2021, the Veterans Appeals Court ruled in Beaudette v. McDonough that caregiver
program benefits decisions are appealable to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals.15 Until this court
decision, clinical appeals were the only appeal option available to those who wanted to contest a
PCAFC decision. However, because of this ruling, VA began offering all three AMA options to
veterans or caregivers dissatisfied with program decisions, in addition to clinical appeals. Two of
these AMA options—higher-level reviews and supplemental claims—are available only for
decisions made by VA on or after February 19, 2019. Veterans and their caregivers can pursue
only one AMA option at a time. According to the Caregiver Support Program director of review
and appeals, after the ruling, the program had from April 19, 2021, through September 2021 to
implement a process for accepting PCAFC AMA decision reviews.
During the period of review, in practice those who are dissatisfied with a program decision can
choose from the options described below:
     ·   If a veteran or caregiver believes an error has been made and they do not have new
         evidence to submit, they can ask for a higher-level review. This decision review option
         provided under the AMA is a reevaluation of a benefit decision by a Caregiver Support
         Program reviewer who reexamines the evidence considered in the previous decision. A
         higher-level review must be requested within one year of the date that the Caregiver
         Support Program provided notice of the program decision.

           necessary to make a recommendation regarding the appeal. VHA Directive 1041(2) does

Caregiver Support Program Roles and Responsibilities for
AMA Options
The Caregiver Support Program, which oversees the PCAFC, is led by an executive director. The
program also has a director of review and appeals who oversees both the higher-level review
team and supplemental review team.
Once a higher-level review or supplemental claim is submitted, a health systems specialist
transfers information from the Centralized Mail Portal—the system used to receive digital and
paper AMA-related claims mail—to the Caregiver Records Management Application (CARMA).
CARMA is a workflow management tool that establishes and maintains records of current and
previous program applicants, as well as participants in the program. According to Caregiver
Support Program staff, CARMA is used as the primary method to track and manage PCAFC
AMA decision reviews and also allows for the tracking and review of clinical appeals.
The specialist reviews the submission and assigns a reviewer, who determines the outcome of the
decision review. The health system specialist then records the decision in CARMA, and the
veteran or caregiver is notified of the outcome. This process is described in more detail in
appendix B.
While the AMA requires VA to publicly report certain Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA)
decision review processing and tracking metrics, VHA’s Claims and Appeals Modernization
Office is awaiting an opinion from VA’s Office of General Counsel about the extent to which
these reporting requirements apply to VHA. Specifically, section 5 requires VA to periodically

publish metrics relating to VBA’s appeals processing. These metrics include the average time it
takes to process higher-level reviews and supplemental claims by regional office.17
The Claims and Appeals Modernization Office is responsible for working with affected VHA
program offices to draft policy and procedures that establish appropriate oversight and tracking
to ensure PCAFC AMA decision reviews are processed efficiently and accurately.18 However,
each program office—including the Caregiver Support Program—manages its own internal
decision review processes, oversight, accountability, and data. 19

Roles and Responsibilities for Clinical Appeals
For level 1 clinical appeals, a VA medical facility patient advocate helps the veteran or caregiver
submit the appeal. The centralized eligibility and appeal team within the appropriate Veterans
Integrated Service Network (VISN) reviews the appeal and recommends granting or denying it. 20
Then the VISN chief medical officer decides the appeal, and the VISN informs the veteran or
caregiver of the decision. The level 2 process is largely the same, except that the VISN patient
advocate coordinator receives the appeal, and a different centralized eligibility and appeal team
reviews the appeal and recommends granting or denying it. 21 In addition to the 45-day time
frame for completion noted in figure 1, the VISN must provide updates to the veteran or
caregiver if their appeal takes longer than 45 days. More details on the clinical appeals process
are provided in appendix B.

                     Results and Recommendations
Finding: PCAFC AMA Decision Review Wait Times Were Lengthy and
Not Communicated to Veterans
The OIG found that PCAFC AMA decision reviews completed from February 11, 2022 (the date
the program began issuing AMA decision reviews), through March 31, 2025, took an average of
more than 400 days, and this information was not publicly reported to help veterans make
informed appeals decisions. Clinical appeals took just under 40 days on average for decisions
from October 15, 2020, through March 31, 2025. The date ranges for these two data points are
different because clinical appeals have been offered longer than AMA decision reviews. The
OIG also found similar outcomes for both types of appeals through the end of calendar year 2025
(for decisions completed from April 1 through December 31, 2025).
PCAFC AMA decision reviews have taken longer to complete in part because timeliness goals
like those that apply to clinical appeals have not been established for AMA appeal options.
Furthermore, a backlog of AMA decision reviews has decreased but still remains. The remaining
backlog, coupled with the fact that new claims arrive each day, means that the program office
still needs to work to decrease wait times for veterans and caregivers who have filed AMA
decision reviews.
Beyond the length of time PCAFC AMA decision reviews take, VHA has not publicly reported
these time frames. This means veterans and caregivers lack important information that could help
them decide which appeal option to pursue when they disagree with a Caregiver Support
Program decision. Additionally, information provided to veterans, caregivers, and program staff
does not clearly communicate that a supplemental claim must be filed within one year from the
date of the decision notification letter to maintain eligibility for the earliest ef fective date for any
granted benefit. Until these timeliness and transparency concerns are addressed, veterans and
caregivers could continue to experience limited or delayed PCAFC benefits.

What the OIG Did
The audit team examined how long it took VHA to complete PCAFC AMA decision reviews and
clinical appeals. To capture the full effect on veterans since the Beaudette court ruling, the audit
team analyzed data on completed AMA decision reviews and clinical appeals. Specifically, the
team examined AMA decision review data (from February 11, 2022, through March 31, 2025)
and clinical appeal data (from October 15, 2020, through March 31, 2025). The team used data
from CARMA to determine how long it took to complete AMA decision reviews and clinical
appeals, measuring the time from receipt of the claim or appeal to the decision date. Some
submissions may be missing required information (such as signatures or medical
documentation), which can delay processing. However, the data did not identify when that
occurred or when the submission was completed or corrected to allow the team to assess how

Review of PCAFC AMA Decision Reviews
According to VHA Notice 2025-09, VHA employees have a duty to decide claims for benefits
quickly and accurately.22 The OIG found, however, that PCAFC AMA decision reviews
completed from February 11, 2022, through March 31, 2025, took about 416 days on average.
About 3 percent of higher-level reviews were granted, and about 4 percent of supplemental
claims were granted during this time.

CARMA data show that higher-level reviews took longer to complete than supplemental
claims—about 444 days compared to about 404, respectively, even though 2,595 higher-level
reviews were submitted compared to 4,796 supplemental claims. The Caregiver Support
Program’s director of review and appeals suspected that the higher-level reviews could be taking
longer to process than supplemental claims for several reasons, including that it was time-
consuming to schedule informal conferences with veterans and caregivers, or their
representatives, when requested. Informal conferences are unique to higher-level reviews and are
optional meetings that allow veterans or caregivers to discuss their claim with a reviewer. The
higher-level review request form allows veterans and caregivers to identify whether they would
like an informal conference.
Regarding supplemental claims timeliness, the Caregiver Support Program’s director of review
and appeals said obtaining documentation for the new evidence considered in these appeals, in
many instances, can take about 300 days. That said, in December 2025, the Caregiver Support
Program executive director said the program had implemented a new process to reduce the time
it takes to process a supplemental claim. As part of this new approach, before a supplemental
claim is assigned to a decision reviewer, health system specialists review the claim to make sure
that the veteran or caregiver included new and relevant information. If that information is
missing, the health system specialist sends a clarification letter to prompt the veteran or caregiver
to send the required records or sign a release of information allowing VA to access the records.
Once the veteran or caregiver has responded, the specialist assigns the claim to a decision
reviewer.

As table 1 shows, the program office began accepting PCAFC AMA decision reviews in
FY 2021 but did not complete any AMA decisions that year. Furthermore, as detailed in the
table, the number of AMA decision reviews completed each fiscal year by the Caregiver Support
Program significantly increased from FYs 2022 through 2024.
For the PCAFC AMA decision reviews filed during these years, the program proportionately has
been able to increase the number of decision reviews completed within 365 days or less. For
example, in FY 2022, the program completed only about 15 percent (235 of 1,578) of AMA
decision reviews filed within 365 days but improved to about 29 percent (749 of 2,609) in
FY 2023, and about 31 percent (744 of 2,373) in FY 2024. The average number of days to

after filing their AMA decision review to hear back from VA, which could delay granted

PCAFC AMA Decision Review Completion Goals
The OIG team determined that PCAFC AMA decision review timeliness may be improved by a
completion goal. However, unlike clinical appeals, VHA has not established a timeliness goal for
completing AMA decision reviews. VHA Directive 1041(2) includes a timeliness goal for
completing level 1 clinical appeals—meaning the appeal is to be decided and the decision
communicated to the veteran or caregiver who submitted the appeal—within 45 business days,
starting on the date that the claim is received. For level 2 clinical appeals, which may include an
external review, if additional time beyond the initial 45 days is necessary, the appeal must be
completed no more than 60 days after VHA receives the appeal. CARMA data show that, for the
most part, the program office has met this goal for clinical appeals.
VA reported in the November 2025 Congressionally Mandated Report Periodic Progress Report
on Appeals that the Claims and Appeal Modernization Office was working with program offices
to aggregate data to establish adequate metrics for the AMA system. That said, VA did not

provide a date for when the office expected to complete this effort. VHA’s Claims and Appeals
Modernization Office reported in May 2026 that the office was waiting for the VA Office of
General Counsel to clarify reporting requirements under AMA. Once that guidance is received,
the Claims and Appeals Modernization Office plans to work with affected program offices to
develop necessary measures and interim tracking tools, if necessary.
Although the Caregiver Support Program has not established overarching timeliness goals for
completing PCAFC AMA decision reviews, it has developed some milestone goals for health
system specialists and reviewers that may help improve timeliness. For example, the Caregiver
Support Program’s executive director said the program aims to move received decision reviews
from the Centralized Mail Portal to CARMA within three to five business days. The audit team
confirmed this was an FY 2025 performance standard for health system specialists, and it
remained for FY 2026. Decision reviewers also had an FY 2026 performance standard to
complete 65–75 decision reviews per quarter. However, neither of these performance standards
specifically address how quickly decision reviews should be completed.
Furthermore, the program office’s instruction manual for completing PCAFC AMA decision
reviews, last updated in September 2025, does not include overarching performance measures or
goals. The OIG team spoke with health system specialists and reviewers who confirmed that the
Caregiver Support Program does not have any overarching performance measures or goals for
completing decision reviews. Federal internal control standards say management should design
control activities to achieve objectives and that such activities can include establishing and
reviewing performance measures.25 As noted, VHA employees have a duty to decide benefits
claims quickly and accurately. But VHA cannot measure its overall effectiveness in doing so
without clearly established programmatic goals for completing these reviews.
The OIG’s first recommendation is for the under secretary for health to direct the Caregiver
Support Program’s executive director to develop and implement timeliness goals for completing
PCAFC AMA decision reviews to make sure that claims are assigned and acted upon in
accordance with these measures.

PCAFC AMA Decision Review Backlog
In July 2026, there was a backlog of 703 PCAFC AMA decision reviews awaiting action in the
Centralized Mail Portal, with some having been submitted as far back as October 2025. This
backlog had decreased by about 56 percent compared to the backlog observed by the OIG team
in December 2025. But because this backlog remains and new claims are regularly submitted, the
program office continues to face the challenge of how to minimize decision wait times for
veterans and caregivers.

PCAFC AMA Decision Review Metrics Reporting
AMA requires VA to publicly report processing and tracking metrics related to AMA appeal and
decision review options. 26 These metrics include “the average duration for processing claims and
supplemental claims, disaggregated by regional office,” and “the average duration for processing
requests for higher-level review … disaggregated by regional office.”27 Sections of the act refer
to VBA and its regional offices but do not specifically refer to VHA or specific program offices.
In September 2023, VHA’s Claims and Appeals Modernization Office asked the Office of
General Counsel which reporting metrics sections of the AMA apply to VHA. According to the
chief of VHA’s Claims and Appeals Modernization Office, VA’s Office of General Counsel had
not provided a response as of May 2026. As a result, VHA—including the Caregiver Support
Program—has yet to report such metrics as part of AMA reporting.
While the AMA does not mention reporting by VHA, that does not prohibit the Caregiver
Support Program from sharing such information, especially as it contributes to transparency and
can inform veteran and caregiver decision-making regarding which options to choose for
contesting a program decision. Although VA’s Office of General Counsel has not clarified AMA

PCAFC AMA Information Provided to Veterans, Caregivers, and

The OIG team also found that information on AMA appeal options provided to veterans and
caregivers is incomplete. VA Form 10‑305, Your Rights to Seek Further Review of Program of
Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC) Decisions, states, “A Supplemental
Claim may be filed at any time.” However, under 38 U.S.C. § 5110, a veteran or caregiver must
file a supplemental claim within one year from the date of the decision notification letter to
maintain eligibility for the earliest effective date for any granted benefit. Additionally, VHA
Notice 2025-09, which has interim guidance for program staff, does not clearly explain that a
supplemental claim filed within one year of the decision under the AMA will have an effective
date of when the original application was filed.30 The notice also does not communicate that
supplemental claims filed more than one year after the date of decision will have an effective
date of when the supplemental claim was filed.31 As a result, veterans and caregivers could be
missing out on earlier effective dates, resulting in fewer benefits received.
The OIG’s third recommendation is for the under secretary for health to update VA Form 10‑305
and VHA Notice 2025-09 to clarify that veterans or caregivers must file a supplemental claim
within one year from the date of the decision notification letter to maintain eligibility for the
earliest effective date for any granted benefit.

Conclusion
The OIG found that the Caregiver Support Program offered veterans and caregivers the option to
appeal decisions through PCAFC AMA decision reviews as required. However, the OIG found
that AMA decision reviews took significantly longer to complete than clinical appeals.
Timeliness goals, like those established for clinical appeals, could improve AMA decision
review wait times. Additionally, the Caregiver Support Program office has decreased its backlog
of AMA decision reviews. However, because a backlog remains and new claims continue to be
submitted, the program office needs to develop a plan to address the time veterans and their
caregivers wait to receive a decision.
Because VHA also has not publicly reported PCAFC AMA decision review time frames,
veterans and caregivers do not have access to important timeliness information when deciding
which appeal option to pursue and may be at risk of delayed benefits. Furthermore, the
information provided to veterans, caregivers, and program staff on the time frame to file a
supplemental claim should be clarified to more fully inform the veteran or caregiver that they
must file a supplemental claim within one year from the date of the decision notification letter to
maintain eligibility for the earliest effective date for any granted benefit. Until these timeliness

              Appendix C: Scope and Methodology
Scope
The audit team conducted its work from March 2025 through July 2026. The team focused on
the Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC) appeals and decision
reviews that are under the responsibility of the Caregiver Support Program. These include
clinical appeals, higher-level reviews, and supplemental claims. Board appeals are adjudicated
by the Board of Veterans’ Appeals and thus fall outside the purview of the program office.
Therefore, they were not included in the scope of this audit. The review population in the team’s
initial analysis was extracted from the Caregiver Records Management Application (CARMA)
and initially consisted of 39,506 records. The team removed 14,765 records because they were
out of scope. Therefore, the number of records in the universe totaled 24,741 that included
decisions Veterans Health Administration (VHA) issued for higher-level reviews and
supplemental claims from February 11, 2022, through March 31, 2025, and clinical appeals from
October 15, 2020, through March 31, 2025. The decision review and clinical appeal time frames
differed because they reflect when VHA issued decisions, and clinical appeals have been
accepted for a longer period. To determine the persistence of observations from the initial
analysis, the team extracted additional CARMA data. This review population consisted of 5,145
records with decisions completed from April 1 through December 31, 2025.

Methodology
The audit team reviewed applicable laws, regulations, policies and procedures related to the
processing and tracking of PCAFC decision reviews according to Veterans Appeals
Improvement and Modernization Act of 2017 (AMA) requirements and applicable VHA policy
for the PCAFC, including

       Decisions, September 28, 2020 (amended March 3, 2025);
   ·   VHA Notice 2025-09, Appeals Modernization Act in the Veterans Health
       Administration, September 19, 2025; and
   ·   VA, Congressionally Mandated Report Periodic Progress Report on Appeals, Pub.
       L. No. 115-55 § 3, November 2025.

The team assessed internal controls to determine whether they were significant to the audit
objective. This included consideration of the five internal control components: control
environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, and
monitoring.33 In addition, the team reviewed the principles of internal controls as associated with

Data Reliability
The audit team obtained records for this report from CARMA, which is the application used by
the Caregiver Support Program to process, store, and track records for clinical appeals,
higher-level reviews, and supplemental claims. Program staff manually enter clinical appeals
data from the Patient Advocate Tracking System Replacement (the system of record for clinical
appeals) and PCAFC AMA decision review information and supporting documentation from the
Centralized Mail Portal—the system used to receive digital and paper PCAFC AMA-related
claims mail—into CARMA. The team randomly selected a sample of 45 cases to compare with
the source data in the Patient Advocate Tracking System Replacement and Centralized Mail
Portal. The data included information on the type of decision review or clinical appeal, dates
decision reviews or clinical appeals were submitted, and veteran information. The team
determined the data were sufficient and reliable for the purposes of the team’s analysis and to
support the results and conclusions.

Recommendation 1: Develop and implement timeliness goals for completing Appeals
Modernization Act decision reviews for the Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family
Caregivers to make sure that claims are assigned and acted upon in accordance with these
measures.
VHA Comments: Concur. The Veterans Health Administration (VHA) agrees that the identif ication of a
target to assist in keeping work on track is an important component of any standardized workflow.
Since the conclusion of OIGs audit, VHA’s Caregiver Support Program (CSP) has continued to consider
the range of tasks required to review and issue decisions and is now better positioned to set timeliness
goals. CSP intends to issue decisions on supplemental claims and higher-level reviews within 60 days
f rom the date a completed request is received or the date a previously submitted incomplete request
becomes complete. A complete request is one which includes all information necessary for CSP to review
and issue a decision. An incomplete request is one that is missing information necessary for the Veterans
Af fairs (VA) to initiate the review, such as the identification of the issue being disputed or a required
signature on applicable forms.
CSP believes issuing decisions within 60 days f or complete claims will account f or the number of
incoming requests received and the time necessary to conduct a case review while maintaining high-
quality standards for comprehensive and accurate decisions.
To implement this 60-day timeliness goal for completed requests, several actions have been taken. In
June 2026, CSP made updates to its workflow management system to support the ability to distinguish
between complete and incomplete requests f or supplemental claims and higher-level reviews. These
improvements include functionality not previously available to identify, monitor, and report on timeliness to
decisions f or complete requests. The update introduced a new date field marking when a request
becomes complete and ready for adjudication, allowing CSP to measure decision timeliness more
accurately. All review and appeal team members completed training on the new functionality.
Retrospective reviews were conducted on all requests received before June 2026, to determine the status
f or each and documenting the relevant date accordingly.
Additionally, CSP intends to take the following additional actions: CSP will update the Program of
Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC) Decision Review and Appeals Instructional
Manual to incorporate the newly established timeliness goals and incorporate performance expectations.
Quality control measures will be implemented to monitor the accurate identification of the date on which a
request meets the criteria for being “complete.”

reviews for the Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers and report progress
routinely and transparently to inform Veterans.
VHA Comments: Concur.

VHA has taken targeted actions to address the backlog of Appeals Modernization Act decision reviews for
PCAFC and report progress. Beginning in July 2025, VHA strategically re-aligned staff within the Review
and Appeals workstream to focus specifically on supplemental claims and higher-level reviews. CSP also
established perf ormance metrics in April 2025 to increase the number of decisions rendered each
quarter. In January 2026, changes were made to how CSP issues requests f or inf ormation to Veterans
who have submitted requests for supplemental claims or higher-level reviews that are missing necessary
inf ormation. In March 2026, employees started collecting missing inf ormation by phone f rom Veterans
and caregivers to reduce delays associated with mailed correspondence. In April 2026, clinical reviewer
duties were adjusted so they can focus exclusively on case reviews and decisions. CSP continues to
update the Decision Letter templates to reduce administrative time and improve standardization and
consistency. These actions have led to a more than 70% reduction in the backlog and significantly
improved processing times, such that by the end of Fiscal Year 2026, CSP will be processing completed
claims within 60 days.
Additionally, to support transparency and accountability, CSP intends to routinely publish metrics related
to PCAFC higher-level reviews and supplemental claims on its website at www.caregiver.va.gov. These
metrics will include the number of completed supplemental claims and higher-level reviews in queue, and
timeliness metrics so that Veterans and caregivers are aware of the number of requests awaiting
decisions and can make informed decisions about which option to pursue.
Status: In Progress      Target Completion Date: November 2026
