Synergy CEO Oversees New Growth for In-home Care Brand

Charlie Young has been in franchising long enough to know that it all starts and ends with the franchisees.

“They’re the ones who are on the front lines, client facing and making it successful. As franchisors, it’s up to us, our responsibility, to build the right systems for them and give them the support and tools they need to succeed,” said Young, CEO of Synergy HomeCare. The brand offers in-home care services.

Young speaks from experience. Prior to taking the reins at Synergy, he spent 18 years in executive leadership roles at real estate franchisor Crendant and then Realogy Holdings Corp., where he served as CEO of Coldwell Banker Real Estate for three years. At Coldwell, he oversaw a system of more than 700 franchisees and 3,000-plus offices in 44 countries.

Since replacing Peter Tourian as CEO at Synergy in 2020, Young has overseen big growth for the brand. The brand has inked 147 new territories over the last three years, while growing its revenue by 40 percent over the same period. Synergy ranked at No. 236, up from No. 252 last year, on the Franchise Times Top 400 list, with more than $223 million in global sales in 2022. The brand opened 55 new territories in 2022, ending the year with 417 units.

Synergy added 16 new territories to its network in the third quarter this year, including seven in Texas along with other openings in Florida, Utah, California, Ohio, Massachusetts, North Carolina and its first-ever agency in eastern Idaho, which will serve the greater Idaho Falls area.

With 37 territories sold so far this year, and a number of new locations ready to open around the country, Synergy is on the verge of surpassing 500 locations.

“Our next big goal is to get to 750 locations, and we are confident we can get there with an aging population increasing and the demand in the home care space growing as fast as it is,” said Young.

Synergy launched a number of new initiatives over the past year to keep pace.

In October, it rolled out a disability care awareness program designed for those with disabilities to “gain a healthier, safer and happier quality of life at home.”

Additionally, Synergy made what Young described as significant investments in innovation across its service lineup. This includes development and enhancement of programs for veterans, memory care and hospital-to-home care, or as Synergy labels it “ConciergeCare,” which can begin as early as pre-discharge from a hospital.

ConciergeCare packages range from a few days of post-discharge support to an entire month or more of care.

Most Synergy franchises are contracted with the U.S. Department of Veteran Affairs, allowing veterans to use their VA benefits to gain access to the brand’s home care. “We're just trying to create a structure that enables our franchisees to go to the market and meet the needs of the market,” Young said.

Synergy’s introduction of additional programs will continue when it launches cancer care this month and adds Parkinson’s disease care early next year, he said. The initial investment range for one Synergy territory is $72,718 to $145,833.

The company is “growing it to meet the demands in the marketplace, both on the franchisee side as well as on the consumer or client demand side. But we've also at the same time, when you're dealing with such rapid growth, we know we have to build the infrastructure of the company along with it,” Young said.

Synergy’s franchisees fall into three buckets: care-minded entrepreneurs, healthcare professionals and what he called “career transitioners” looking to leave corporate jobs to pursue their passion of helping an aging population.

“It’s pretty evenly divided across the board,” said Young “What resonates with them is they understood the needs for quality home care in the marketplace and want to make things better for older people.”
