| Point | Page |

| **Method:** online survey in May and June 2025 of 16,029 people responsible for cybersecurity at workplaces in North America, Latin America, Asia-Pacific, and Europe, the Middle East and Africa. ISC2 calls it a record number. People outside English-speaking countries got a translated survey. | pp. 3, 40 |
| **No workforce gap number this year.** ISC2 says it did not estimate the workforce gap in 2025 because respondents now rank needed skills above needing more people. Earlier years did give a gap estimate. So this report has no "workforce size" or "gap" figure to cite. | p. 3 |
| Who answered: 22% IT services, 11% financial services, 11% government, 5% healthcare. 34% were in the United States. 78% male, 16% female. 39% were nonmanagerial mid- or advanced-level staff and 5% were entry-level. | pp. 41, 44, 46, 47 |
| **Budget pressure has leveled off, not eased.** In the past 12 months: 24% of organizations had cybersecurity layoffs, 36% budget cuts, 39% hiring freezes, 34% promotion freezes. Budget cuts were 30% (2023), 37% (2024), 36% (2025). | pp. 5-6 |
| Large organizations were hit harder: 32% of organizations with 10,000+ staff reported layoffs, compared with 17% for 1-99 staff and 18% for 100-499 staff. 49% of the biggest organizations had hiring freezes. | p. 7 |

Budget cuts: most in agriculture (43%); fewest in legal (19%), education (26%), construction (28%), nonprofit (31%). Healthcare is not named in these top and bottom lists. | p. 8 |

These are opinions, not measured breach rates. | p. 9 |
| **Staffing:** 63% report a staff shortage in 2025 (19% significant, 44% slight), down from 68% in 2024. 34% say they have the right amount of staff. 4% say they have too much. | p. 10 |

| Cloud skills named: cloud architecture and secure design (50% of those focused on cloud), platform and infrastructure security 41%, secure deployment and configuration 38%, identity and access management 35%, cloud data protection 34%. | p. 12 |

| **Nontechnical skills:** problem solving (hiring managers 54%, professionals 55%), teamwork (51%, 50%), communication (48%, 59%). When technical and nontechnical are ranked together, the top five that hiring managers want are all nontechnical (problem solving 29%, collaboration 24%, communication 22%, willingness to learn 20%, strategic thinking 16%). | pp. 18-20 |
| **How people enter the field:** 56% came through IT (36% took on security duties while in an IT role; 20% moved straight from IT). 10% came through cybersecurity education, 8% from another field, 6% through a certification, 4% self-taught, 3% military, 3% internship. Among ages 21-29, 38% came through IT and 23% through a cybersecurity degree. | pp. 21-22 |

| **AI adoption in security teams:** 28% have integrated AI security tools, 19% are testing, 22% are starting to evaluate. Together 69% are on a path to regular use. 63% of active users report a significant productivity boost. | p. 24-25 |
| Where respondents expect AI to help fastest: network monitoring and intrusion detection 40%, security operations 30%, security testing 30%, vulnerability management 29%, threat modeling 28%. | p. 25 |

The conclusion says 70% have or are pursuing AI qualifications (17% plus 53%). | pp. 26, 39 |
| **AI-related security events in the past year:** social engineering 40%, unauthorized access 29%, privacy violations 27%, data leakage 25%, suspected AI-powered attacks 23%, data breaches 23%, deepfakes 21%, data poisoning 11%, model theft 9%. Small organizations (1-99 staff) reported the most: 70% had at least one. | pp. 26, 28-29 |
| **AI skills wanted:** using AI in threat detection and response 42%, AI in threat modeling and risk assessment 39%, defending AI models 35%, securing AI in cloud and edge 31%, AI governance and policy 30%, data integrity and privacy in AI 30%, AI regulatory compliance 29%. | p. 27 |

## Useful for the paper

- **Topic 2 (career relevance):** shows demand is about skills more than headcount: 59% report critical or significant skills needs, 95% have at least one. Application security (28%), risk assessment (29%), GRC (27%) and cloud/IAM skills match the kind of work a proposed health-related coordinator would need. The entry-route table (pp. 21-23) and the nontechnical-skills tables (pp. 18-20) fit a career section.
- **Topic 6 (trends):** AI is the top skills need (41%); 69% of teams are on a path to AI security tools; AI-related incidents are reported by many (social engineering 40%); budgets have leveled off rather than recovered.
- **Topic 5 (challenges):** budget limits, skills gaps, burnout (47% and 48%), retention risk (66% likely to stay two years), and the 88% who report a consequence from skills gaps.
- It is a workforce study, not a threat or breach study. Do not use it as proof of how often breaches happen.

## Limits and cautions

- **Survey of self-reports.** 16,029 respondents answered an online survey; ISC2 does not say in this report how they were chosen, and the report gives no margin of error. Opinions (for example "cuts raise breach risk," 72%) are beliefs, not measured results. The sample is 34% U.S. and 78% male.
- **ISC2 sells certifications** (CISSP, CC and others) and its recommendations favor training and upskilling. Read recommendation pages (pp. 38-39) as the publisher's view.
- **No workforce size or gap number** in 2025 (p. 3). Do not quote an older ISC2 gap figure as if it came from this report. An older figure would need its own source (the 2024 study, not read here).
- **Small inconsistencies in the PDF text:** p. 7 compares the biggest organizations' hiring freezes (49%) to "the overall average of 34%," but p. 6 gives 39% overall for hiring freezes (34% is the promotion-freeze figure). The p. 30 satisfaction text says 68% overall; the chart rows are read from text and I did not match each row to its year. Quote the sentence numbers, not the chart rows, and prefer the percentages that appear in sentences.
- **Healthcare:** 5% of respondents worked in healthcare (p. 41). The report gives no healthcare-specific findings.
- **Two versions:** the web page is dated December 4, 2025; the PDF footer says 02/2026. I found no difference in the numbers I checked, but I did not compare every number.
- The "Research Note" says ISC2 chose not to estimate the gap; this is ISC2's own choice, not an outside finding.
