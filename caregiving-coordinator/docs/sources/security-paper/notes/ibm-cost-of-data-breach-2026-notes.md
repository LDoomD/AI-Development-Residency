## What the report says (my words)

| Point | Printed page |
|---|---|
| The study covers 602 organizations hit by breaches from March 2025 through February 2026, in 17 industries and 16 countries and regions. The breaches had 2,590 to 115,380 compromised records. Ponemon interviewed 3,558 people. | p. 5 |
| The global average cost of a breach rose 12% to a record USD 4.99 million. Detection and escalation plus lost business made up 63% of it. | pp. 6, 10, 12 |
| The U.S. average was a record USD 11.5 million. The report gives the rise as 13% on p. 6 and 11% on p. 10. The two pages disagree, so do not quote the percentage. | pp. 6, 10 |
| Healthcare was the costliest industry for the 13th year in a row, at USD 6.64 million. That is down 10.5% from USD 7.42 million. IBM says attackers value patient PII for identity theft and insurance fraud. | p. 14 |
| The mean time to identify and contain a breach rose to 247 days, a 2.5% rise that reversed a five-year decline. | p. 14 |
| Customer PII was the most stolen data type (52% of breaches) at USD 192 per record. Employee PII was in 35%. Intellectual property was in 32% and cost most per record (USD 196). | p. 16 |
| In 30% of breaches the data was stored on premises. Public cloud breaches cost USD 5.38 million. Data spread across all three locations took longest to find and contain (256 days). | p. 18 |
| 53% of breached organizations had not encrypted sensitive data at rest and in motion, and another 10% were not sure. | p. 20 |
| For the fourth year in a row phishing was the top attack vector, then supply chain compromise. Voice and SMS phishing was used in 17% of attacks and cost USD 5.29 million on average. Social engineering such as help desk impersonation cost USD 5.23 million. | p. 22 |
| Malicious or criminal attacks caused 55% of breaches, IT failures 22% and human error 23%. | p. 24 |
| Breaches that copied data to removable media and supply chain compromises each took 258 days to identify and contain. | p. 24 |
| Internal teams found 38% of breaches and managed security providers 31%. A breach the attacker announced cost the most (USD 5.12 million). | p. 26 |
| Breaches that lasted over 200 days cost USD 5.65 million. Shorter ones cost USD 4.32 million. | p. 30 |
| 42% of organizations fully recovered, up from 35%. Fewer than one in 20 recovered in under 50 days. | p. 32 |
| More than one in four organizations had an AI-driven malicious attack, a 56% increase. Deepfake impersonation was the largest type. AI-driven attacks added about USD 1 million to the average malicious breach. | p. 36 |
| Ransomware hit 39% of breached organizations. Threatening the brand's reputation was in 41% of those attacks. | p. 38 |
| AI-related breaches reached 21% of organizations, up from 13%. 92% of those lacked proper AI access controls. 43% had a shadow AI incident (workers using unapproved AI). | pp. 42, 46 |
| 68% of breached organizations lacked AI governance policies. | p. 48 |
| Factors that lowered cost the most: a DevSecOps approach, identity and access management, and a managed security service. Factors that raised cost most: a supply chain compromise (about USD 227,250 more), security system complexity, and shadow IT. Mismanaged secrets and keys, and excessive privileges and poor role management, are also listed as cost raisers. | pp. 50 to 51 |
| Extensive use of AI and automation saved USD 1.93 million and shortened the breach by 65 days. Only 36% of organizations used it extensively. | p. 52 |
| Fewer than half (46%) secured non-human identities in AI workflows. 61% lacked controls to secure cryptographic keys and certificates. 26% had a post-quantum cryptography project. | p. 62 |
| Methods: activity-based costing, four cost categories (detection and escalation, notification, post-breach response, lost business). | p. 68 |
| Do not use the per-record cost to price breaches of millions of records. The study's breaches had at most 115,380 records. | p. 69 |
| Limits IBM states: the sample is non-statistical and no margins of error can be applied. Non-response was not tested. The sample leans toward organizations with more mature security programs. Costs are partly estimated. | p. 70 |

## Possible use in the paper
- Topic 3 (background): the cost of breaches, with healthcare as the costliest sector, and how attacks usually start (phishing and stolen or misused accounts).
- Topic 8: the idea would hold health notes and family details, which fit the "customer PII" and healthcare points. The report says nothing about caregiver apps, so it supports only the general point. It does not say the idea would be secure or compliant.

## Limits
- The report is sponsored and published by IBM, which sells security products and services. It is a benchmark of 602 volunteer organizations, not a random sample (p. 70).
- It mostly describes organizations with at least 2,590 compromised records, so it may not describe small startups.
- Two page-to-page inconsistencies were found (the U.S. percentage rise; "more than twice" on p. 6 against "nearly double" on p. 10). Use only the dollar figures.
