## What the document says (my words)

| Point | Printed page |
|---|---|
| The CSF gives any organization, whatever its size, sector or maturity, a list of high-level security outcomes to understand, assess, prioritize and communicate its security work. It does not say how to reach the outcomes. | p. i (Abstract); p. 1 |
| Version 2.0 adds a focus on governance and on supply chains, and it is meant to fit small organizations too, through Quick Start Guides. | p. iv (Preface) |
| It has three parts: the Core (a list of outcomes), Organizational Profiles (a current and a target picture) and Tiers (how rigorous the practices are). | p. 1 |
| The Core has six Functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. Govern is new in 2.0 and sits in the middle of the wheel because it informs the other five. | pp. 3 to 5; Fig. 2 on p. 5 |
| The outcomes are not a checklist, and the order and size of the list do not show sequence or importance. | p. 3 |
| The Functions are worked on at the same time. Respond and Recover must be ready at all times and used when an incident happens. | p. 5 |
| The Core applies to all technology an organization uses, including cloud, mobile and AI systems. | p. 5 |
| A Profile is built in five steps: scope it, gather information, create it, analyze the gap between Current and Target, and carry out the action plan. These steps can be repeated. | pp. 6 to 7 |
| The four Tiers are Partial (1), Risk Informed (2), Repeatable (3) and Adaptive (4). They describe how informal or how mature an organization's risk practices are. | pp. 7 to 8; Appendix B, pp. 24 to 25 |
| NIST publishes extra online material that changes more often than the paper: Informative References, Implementation Examples and Quick Start Guides. | p. 9 |
| The CSF should be linked with other risk work: enterprise risk management, privacy, supply chain, and new technology such as AI. Security and privacy overlap, but privacy risks can also arise with no security incident. | pp. 10 to 14 |
| Protect includes identity, authentication and access control (PR.AA), data security (PR.DS), and secure software development practices (PR.PS-06). PR.AA-05 names least privilege and separation of duties. | pp. 15, 19 to 21 |
| Data security includes protecting data at rest and in transit, and backups that are created, protected and tested (PR.DS-01, -02, -11). | p. 20 |
| Respond includes notifying internal and external stakeholders of incidents (RS.CO-02). | p. 22 |
| The glossary defines Category, Core, Function, Profile, Subcategory and Tier. | p. 26 |

## Possible use in the paper
- Topic 1 (introduction) and Topic 5 (what the field includes): the six Functions as the plain map of what a security program covers.
- Topic 8 (the proposed caregiving coordinator): the Functions show where each of the idea's security tasks would sit. This does not say the idea is secure or compliant.

## Limits
- The Subcategory wording is a list of outcomes. NIST says plainly that it is not a how-to (p. 1).
- The CSF's own online resources (Informative References, Implementation Examples) were not part of this download.
