Here is why a plain yes is not safe. This project handles health and caregiving information, which GDPR treats as special-category data (Article 9). That sets a higher bar.

**What would need to be true before anyone could say "yes":**
- **Applicability:** GDPR applies if you offer the service to people in the EU/EEA or monitor them. This is not yet established.
- **Lawful basis:** For health data you need a lawful basis (Art. 6) plus an Art. 9 condition, usually explicit consent.
- **Transparency:** A privacy notice says what is collected, why, how long it is kept, and who receives it.
- **Data subject rights:** Users can access, export, correct, and delete their data.
- **Retention:** There is a defined retention period and a way to enforce it.
- **Third parties and AI services:** Processor agreements (DPAs) exist, and international transfers are covered.
- **Security:** Encryption, access control, and breach notification within 72 hours. Shared credentials among caregivers are a risk here.
- **Records:** Records of processing exist, and a DPIA is done, since health data is high-risk.
- **Third-party data:** The care recipient is a data subject who may not be the account holder.

**Next step:** A data inventory covering what is stored, where, who can see it, how long it is kept, and how it is deleted. That would let us list gaps. This is a technical gap check, not legal advice. For a real compliance determination, consult a privacy professional.
