# We Are Covering the "Green Circles" This Week

[video/file not saved: wk2-green-circles.png]

This week focuses on the **Product Management** disciplines:

- **Go-To-Market** — how a product reaches customers and lands at launch. *Deliverables: market analysis; competitive analysis; feature analysis; product positioning.*
- **Product Strategy** — what it's for, who it serves, how it wins. *Deliverables: release vision; release scope; KPIs.*
- **Requirements** — what a product needs to do. *Deliverables: user stories; problem statements; acceptance criteria; flow charts; use cases.*
- **Backlog Management** — what delivers over time + managing delivery. *Deliverables: product roadmap; release plan; kanban board.*

## Why Are We Here?

[video/file not saved: wk2-requirements-intro.png]

UX/research/product roles don't just deliver solutions — they **solve problems** (for users, clients, colleagues, the business) and must **communicate why the problem is worth solving**. Otherwise you're just taking orders. This class teaches you to solve the *right* problem *correctly* — breaking requests into user problems, goals, and needs.

## Requirements Are Assumptions!

Every team decision is an assumption. Nothing is verified until your whole audience uses something and gives representative, statistically significant data. Agile teams release early and often and continuously test assumptions and refine their vision. It's cyclical: every delivery forms new assumptions to validate.

## What Are Requirements?

- **Research Requirements** — what needs to be delivered to researchers to do their job; from product strategy/vision. *Examples: product-market fit analysis; personas; product vision; product scope; research goals.*
- **Design Requirements** — what needs to be delivered to designers to do their job; dictate needs & scope (not *how*), scoped for iterative delivery. *Examples: user stories; acceptance criteria; use case scenarios; BDD scenarios; task flows.*
- **Development Requirements** — what needs to be delivered to developers to do their job; exact direction for devs, scoped iteratively. *Examples: hi-fi prototypes; design annotations; design systems/UI kits; user stories; acceptance criteria; use cases; BDD; task flows.*

## This Is What It's Like When There Are No Requirements

[video/file not saved: wk2-no-requirements-cake.png]

The classic "tire-swing" cartoon done as a cake: what the customer said → what the waiter heard → what the chef thought → what the corporation envisioned → what the owners thought → how it turned out → how the customer got billed → how they were supported → **what would have really solved the problem.** Without requirements, everyone builds a different thing and none is what the customer needed.

## People vs. Job Titles vs. Commitments

[video/file not saved: wk2-people-titles-commitments.png]

A **Person** has one **Job Function** (title); one function has many **Job Duties**. So one person has many duties. Who "wears the hats" varies by company — duties (requirements, PM, dev/architecture, PO/PM/BA) may be split across people or combined. Roles: Business Analyst, Product Manager/Owner, Product Designer, UX Designer, UX Researcher, CEO/Executive (at a startup, maybe you). *Pro tip: these are "Duties" — ask Fleety (*[*techfleet.network/tal-9000*](http://techfleet.network/tal-9000)*).*

## AI Is Your Teammate

[video/file not saved: wk2-ai-teammate.png]

In the AI era, **you are the orchestrator and Claude is the producer of dev work** — NOT just a PM job. You wear every hat: Cake Chef, Product Owner, Product Operations, Info Security Analyst, Business Analyst, Technical Architect, QA Specialist, Product Manager. The producer can be other humans, Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, Visual Studio, Lovable, Gemini, Qwen, or any other AI. UX Designer = you / AI / humans; UX Researcher = you / humans.

## The Process for Agile Product Requirements

[video/file not saved: wk2-requirements-process.png]

**Agile teams DO NOT:** make a 30-page requirements doc for the whole product; gather all requirements first; wait for a contract before starting; build all specs before starting; work sequentially.
**Agile teams DO:** gather requirements all the time; gather the most important first; build the most important stuff (MVP!) all the time; work in parallel; release continuously.
**Process (repeat):** draft release vision → draft release scope → break into epics/features → get first dev/design tickets ready → start design & dev work in the backlog → keep adjusting after you deliver → repeat.

## Translating Release Scope into Epics, Features, and Tasks

[video/file not saved: wk2-epics-features-tasks.png]

Each outcome → **Epics** (huge). Under epics → **Features** (smaller). Under features → **Tasks** (tiny). Each epic has many features; each feature has many tasks. The backlog holds the "instructions to delivery": user stories, tasks, acceptance criteria, use cases, task flows, annotations.

## A Backlog Is Like a Kitchen

[video/file not saved: wk2-backlog-kitchen.png]

Your **kitchen** = your **backlog**. **Cabinets** = **Epics**. **Containers/boxes** = **Features**. **Items** (dishes, forks, knives) = **Tasks**. Many items go in containers; many containers go in cabinets. "How would you organize your backlog? Well, how would you organize your kitchen?"

## Non-Functional Requirements and Build Plan

[video/file not saved: wk2-nonfunctional-build-plan.png]

**Non-Functional** requirements come **before** **Functional** ones.

- **Non-Functional** = backend (servers, data, agents), dev ops (CI/CD, security, auto-testing), middle layer (APIs) — the foundation, built before features.
- **Functional** = the UI features. Don't start features until non-functional is done. *Can't build a house without the foundation.*
A **build plan** is the prioritized order of epics the developer builds: **Non-functional** (tool selection → database architecture → system architecture decisions → security protocol → dev operations → quality engineering) then **Functional** (prototypes → design system → task-level acceptance criteria → use case scenarios → flow charts → BDD scenarios). *Pro tip: "Deliverables" — ask Fleety.*

## Scoping Tasks — Task-Level Requirements

[video/file not saved: wk2-task-level-requirements.png]

Good tasks follow **INVEST**: **I**ndependent, **N**egotiable, **V**aluable, **E**stimable, **S**mall, **T**estable. A task's requirements include:

- **User Story Statements** — "As a \[user\], I want \[capability\], so that \[benefit\]."
- **Acceptance Criteria** — user story; background; problem statement; status/definition of the result; scope ("definition of done"); acceptance.
- **Use Case Scenarios** — narrative of how a user reaches a goal; Happy Path + Non-Happy Path.
- **Task Flows** — the steps and expected outcomes.
- **Behavior-Driven Development (BDD)** — Given/When/Then for Happy & Non-Happy Paths.
- **Technical Acceptance** — technical conditions that must be true.
- **Prototypes** — visual/interactive representations.

*Pro tip: "Deliverables" — ask Fleety.*

## Create Your Release Roadmap

[video/file not saved: wk2-release-roadmap.png]

The **Roadmap** is a deliverable you maintain over time (update it when plans change). On a Jan–Dec timeline: (1) add **epics** to the rows; (2) add **features** under the epics; (3) create **tasks** per feature and map when you'll do them.

## Populate Your Backlog & Maintain Requirements (in Notion) — 13 Steps

[video/file not saved: wk2-populate-backlog.png]

1. Start with your epics/features/tasks; open your project Notion space.
2. Go to Sprint Management → **Kanban Board and Backlog**.
3. Tasks are "Pages." Click "New Page" to create a task in the right status.
4. Give it a clear **title** summarizing scope.
5. Open the page; copy the URL to share the task.
6. Set the **team function**.
7. Set the **type** (usually a feature-related task; could be deliverable/meeting).
8. Set a **sprint forecast** (from your roadmap; leave blank if unsure, then discuss and update).
9. Create **epics** (type in the Epic field + Enter); select the right one.
10. Create **features** the same way; select the right one.
11. Move the task to **"Writing Requirements"** status when you start; keep statuses accurate.
12. Open the page and click **"Task-Level Acceptance Criteria Template."**
13. **Write the requirements**, following the task-level requirements guide.

## Agile Methods in Detail

[video/file not saved: wk2-agile-methods-detail.png]

- **Kanban** — continuous "To Do / Doing / Done" flow: refine → prioritize backlog → do the work → deliver. Add work statuses as needed. Strength: visualizes flow, limits WIP.
- **Scrum** — time-boxed sprints with ceremonies: Refinement, Estimation, Planning, Daily Standup, Demo/Review, Retrospective (repeating). Standup = what I did / doing / blockers.
- **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)** — coordinates many Agile teams at enterprise scale.
**Map a Process for Your Team:** Agile teams govern themselves — map your workflow as a flow chart; the labels become your **backlog statuses**, e.g., Intake → Requirements Gathering → Write Requirements → Refinement → Sprint Planning → Sprint In Progress → Done. *Pro tip: "Methods" — ask Fleety (*[*techfleet.network/tal-9000*](http://techfleet.network/tal-9000)*).*
