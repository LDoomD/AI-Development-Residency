### What the comparison shows

Our app is the only one of the four that grounds the decode in a standard role framework. The five framework-grounded features (expected duties, unwritten expectations, day-to-day expectations, job-title match rate, and the extra-responsibilities alert) are ours alone; none of the three direct competitors offer them. The competitors cluster on the adjacent resume, interview, salary, and flag features. So the differentiation is "what the role actually is, measured against a standard," not resume keywords or generic prep.

### What each feature means

- Expected duties for the job: the concrete duties stated in the posting, compared against a standard baseline for the title.
- Unwritten expectations for the job: duties not written in the posting but standard for the title (what experts and coaches usually charge to explain).
- Day-to-day expectations: the deliverables, tools, activities, and skills the job will expect, often unknown to less-experienced applicants.
- Job title match rate: how closely the posting matches the standard expectations for the title.
- Extra job responsibilities alert: duties in the posting that go beyond the traditional scope of the title ("two jobs in one").
- Strategy to prepare for the interview: how to position resume, cover letter, and interviewing based on the posting.
- Prioritize must-haves and nice-to-haves on resume: what matters most and least to prepare and showcase.
- Translating the language of the job description: plain-English meaning of corporate speak and jargon.
- Red flags and green flags: what to be wary of and what to be excited about as an employee.
- Resume keyword identifier: the keywords to put on a resume to stand out for this posting.
- Salary alignment with market: how the posting's salary compares to market expectations for the title.
- Questions to ask the employer: questions to ask in the interview based on the posting.
- Seniority level from the description: the seniority the posting implies.
