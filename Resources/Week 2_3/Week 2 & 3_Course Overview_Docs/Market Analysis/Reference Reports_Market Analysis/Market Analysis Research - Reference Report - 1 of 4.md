# Market Analysis Research: Reference Report (1 of 4 market analysis tables)

Written 2026-11-08 from `Market Analysis Research.csv` (same folder). The CSV was read in full: 16 rows, 13 columns, all rows the same length (script check). Every cell is in section 4. Tags: **Fact** = copied from the table. **Proposed** = my reading, not yet reviewed by the owner.

The other three tables in this deliverable are Possible Market Segments, Market Size and Market Opportunity. They are not covered here.

---

## 1. Purpose of this table

- **Plain words:** the table lists 16 kinds of business that touch the family caregiver problem. For each one it shows how big the market is, how fast it grows, who competes, how many people use their apps, and how much money they raised.
- **Why it exists:** market analysis turns scattered facts into a clear read on whether the opportunity is real. This table is the evidence base. The other three tables sort and score it (Fact: the owner's description of the deliverable, 2026-11-08).
- **What it can answer:** who is already serving caregivers, how big each market is, and where gaps are.
- **What it cannot answer:** what caregivers need, or who pays. That comes from the audience work and later research.

## 2. How it connects to the Discovery milestone

Discovery means learning the "current state" of the market, user needs, current behavior, and competitor opportunities before planning a solution. Market analysis feeds it, then segmentation, competitive analysis and feature analysis build on it (owner's description, 2026-11-08).

| What a finished market analysis should give you | What this table gives | Proposed read |
|---|---|---|
| A clear picture of the market landscape | 16 segments, each with size, growth, competitors | **Covered.** Sizes are mostly global and not caregiver-only |
| Validated customer needs | Nothing directly | **Not covered here.** Needs come from the audience work |
| Evidence the opportunity is real | Sizes and growth for the two Core rows | **Partly.** Big markets exist, but none is measured for caregivers |
| What is already solved and by whom | Competitors, app ratings, funding columns | **Covered** for most rows. Gaps listed in section 7 |
| Gaps worth pursuing | Respite has no consumer app. Mental health has no caregiver report | **Covered.** Matches the owner's approved opportunity (helping worn-out caregivers reach relief) |

## 3. The 13 columns: what each measures

| # | Column | What it measures | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market segment (plain name) | The kind of business, in everyday words | One row per segment. 16 rows |
| 2 | Official category (NAICS) | The US government's industry code that fits best | "None exact. Closest: ..." means no code fits. Used to look up Census revenue |
| 3 | Relationship to the problem | How close the segment is to the caregiver problem | Core (2 rows), Nearby (8), Indirect (5), Background (1). The text after the colon says why |
| 4 | Representative research market | The research firm's market that stands in for the segment | Often broader or different from the segment. Notes say when it is a stand-in (a "proxy") |
| 5 | Market size (base year) | How big the market was in the base year | Dollars. Mostly global. Base years vary: 2023, 2024 or 2025 |
| 6 | Forecast (year) | How big the firm expects it to be later | Dollars, with the end year. End years run from 2030 to 2036 |
| 7 | CAGR | The yearly growth rate (compound annual growth rate) | A percentage. Rows with several firms list several rates |
| 8 | Source | The research firm and web address | Each says "Checked on the firm's own page" |
| 9 | Competitors in this segment | The main companies or products | Includes the owner's named companies |
| 10 | Notes / alternate estimates | Where other firms disagree, and cautions | Where the uncertainty shows. Section 6 summarizes it |
| 11 | Census 2022 actual US revenue (NAICS code) | What US businesses in that industry code earned in 2022 | A reality check against research-firm guesses. "Too broad to use" in 7 rows |
| 12 | App store usage (ratings; Google Play install band) | How many people rated or installed the apps | A rough traction signal. Ratings count users who rated, not all users |
| 13 | Company funding / value, sourced (2026-11-02) | Money raised, sale prices, valuations, earnings | Looked up 2026-11-02. "Not found" means a search found nothing |
