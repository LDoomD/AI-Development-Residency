## 1. Purpose of this table

- **Plain words:** the table takes the segments and asks three questions of each: is the market big enough, are there few competitors, is there an unmet need. It then gives a verdict and shows what caregivers say, with counts.
- **Where it sits in the market analysis:** it is the fourth and last table. Table 1 scanned the market, table 2 mapped who is in it, table 3 sized it. This table judges it. It is where the earlier tables become an answer about which opportunity is open.
- **What it can answer:** which segments are open, which are crowded, and which have a real unmet need.
- **What it cannot answer:** who pays, or how many caregivers would pay. Rows 7 and 14 say the buyer is not the family.

## 2. How it connects to the Discovery milestone

Discovery means learning the "current state" of the market, user needs, current behavior, and competitor opportunities before planning a solution. The table below shows what this table gives, and what the four tables together achieved.

## 5. The most important things this table shows (all **Proposed**)

1. **Only one segment is rated both a real need and no consumer app: respite (row 11).** The market is large ($19.3B global, $7.6bn US), many local providers serve it, and "no consumer app category" exists. Caregivers say they want breaks far more than they get them.
2. **Burnout is the top caregiver problem in the evidence.** 210 of 412 Reddit posts (51.0%, recomputed) and 55 of 141 forum threads (39.0%). No other theme comes close on Reddit. The next is money, accounts and legal papers at 117 (28.4%).
3. **Caregivers get burnout support from people, not apps.** Row 12 says the big apps (Calm, Headspace) crowd the market but don't reach caregivers. Caregivers use therapists, support groups and other caregivers online.
4. **Most segments are closed or the wrong customer.** 6 are crowded or served (rows 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 13). 2 have a different buyer (rows 7 and 14). Only 1 is open but unproven (row 8), and only 1 has a real need with weak products (row 1).
5. **Two real needs are hard to enter:** choosing and paying for care (row 4, 90 of 412 posts) and getting into a parent's accounts (row 10, 117 of 412 posts, bank rules and security risk).
6. **For respite, the barriers are named:** cost, a parent who says no, paperwork, and family who won't help (row 11 verdict).

## 8. Where this table points (**Proposed**)

**The outcome.** The table points to **respite and adult day services (segment 11)** and **caregiver mental health (segment 12)** as the open opportunity. Burnout is the top theme (210 of 412 Reddit posts, 55 of 141 forum threads). Respite is a $19.3B global market with no consumer app, and many caregivers cannot get a break: of 341 posts about respite, 78 couldn't get one and 81 were still trying. Mental health apps are large but don't reach caregivers, who get support from people. Every other segment is closed (crowded, served, or hard to enter), has a different buyer, or is unproven.

**What was achieved.** Together the four tables give the market landscape, who is already serving caregivers, and the gaps. They only partly give validated needs and evidence that the opportunity is real (section 2).

**How it relates to the overall market analysis.** This is the opportunity the owner already approved: helping worn-out caregivers reach relief, segments 11 and 12 (`dogfood-2026-11-07-handoff-feature-comparison-analysis.md`, section 1). Table 4 is the evidence trail behind that choice. Rows 11 and 12 are the only ones that are Core in table 1, name burnout in table 2, and are rated Medium in table 3 (section 6).

**What it hands on.** The help caregivers name for these two segments (Medicaid and waiver programs, therapists, support groups, online caregiver groups) led to the 7 competitor rows added to the competitive analysis on 2026-11-07 (same handoff report, section 5).

**What it does not settle.** Who pays for relief, how many caregivers have the need, whether the answer differs for adult children (Audience 1) and spouses (Audience 2), and a caregiver-only market size.
