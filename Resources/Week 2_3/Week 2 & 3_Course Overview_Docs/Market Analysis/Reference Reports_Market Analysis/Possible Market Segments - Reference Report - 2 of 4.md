## 1. Purpose of this table

- **Plain words:** the table sorts the market into 15 groups of companies and programs that serve family caregivers. For each group it says what they do, which caregiver problem they solve, who the examples are, and whether they are for-profit or nonprofit.
- **How it differs from table 1:** table 1 looks at the market with numbers (size, growth, funding). This table looks at it with words: who is out there, what problem each group addresses, and what sector it is in (owner's description, 2026-11-08).
- **What it can answer:** where the market is crowded, where it is thin, and which caregiver problems still need better solutions.
- **What it cannot answer:** how big each group is, how many people use it, or who pays. Table 1 holds the numbers.

## 2. How it connects to the Discovery milestone

Discovery means learning the "current state" of the market, user needs, current behavior, and competitor opportunities before planning a solution. This table contributes the map of who already serves caregivers, and which problems they address (owner's description, 2026-11-08).

## 5. The most important things this table shows (all **Proposed**)

1. **Most segments solve information and logistics problems.** Ten of 15 rows address coordination, medications, records, logins, choosing care, insurance or getting to appointments (rows 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 13, 14, 15). The grouping is mine.
2. **Burnout is named in only 2 of 15 rows:** segment 11 ("Burnout, exhaustion") and segment 12 ("Burnout, guilt"). No other row names exhaustion, stress relief, or guilt. Row 7 mentions "time and stress."
3. **The two burnout rows are the only ones where what caregivers use is not a company.** Row 11 lists programs caregivers name most (Medicaid and its waiver programs, hospice, the VA, Area Agencies on Aging). Row 12 lists therapists, support groups and online caregiver groups.
4. **The market is mostly for-profit.** 11 rows are For-profit. 4 are Mixed (rows 1, 10, 11, 12). No row is nonprofit-only. The Mixed rows with the biggest nonprofit and government role are 11 and 12.
5. **In 3 rows the family is not the buyer.** Care providers pay in row 4, employers or plans in row 7, hospitals and health systems in row 9.

## 8. Where this table points (**Proposed**)

The table points to a crowded middle and a thin end:
- **Crowded:** information and logistics (coordination, medications, records, insurance). Ten rows, mostly for-profit, often sold to someone other than the family.
- **Thin:** burnout and exhaustion. Two rows (11 and 12), both Mixed, where caregivers rely on programs, therapists and support groups rather than a product.

This matches the opportunity the owner already approved: helping worn-out caregivers reach relief, segments 11 and 12 (`dogfood-2026-11-07-handoff-feature-comparison-analysis.md`, section 1). The table does not show how many caregivers have each problem, or who would pay. Those come from the audience work and tables 3 and 4.
