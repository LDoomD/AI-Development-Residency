## 1. Purpose of this report

- **Plain words:** one report that holds the result of the whole competitive analysis, so no one has to reopen the Figma section.
- **What a competitive analysis is in this course** (Fact, Week 2 lecture, 19:01 to 19:36): one of the early requirements, like personas and problem statements. It's written from what we believe now, and research then checks those beliefs.

- **What this report can answer:**

  - where every competitor falls short
- **What it cannot answer:**

  - 8 cells that still need data (section 9)

## 6. What was discovered (all **Proposed**, each based on the table named)

1. **No one fully helps with 3 of the 5 needs.** Not one of the 16 competitors gets a Yes on the parent's money, a parent who refuses help, or relatives sharing the work (4c).
2. **A parent who refuses help is the emptiest need.** 11 of 16 competitors get a No on it, and none gets a Yes (4c). It's a top problem for adult children (60 posts and threads, 2a).
3. **The help that works is locked behind money, a job or eligibility.** All 5 Yes cells come with a limit (4c, 4b):
   - Cariloop is through an employer.
   - Care managers cost $150 to $350 an hour.
   - Respite and the VA work only once a family gets in.
4. **Relief exists, but most can't reach it.** That's the same finding as the market analysis and the audience segmentation (gap 4). Only 128 of 341 respite posts describe actually using it.
5. **Most of the help caregivers actually use isn't a company.** 10 of 16 competitors are indirect: workarounds, government programs or a kind of person (table 3). Families often patch care together with group chats, aides and programs.
6. **Business models point away from families paying.** Most who pay are employers, places that pay referral fees, or the government (4b). Only 12% of caregivers would pay out of pocket (gap 2 evidence).
7. **Coordination apps exist but don't solve sharing.** Caring Village can't get relatives to join, and group chats get ignored (4a, 4c).
8. **Consent and health data are real risks.** Caring Village shows medical details to invited friends, and Everplans lets parents lock sections (gap 3).

## 7. What was learned (all **Proposed**)

**About the method**
- **Re-check the summary tables against the scores whenever scores change.** The 2026-11-09 re-check turned "No" into "Partly" in 2 problem rows (dementia behavior and refusing help), because the needs-met scores already showed partial help.
- **"Yes" needs a "who can get it."** The "Open to:" note shows that most help is narrow, even when it works.
- **Indirect competitors matter.** Programs, people and workarounds are what caregivers actually use for relief.
- **Some cells still need data.** 8 cells say Verify. A silent page is not a No (approved rule, 2026-11-06).
- **Numbers must be traced to the right deliverable.** The respite counts come from the market analysis, not the audience tables. They were fixed on 2026-11-09.

**About the deliverable**
- **The competitive analysis turns audience problems into scored needs, and scored needs into gaps.** Each gap is the opening for positioning.
- **Each gap carries a risk,** such as consent, or who pays.
