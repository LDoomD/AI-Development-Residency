# What is a usability test?

- In a usability test, you watch a real person try to do real tasks in your app.
- There are three parts: the facilitator (you), the tasks, and the participant (the person testing).
- You ask the person to think out loud while they work, so you can hear what they expect and where they get confused.
- You are looking for problems in the app. You are not testing the person.

## 5. Tasks

Write 3 to 5 tasks. Each task should match a feature in your MVP #1.

Rules for good tasks:

- Make it realistic. Ask people to do something they would really do.
- Ask them to do it, not to tell you how they would do it.
- Do not give clues. Do not use the words on your buttons or describe the steps.
- Give all the details they need, like an amount of money or a date.

## 7. Before the test

- [ ] Make a test account with fake data, so no one types real money information.
- [ ] Practice the test once with a friend. This is called a pilot test. Fix any task that was confusing.
- [ ] Ask a teammate or friend to take notes, if you can.
- [ ] Have the consent form ready.

# Consent Form

Read this to each person, or send it before the session. Get a yes before you start.

- **Why we are doing this:** We are testing a budgeting app to find out what is hard to use, so we can fix it.
- **What you will do:** You will try a few tasks in the app and think out loud. It takes about 30 minutes.
- **You are in control:** You can skip any task or stop at any time. You do not have to give a reason.
- **Your information:** We will not use your name in our report. We will call you P1 or P2. Please do not type any real money or bank information. Tell us if you want your notes deleted.
- **Recording:** Please choose:
  - [ ] Yes, you may record the screen.
  - [ ] Yes, you may record my voice.
  - [ ] No recording, notes only.

# Part 2: The Script

Read the words in quotes out loud.

## Welcome (2 minutes)

- "Thank you for helping today. I'm going to ask you to try a few things in an app."
- "We are testing the app, not you. You can't do anything wrong here. If something is hard, that helps us find what to fix."
- "Please think out loud as you go. Tell me what you are looking at, what you are trying to do, and what you expect to happen."
- "I may not answer your questions right away, because I want to see what you would do on your own. I will answer them at the end."
- "You can stop at any time. Do you have any questions before we start?"

## Warm-up questions (3 minutes)

1. "How do you keep track of your money today?"
2. "What is the hardest part of that for you?"
3. "What apps or tools have you tried for this?"

**What to say if they go quiet:**

- "What are you thinking right now?"
- "What are you looking for?"
- "What do you expect to happen if you click that?"

**What not to say:**

- Do not tell them where to click.
- Do not say "Good job" or "That's right." It changes how they act.
- Do not explain how the app works. Stay quiet and watch.

**After each task, ask:**

- "How easy or hard was that, from 1 (very hard) to 5 (very easy)?"
- "Was anything confusing?"

## Wrap-up (5 minutes)

1. "What was the easiest part of the app?"
2. "What was the most frustrating part?"
3. "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
4. "Would you use an app like this? Why or why not?"

- "Thank you so much. Now I can answer any questions you had."
