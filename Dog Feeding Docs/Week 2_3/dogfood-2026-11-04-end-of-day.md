## 3. What the research says now, across all deliverables

1. **Exhaustion is the #1 caregiver problem for both audiences:** 138 adult-child posts, 45 spouse posts; 51% of the Reddit random sample.
2. **The unmet part of exhaustion is access to relief, not a lack of relief.**
   - AARP/NAC 2025: 39% of caregivers say respite would help, but 13% use it.
   - Caregiver posts: of 341 respite posts, 128 used it, 78 couldn't get it and 81 were still looking.
   - 26% of caregivers writing about relief need a break and have no help (adult children 25%, spouses 30%).
3. **What blocks relief:**
   - **Adult children:** cost (16% of their posts), the parent refusing (15%), family not helping (12%).
   - **Spouses:** family not helping (12%), eligibility rules (10%), cost (8%). Small numbers.
   - Also: eligibility rules and applications for the VA and Medicaid, waitlists, rigid hours and minimum stays, quality worries, guilt.
4. **Burnout and meditation apps are almost never used by caregivers** (9 of 49,315 posts). They rely on therapy, support groups and online peer communities. Spouses lean on therapy and support groups more (26% and 22%, vs 17% and 12%).
5. **Who pays for relief today:** mostly government (the VA, Medicaid waivers, Medicare hospice respite, Area Agencies on Aging), plus families on sliding scales. Only 12% of caregivers would pay out of pocket, and willing payers cap near $70 a month.
6. **The 4 gaps (competitive analysis version 2):**
   1. No one handles a parent who refuses help or gets relatives to share the work. These are also top reasons caregivers can't take breaks.
   2. Personal, ongoing guidance is locked behind an employer (Cariloop) or a $150-an-hour bill (care managers). Free help stops at helplines, referrals and veterans' programs. *(Reworded today. Version 1 wrongly said all real guidance was locked.)*
   3. Help with money and accounts works only if the parent prepared ahead of time. This is the strongest unmet need among the software segments.
   4. **New:** relief exists, but caregivers can't reach it. The opening is helping caregivers find, qualify for, pay for and actually use relief, including getting the parent to agree and relatives to cover.
7. **How the gaps connect:** gaps 1 and 4 share blockers (the parent refusing, family not helping). Gap 4's eligibility paperwork links to gap 3 (papers and accounts) and to the who-pays question.
